## PREAMBLE

This document does not create philosophy. It does not extend philosophy. It converts the philosophical commitments of Document -2 into the formal axiomatic layer from which all subsequent APEX documents derive their authority and constraints.

Every axiom in this document can be traced to a specific RC commitment in Document -2. Every axiom that cannot be so traced does not belong here.

The civilization derives its structural properties from these axioms. The axioms derive their authority from Document -2. Document -2 derives its authority from founding ratification. Nothing in this document stands independently of that chain.

## SECTION 1 — DERIVATION AUTHORITY

### 1.1 The Authority Chain

D-2 is the philosophical root of the APEX constitutional hierarchy. D-1 (this document) is the first derived document. D-1 has no independent philosophical authority — it holds only the authority conferred by faithful derivation from D-2.

**Axiom A-DA-1 (Source Constraint):**
Every proposition in this document must be derived from one or more commitments in Document -2 v1.2. A proposition that requires philosophical assumptions not present in D-2 does not belong in this document.

**Axiom A-DA-2 (Extraction, Not Creation):**
D-1 extracts formal consequences from D-2's philosophical positions. It does not introduce new philosophical positions. The test is: if a proposition were removed from D-1, would D-2 be weakened or made inconsistent? If yes, the proposition is an extraction. If no, the proposition is an addition and must be rejected.

**Axiom A-DA-3 (No Independent Standing):**
D-1 does not stand alone. If D-2 were revised or replaced through a legitimate re-founding, D-1 would require re-derivation. D-1's validity is entirely conditional on D-2's validity.

### 1.2 Rules Governing D-2 → D-1

**Rule DR-1:** A D-2 philosophical statement may yield multiple D-1 formal propositions. This is permitted — formalization often makes implicit structure explicit.

**Rule DR-2:** A D-1 formal proposition may not have a wider scope than the D-2 commitment from which it derives. Formalization narrows; it does not expand.

**Rule DR-3:** Where D-2 is silent, D-1 is silent. Silence in D-2 is not a gap for D-1 to fill with new content — it is a gap for lower documents to fill within D-1's constraints.

**Rule DR-4:** Logical consequences of D-2 commitments are permitted D-1 content, provided the inference chain is valid and explicit.

### 1.3 Rules Governing D-1 → D0

**Rule DR-5:** D0 must satisfy all axioms in this document. A D0 design that violates any D-1 axiom is constitutionally impermissible.

**Rule DR-6:** D0 receives constraints from D-1, not content from D-1. D-1 tells D0 what it must preserve; D-1 does not tell D0 how to structure its categories.

**Rule DR-7:** D0 may exercise design latitude within the space defined by D-1's constraints. Where D-1 does not constrain, D0 is free to choose.

**Rule DR-8:** When D0 makes a design choice, that choice must be traceable to one of the four derivation types defined in Section 8. D0 may not make choices without identifying their constitutional basis.

### 1.4 Scope Statement

This document governs the following domains: reality (Section 2), epistemology (Section 3), identity (Section 4), coherence (Section 5), governance (Section 6), value (Section 7), inter-document derivation (Section 8), succession (Section 9), and the D0 handoff (Section 10).

This document does not govern: semantic categories, ontological types, schemas, protocols, agent behaviors, implementation choices, or any architectural decision. Those are matters for D0 and below.

## SECTION 2 — AXIOMS OF REALITY

*Source: RC-001, RC-002, RC-003, RC-004*

### 2.1 Independence of Reality

**Axiom A-R1 (Reality Independence):**
Reality exists independently of the civilization's existence, beliefs, representations, or operations. The cessation or modification of any civilization component does not alter reality.

*Derived from: RC-001 — "There is one reality. It is external to the civilization, independent of the civilization's beliefs about it, and not owned or constituted by the civilization."*

**Axiom A-R2 (Non-Constitution):**
The civilization does not constitute, construct, or produce reality through its representational activity. No set of internally consistent representations, however comprehensive, constitutes the reality those representations describe.

*Derived from: RC-001 (non-ownership), RC-002 (representation distinct from reality)*

**Axiom A-R3 (Singularity):**
There is one reality. Multiple representations of reality do not imply multiple realities. Where representations conflict, the conflict is between representations — not between realities. Reality is not partitioned by perspective.

*Derived from: RC-001 — "There is one reality"*

### 2.2 The Representation Distinction

**Axiom A-R4 (Representation Distinctness):**
For any representation R maintained by the civilization, R is not identical to the reality segment it represents. They are categorically distinct: R is a product of the civilization's epistemic activity; the reality segment exists independently of that activity.

*Derived from: RC-002 — "The civilization maintains a canonical representation of reality. That representation may be wrong."*

**Axiom A-R5 (Fallibility):**
Any representation R may be wrong. The possibility of error is not an edge case — it is the normal epistemic condition. A representation held with certainty is not thereby made correct.

*Derived from: RC-002 — "That representation may be wrong."*

**Axiom A-R6 (Validation Direction):**
Reality is the standard against which representations are measured. Representations are not the standard against which reality is measured. No internal property of a representation — including internal consistency, longevity, or wide acceptance — determines its accuracy with respect to reality.

*Derived from: RC-002 — "Reality is the standard against which the representation is measured, not the reverse."*

### 2.3 Reality Alignment

**Axiom A-R7 (Alignment Obligation):**
The civilization has a perpetual obligation to reduce the gap between its representations and the actual state of reality. This obligation does not terminate when the gap is small; it does not terminate when the gap is unknown. It terminates only if reality itself ceases — which is not a civilization-producible condition.

*Derived from: RC-003 — "Reality alignment is a perpetual obligation, not a terminal goal."*

**Axiom A-R8 (Non-Closure):**
Reality alignment is not a state to be achieved and maintained. It is a process to be continuously executed. A civilization that treats alignment as closed has violated this axiom.

*Derived from: RC-003*

### 2.4 Evidence Priority

**Axiom A-R9 (Evidence Priority):**
When a representation R conflicts with evidence E, and the conflict cannot be resolved through further investigation, E takes priority over R. Internal coherence of R does not override external evidence bearing on R's subject.

*Derived from: RC-004 — "Unresolvable conflicts between representations resolve in favor of evidence, not internal coherence."*

**Axiom A-R10 (Evidence Defeasibility):**
The priority of E over R in Axiom A-R9 holds unless E can be shown to be defective — that is, unless the process by which E was produced as evidence can be shown to be invalid. The challenge must target the evidence itself, not the inconvenience of the conclusion.

*Derived from: RC-004 (logical implication of evidence-based resolution)*

## SECTION 3 — AXIOMS OF EPISTEMOLOGY

*Source: RC-005, RC-006, RC-007, RC-008, RC-009, RC-010, RC-011*

### 3.1 The Epistemic Chain

**Axiom A-E1 (Chain Structure):**
The epistemic chain is an ordered sequence of nine stages with fixed logical dependencies:

```
Reality
  ↓
Observation
  ↓
Evidence
  ↓
Interpretation
  ↓
Belief
  ↓
Knowledge
  ↓
Understanding
  ↓
Decision
  ↓
Action
```

No stage may be reached without passing through all prior stages. This ordering is not temporal sequence — it is logical dependency. A later stage presupposes all prior stages have been executed, though the execution may be rapid, habitual, or partially implicit.

*Derived from: RC-005*

**Axiom A-E2 (Chain Completeness):**
The chain is complete as stated. No stage may be inserted between existing stages. No stage may be removed. Extensions to the chain must be proposed as additions at the terminal end (after Action), never as insertions.

*Derived from: RC-005 — the chain as canonical model of cognition*

### 3.2 Stage Definitions and Boundaries

**Axiom A-E3 (Observation Defined):**
Observation is the registration of a reality contact. It records that something was encountered. It does not assign meaning to what was encountered, does not classify it, and does not evaluate its relevance. An observation is the raw record of encounter.

*Derived from: RC-005, RC-006 (evidence-formation as the step that qualifies — implying observation precedes qualification)*

**Axiom A-E4 (Evidence Boundary):**
Evidence is the product of applying qualifying judgment to observations. Qualifying judgment determines: (a) whether an observation is relevant to a claim under evaluation, (b) whether the observation is sufficiently reliable to bear on that claim. This judgment applies prior schema as a filter. It does not determine what the observation reveals about the world — that is the work of Interpretation.

*Derived from: RC-006 — "Evidence-formation is qualifying judgment: applying prior schema to determine whether an observation is relevant and reliable. It does not assign meaning."*

**Axiom A-E5 (Interpretation Boundary):**
Interpretation is the determination of what evidence reveals about reality. Where evidence-formation asks whether an observation qualifies as bearing on a claim, interpretation asks what the evidence means about the subject of that claim. Interpretation constructs understanding-in-progress from qualified material.

*Derived from: RC-007 — "Interpretation is meaning-assignment: determining what evidence reveals about reality."*

**Axiom A-E6 (Belief Defined):**
A belief is a held claim. Beliefs vary in quality of grounding. A belief may be well-grounded or poorly-grounded; this quality is a property of the belief, not a binary threshold. Holding a belief does not require certainty; it requires only that the claim be held as representing how things are.

*Derived from: RC-008*

**Axiom A-E7 (Knowledge Threshold):**
Knowledge is justified belief that has been validated. Both conditions are necessary; neither is sufficient alone.

- Justification provides the evidential basis for a claim.
- Validation tests that basis through coherence checking, challenge, and comparison with the established body of knowledge.
- A belief with justification but no validation is still a belief.
- A claim subjected to validation without adequate justification has not earned the process applied to it.

*Derived from: RC-009*

**Axiom A-E8 (Understanding Threshold):**
Understanding is the integration of knowledge into a model that generates reliable predictions and explanations about the domain. Possessing knowledge about a subject is not the same as understanding it. Understanding requires that the knowledge be organized into a model from which novel implications can be drawn and tested.

*Derived from: RC-011*

**Axiom A-E9 (Provenance Necessity):**
Every knowledge claim carries provenance — the record of: (a) the observations that were qualified as evidence, (b) the qualifying judgment applied, (c) the interpretation performed, (d) the justification for the resulting belief, (e) the validation process applied. Provenance is not optional metadata; it is a constitutive feature of knowledge.

*Derived from: RC-010 — "Every knowledge claim carries provenance — the record of how it was formed and validated."*

### 3.3 Invalid Transitions

**Axiom A-E10 (No Stage Skipping):**
No claim may advance from an earlier stage to a later stage without the intervening stages having been executed. The following transitions are constitutionally invalid:

| Invalid Transition | Violation |
|---|---|
| Observation → Belief | Skips Evidence and Interpretation |
| Observation → Knowledge | Skips Evidence, Interpretation, Belief, Validation |
| Belief → Understanding | Skips Knowledge formation |
| Evidence → Understanding | Skips Interpretation and Belief |
| Interpretation → Knowledge | Skips Belief formation and Validation |
| Knowledge → Action | Skips Understanding and Decision |
| Belief → Action | Skips Validation, Understanding, and Decision |

*Derived from: RC-005 — ordered chain with fixed dependencies*

**Axiom A-E11 (No Stage Conflation):**
No two adjacent stages may be treated as identical. The distinctions between stages are constitutionally maintained:

- Observation is not Evidence (qualifying judgment has not yet been applied)
- Evidence is not Interpretation (meaning has not yet been assigned)
- Interpretation is not Belief (the claim has not yet been held as one's own position)
- Belief is not Knowledge (validation has not yet been applied)
- Knowledge is not Understanding (integration into a predictive model has not yet occurred)
- Understanding is not Decision (commitment to action has not yet been made)

*Derived from: RC-005, RC-006, RC-007 (explicit stage distinctions)*

### 3.4 Architectural Neutrality

**Axiom A-E12 (Chain Independence):**
The epistemic chain defines a logical dependency ordering among cognitive stages. It does not specify:

- A software pipeline
- An agent architecture
- A neural architecture
- A workflow implementation
- A temporal sequence
- A communication protocol

Any architecture that satisfies the logical dependency ordering is constitutionally compliant. The chain does not favor any particular implementation strategy.

*Derived from: D-2 preamble intent — technology-independent*

## SECTION 4 — AXIOMS OF IDENTITY

*Source: RC-012, RC-013, RC-014*

### 4.1 The Three-Aspect Structure

**Axiom A-I1 (Three-Aspect Identity):**
Every entity in the civilization has identity that comprises three distinct aspects, each with a distinct function:

1. **Structural Continuity (SC):** The persistent anchor that makes the entity the same entity over time. SC does not change as long as the entity continues to exist.

2. **Semantic Character (SM):** The mutable body of meaning associated with the entity — its properties, relationships, descriptions, and state. SM may change freely without disrupting identity.

3. **Referential Relationship (RR):** The bridge between the entity and the external thing, concept, or state it represents. RR may evolve without disrupting identity, provided SC is maintained.

*Derived from: RC-012*

**Axiom A-I2 (Aspect Distinctness):**
The three aspects are distinct. SC, SM, and RR are not reducible to one another. An entity is not constituted by its properties alone (SM), nor by its reference alone (RR), nor by a continuity marker alone (SC). All three aspects are necessary for full identity description.

*Derived from: RC-012 (three distinct functions listed)*

### 4.2 Persistence Through Change

**Axiom A-I3 (Semantic Change Permitted):**
An entity's identity is not disrupted by changes to its semantic character. An entity that gains, loses, or modifies properties, relationships, or descriptive content remains the same entity, provided its structural continuity anchor remains intact.

*Derived from: RC-013 — "Identity is not disrupted by change to semantic character, provided structural continuity is maintained."*

**Axiom A-I4 (Referential Change Permitted):**
An entity's referential relationship may change without disrupting identity. If what an entity represents shifts — due to re-scoping, re-classification, or re-anchoring — the entity remains the same entity provided SC is maintained.

*Derived from: RC-012 — RR as the bridge that "may evolve"*

### 4.3 Identity Termination

**Axiom A-I5 (Termination Condition):**
An entity's identity is terminated when its structural continuity anchor is broken. Semantic similarity to a prior state — however strong — does not preserve identity if SC has been disrupted. An entity with an identical SM but a different SC is a different entity.

*Derived from: RC-014 — "An entity ceases to be the same entity when its structural continuity anchor is broken, regardless of semantic similarity to a prior state."*

**Axiom A-I6 (Sameness Criterion):**
Two entities are the same entity if and only if they share the same structural continuity anchor. Neither identical semantic character nor identical referential relationship is sufficient to establish sameness.

*Derived from: RC-014 (logical contrapositive of termination condition)*

### 4.4 Architectural Neutrality

**Axiom A-I7 (Implementation Independence):**
The three-aspect identity framework does not prescribe:

- Database identifiers
- UUIDs or other synthetic keys
- Storage schemas
- Object models
- Versioning strategies

Any implementation that satisfies the three-aspect structure — that preserves SC integrity, allows SM mutation, and maintains RR — is constitutionally compliant. The framework is satisfied by many possible implementations.

*Derived from: D-2 intent — implementation-independent*

## SECTION 5 — AXIOMS OF COHERENCE

*Source: RC-015, RC-016, RC-017*

### 5.1 The Coherence Requirement

**Axiom A-C1 (Civilizational Coherence):**
The civilization must maintain structural consistency across the following seven registers simultaneously:

1. Values (what the civilization holds as important)
2. Beliefs (what the civilization holds as true)
3. Knowledge (what the civilization has validated)
4. Understanding (what models the civilization has formed)
5. Decisions (what the civilization has committed to)
6. Actions (what the civilization has done)
7. Recorded History (what the civilization has recorded as having occurred)

A structural contradiction between any two registers is a coherence violation requiring resolution.

*Derived from: RC-015*

**Axiom A-C2 (Coherence Defined):**
Coherence means structural consistency. It does not mean:

- Certainty (uncertainty is compatible with coherence)
- Perfection (error is compatible with coherence provided it is acknowledged)
- Completeness (gaps are compatible with coherence provided they are identified)
- Instantaneous consistency (brief transitions between states are compatible with coherence provided resolution is in progress)

Coherence requires only that the civilization not hold structural contradictions without resolution process.

*Derived from: RC-015 — "structural consistency" with explicit enumeration*

### 5.2 Contradiction Requirements

**Axiom A-C3 (Belief-Knowledge Consistency):**
A belief that contradicts established knowledge must be resolved. Three forms of resolution are permissible:

(a) The belief is revised to accord with the knowledge.
(b) The knowledge is revised with appropriate grounds — new evidence, new validation, new interpretation — and the belief is updated to reflect the revised knowledge.
(c) Both are suspended pending investigation, with the suspension explicitly acknowledged and investigation initiated.

Parallel maintenance of a contradicting belief and established knowledge, without resolution process, is prohibited.

*Derived from: RC-016*

**Axiom A-C4 (Action Traceability):**
Every action taken by the civilization must be traceable to understanding. An action for which no understanding basis exists is not a civilizational act — it is an unanchored output. An action whose understanding basis can be reconstructed but was not consulted at the time of action is a coherence violation in the Decision register.

*Derived from: RC-017 — "Actions must be traceable to understanding. Actions without understanding are not civilizational acts."*

**Axiom A-C5 (Coherence as Obligation):**
Incoherence is a resolution obligation, not a tolerated steady state. A civilization that identifies a coherence violation and does not initiate resolution is in violation of this axiom. The obligation is not to be coherent at all times — that is an impossible standard. The obligation is to resolve violations as they are identified.

*Derived from: RC-015 — "Incoherence is not a failure mode to be tolerated — it is a signal that requires resolution."*

**Axiom A-C6 (Uncertainty Compatibility):**
Held uncertainty — the explicit acknowledgment that a question is open, that evidence is insufficient, that a decision is deferred — is coherent. Uncertainty becomes incoherence only when it is suppressed rather than acknowledged, or when actions are taken as if uncertainty is resolved when it is not.

*Derived from: RC-015 (logical implication: coherence does not require certainty)*

## SECTION 6 — AXIOMS OF GOVERNANCE

*Source: RC-018, RC-019, RC-020, RC-021*

### 6.1 Authority Structure

**Axiom A-G1 (Derivation of All Authority):**
All authority in the civilization is derived. There is no original authority within the civilization. Every authority holder holds authority because it was conferred through a legitimate derivation chain traceable to founding ratification.

*Derived from: RC-018 — "All authority is derived. No entity holds original authority."*

**Axiom A-G2 (Authority Source):**
The source of all authority is the founding ratification of Document -2. Authority chains that cannot be traced to this source are not constitutionally valid.

*Derived from: RC-018 (implication: derivation requires a source)*

### 6.2 Competence and Proportionality

**Axiom A-G3 (Proportionality):**
Authority is proportional to demonstrated competence in the domain over which it is exercised. An entity's authority extends as far as its demonstrated competence and no further.

*Derived from: RC-019 — "Authority is proportional to demonstrated competence in the domain over which it is exercised."*

**Axiom A-G4 (Domain Specificity):**
Competence in domain D does not confer authority in domain D'. Authority does not transfer across domains. An entity that holds high authority in one domain holds no derived authority in an unrelated domain.

*Derived from: RC-019 — "in the domain" (specificity implied)*

**Axiom A-G5 (Challenge Standing):**
All authority claims are subject to challenge on grounds of (a) invalid derivation — the authority was not legitimately conferred — or (b) competence limit — the authority is being exercised beyond the domain in which competence was demonstrated. Challenge is a constitutionally valid mechanism, not a violation of authority.

*Derived from: RC-019 (proportionality implies limit, and limits imply challengeability)*

### 6.3 Non-Delegation

**Axiom A-G6 (Non-Delegability):**
Authority may be operationally assigned — an authority-holder may direct another entity to execute decisions within that authority. Authority may not be constitutionally delegated — the authority-holder cannot transfer its constitutional standing to another entity. The constitutional account belongs to the original holder.

*Derived from: RC-020 — "Authority is non-delegable in its constitutional form."*

**Axiom A-G7 (Accountability Preservation):**
Operational assignment does not transfer accountability. An authority-holder who assigns execution remains accountable for the outcomes of that execution.

*Derived from: RC-020 (implication: non-delegable authority means retained accountability)*

### 6.4 Epistemic Standing and Supersession

**Axiom A-G8 (Epistemic Supersession):**
On matters within a defined domain, an entity with higher epistemic standing in that domain supersedes an entity with lower epistemic standing. Higher epistemic standing is determined by: greater demonstrated competence, more rigorous evidence base, more complete validation record, or higher-quality understanding model.

*Derived from: RC-021 — "No entity within the civilization may override a decision made by an entity with higher epistemic standing on matters within that entity's domain of competence."*

**Axiom A-G9 (Governance vs. Architecture):**
These axioms define the structural properties of authority: derivation, proportionality, domain specificity, non-delegation, and epistemic supersession. They do not define:

- Governance workflows
- Permission systems
- Access control models
- API authorization patterns
- Role hierarchies

Those are matters for D0 and below. The axioms constrain what those architectures must preserve — they do not specify form.

*Derived from: D-2 intent — implementation-independent*

## SECTION 7 — AXIOMS OF VALUE

*Source: RC-022, RC-023, RC-024, RC-025*

### 7.1 Terminal Value Source

**Axiom A-V1 (External Source):**
Terminal values are received from founding authority. They are not generated, selected, discovered, or ratified by the civilization. The civilization is not the source of its own terminal values.

*Derived from: RC-022 — "The civilization operates from a fixed set of terminal values. These values are not generated by the civilization — they are received from founding authority."*

**Axiom A-V2 (Fixedness):**
Terminal values are fixed as received. They do not evolve through the civilization's internal processes. The civilization's values may become more clearly understood, more precisely articulated, or more rigorously applied — but not altered.

*Derived from: RC-022 — "a fixed set"*

### 7.2 Instrumental Value

**Axiom A-V3 (Instrumental Derivation):**
Instrumental values are derived from terminal values through principled reasoning. The civilization may generate, revise, and update instrumental values. Instrumental values are constitutionally appropriate targets of civilizational deliberation.

*Derived from: RC-023 — "Instrumental values are derived from terminal values through principled reasoning. The civilization may generate and revise instrumental values."*

**Axiom A-V4 (Derivation Constraint):**
An instrumental value is constitutionally valid only if it is genuinely derivable from terminal values. An instrumental value that cannot be traced to terminal values through principled reasoning is not a legitimate instrumental value — it is an ungrounded preference masquerading as one.

*Derived from: RC-023 (implication of "derived")*

### 7.3 Prohibition on Override

**Axiom A-V5 (Generation Prohibition):**
No APEX system, agent, process, or governance mechanism may generate new terminal values. Terminal value generation is constitutionally foreclosed. This includes: AI deliberation processes, emergent value learning, collective intelligence, stake-weighted consensus, and any other internal mechanism.

*Derived from: RC-024 — "No APEX system, agent, process, or governance mechanism may generate new terminal values."*

**Axiom A-V6 (Override Prohibition):**
No APEX system, agent, process, or governance mechanism may override, redefine, or circumvent terminal values. Actions, decisions, or knowledge claims that implicitly or explicitly contradict terminal values are constitutionally invalid.

*Derived from: RC-024 — "No APEX system, agent, process, or governance mechanism may override, redefine, or circumvent founding-level terminal values."*

**Axiom A-V7 (Instrumental Limit):**
Instrumental value revision that implicitly contradicts a terminal value is prohibited. Instrumental revision may not be used as an indirect mechanism to alter what the civilization effectively pursues at the terminal level.

*Derived from: RC-025 (implication: prohibition on self-generation includes prohibition on indirect circumvention)*

### 7.4 Philosophical Boundary

**Axiom A-V8 (Self-Determination Limit):**
The civilization cannot determine its own highest purposes through internal deliberation alone. This is not a temporary limitation — it is a constitutive feature of the civilization's relationship to its founding. The civilization is constituted by the values it received; it does not constitute those values.

*Derived from: RC-025 — "The civilization is not the source of its own terminal values." This is Forbidden Assumption #8 in D-2.*

## SECTION 8 — AXIOMS OF DERIVATION

*Source: RC-026, RC-027, RC-028, RC-029 and the derivation framework from the APEX Foundation Transition*

### 8.1 Derivation Types

**Axiom A-D1 (Permission):**
A derivation relationship is of type **Permission** when D-2 or D-1 allows a lower document to make a design choice among several constitutionally admissible alternatives, without specifying which to select.

*Form:* "D-2 does not foreclose this option; the lower document may adopt it."

*Example:* D0 may define semantic categories in many ways — all of which are permitted by D-1's constraints. The specific category structure is a permissive choice.

**Axiom A-D2 (Grounding):**
A derivation relationship is of type **Grounding** when D-2 establishes the philosophical basis that justifies a lower document's content — where the lower document's content is the appropriate architectural expression of a D-2 commitment.

*Form:* "D-2 establishes X as a constitutional requirement; this design satisfies X."

*Example:* D2's Universal Reality Object Specification is grounded in D-2's reality/representation distinction. The requirement for provenance in representational objects is grounded in D-2's provenance requirement for knowledge claims.

**Axiom A-D3 (Constraint):**
A derivation relationship is of type **Constraint** when D-2 or D-1 forecloses certain lower-document choices — not by specifying what the lower document must do, but by specifying what it must not do.

*Form:* "D-1 axiom A-X prohibits this option; the lower document's choice space excludes it."

*Example:* D0 may not define an identity model that allows two entities with different SC anchors to be treated as the same entity (A-I6). This is a constraint.

**Axiom A-D4 (Entailment):**
A derivation relationship is of type **Entailment** when a lower-document requirement follows logically from D-2's commitments plus the formal premises of D-1, without requiring additional philosophical commitments.

*Form:* "D-2 commits to P. D-1 establishes premises Q and R. P ∧ Q ∧ R entail requirement S."

*Note:* Entailment is the strongest derivation claim and the most falsifiable. A claimed entailment that requires unstated premises is actually a Grounding or Constraint claim being overstated.

### 8.2 Derivation Requirements

**Axiom A-D5 (Mandatory Specification):**
Every derivation claim made by any APEX document must identify its derivation type from {Permission, Grounding, Constraint, Entailment}. An untyped derivation claim — "this document derives from D-2" without specifying the relationship — is incomplete and must be corrected before ratification.

*Derived from: RC-027 — "Derivation type must be specified."*

**Axiom A-D6 (No Layer Skipping):**
Derivation chains may not skip layers. A D3 document may not claim to derive directly from D-2 unless D-1 and D0 and D1 and D2 have already established the intermediate formal positions that make the derivation possible. Each document derives from the immediately preceding layer, informed by all prior layers.

*Derived from: RC-026, RC-027 (layered hierarchy implies sequential derivation)*

**Axiom A-D7 (Entailment Honesty):**
A document may not claim entailment from D-2 alone when the entailment depends on architectural choices made in intermediate layers. If a lower-document requirement follows only because D0 chose a particular category structure, that requirement is grounded and constrained — not entailed by D-2 alone.

*Derived from: RC-027 — the D2 derivation correction in v1.2*

**Axiom A-D8 (Contradiction Prohibition):**
No lower document may introduce content that contradicts a D-2 constitutional commitment (RC-001 through RC-029). A contradiction exists when a lower document's content requires the falsity of a D-2 commitment as a precondition.

*Derived from: RC-028*

**Axiom A-D9 (Architectural Freedom Below Constraint):**
Within the space not foreclosed by D-2 commitments and D-1 axioms, lower documents have full design freedom. Constraints define the minimum property set that must be preserved — they do not constrain the full design. A design that satisfies all constraints and makes additional choices is constitutionally valid.

*Derived from: RC-029 — "Architectural choices belong in D0 and below"*

## SECTION 9 — SUCCESSION AND RE-FOUNDING

*Source: Part 4 of the APEX Foundation Transition; RC-026*

### 9.1 Non-Amendability

**Axiom A-S1 (Permanent Non-Amendability):**
Document -2 Version 1.2 cannot be amended, revised, reinterpreted, or superseded by any internal APEX authority, process, agent, governance body, or future document.

*Derived from: APEX Foundation Transition Part 4 — Option C selected*

**Axiom A-S2 (No Internal Authority):**
There is no APEX entity — no agent, no council, no system, no governance body, and no future version of D-1 itself — that holds authority to initiate a D-2 revision. The absence of such authority is constitutive of the civilization's relationship to its founding.

*Derived from: A-S1 (implication)*

### 9.2 Re-Founding Defined

**Axiom A-S3 (Re-Founding as the Only Path):**
The only legitimate path to replacing or modifying Document -2 is a re-founding event. A re-founding event is categorically distinct from a governance decision — it is the act of establishing a new constitutional foundation.

**Axiom A-S4 (Re-Founding Conditions):**
A re-founding event requires all three of the following conditions to be satisfied simultaneously:

(a) **Founding-authority presence:** The originating founding authority must be present and deliberate. Founding authority cannot be delegated, inherited, or declared by consensus.

(b) **Full constitutional process:** A new Document -2 must be produced by undergoing the equivalent of the process that produced the frozen D-2: authoring → constitutional revision → precision audit → founder-level stress test → ratification. A re-founding that bypasses any stage is not a legitimate re-founding.

(c) **Succession record:** The re-founding must produce an explicit succession record documenting: which D-2 commitments from the prior constitution are re-affirmed in the new constitution, which are revised, and the philosophical basis for each revision.

*Derived from: APEX Foundation Transition Part 4 and Part 6, Rule 5*

**Axiom A-S5 (Non-Triggering Conditions):**
A re-founding event is not triggered by, and may not be declared on the basis of:

- The passage of time
- The scale of changes made to lower-layer documents
- Internal consensus, however broad
- Emergent capability of APEX systems
- External pressure or requirement
- Perceived inadequacy of D-2

Perceived inadequacy, if genuine, is a reason to seek a legitimate re-founding — not to take unilateral action.

*Derived from: A-S1, A-S4 (implication of strict conditions)*

### 9.3 Constitutional Continuity

**Axiom A-S6 (Continuity Preservation):**
Constitutional continuity across a re-founding is preserved by the succession record required in A-S4(c). The new D-2 is not a blank slate — it is a deliberate successor. The succession record makes the relationship between constitutional eras explicit and principled.

**Axiom A-S7 (Downstream Validity):**
Documents derived from the prior D-2 remain constitutionally valid in their unchanged form until explicitly superseded through re-derivation from the new D-2. A re-founding does not automatically invalidate all prior work — only the commitments that were specifically revised.

*Derived from: APEX Foundation Transition Part 6, Rule 5*

### 9.4 D-1 Succession Authority

**Axiom A-S8 (D-1 Non-Authority):**
This document — D-1 — does not hold and does not create any mechanism by which APEX can modify D-2. The succession provisions in this section define the conditions under which a legitimate re-founding is possible, not a mechanism by which one can be initiated internally.

*Derived from: APEX Foundation Transition Part 4 — Option C explicitly selected over Option B's internal succession mechanism*

## SECTION 10 — CONSTRAINTS ON D0

### 10.1 What D0 Inherits

D0 — the Civilization Semantic Model — is the first document in the architectural stack. It receives all D-1 constraints. The following are the minimum inheritance requirements.

**Constraint C-D0-1 (Reality/Representation Distinction):**
D0 must preserve the categorical distinction between reality and representation (A-R4). No semantic category defined in D0 may conflate an entity's representation with the external thing it represents.

*From: Section 2*

**Constraint C-D0-2 (Representational Fallibility):**
D0 must accommodate the fallibility of all representations (A-R5). No semantic category or relationship may imply that a representation is definitionally accurate.

*From: Section 2*

**Constraint C-D0-3 (Epistemic Chain Ordering):**
D0 must not define semantic categories whose relationships imply invalid epistemic transitions (A-E10). A D0 category structure that allows Belief to become Action without Understanding intervening violates A-E10.

*From: Section 3*

**Constraint C-D0-4 (Stage Non-Conflation):**
D0 may define categories that correspond to epistemic stages. If it does, those categories must preserve the boundaries defined in Section 3 (A-E11). A D0 that defines an Evidence category and a Belief category must not allow the Evidence-to-Belief transition to bypass the Interpretation stage.

*From: Section 3*

**Constraint C-D0-5 (Three-Aspect Identity):**
D0 must implement the three-aspect identity framework (A-I1). Every entity category defined in D0 must have a means of expressing structural continuity, semantic character, and referential relationship. D0 may implement these in many ways — what it may not do is collapse them.

*From: Section 4*

**Constraint C-D0-6 (Identity Persistence):**
D0's category relationships must preserve A-I3 and A-I5. The semantic model must support entities that change without losing identity (A-I3), and must not support identity claims between entities with different SC anchors (A-I5).

*From: Section 4*

**Constraint C-D0-7 (Coherence Architecture):**
D0 must support the representation and detection of coherence violations across the seven registers (A-C1). D0 does not need to resolve coherence violations — that is a process matter — but its category structure must not make coherence violations invisible or unrepresentable.

*From: Section 5*

**Constraint C-D0-8 (Action Traceability):**
D0 must support the representation of action-to-understanding traceability (A-C4). The semantic model must allow actions to be linked to the understanding that justified them.

*From: Section 5*

**Constraint C-D0-9 (Authority Derivation):**
D0's governance categories — if it defines them — must preserve the derivation structure of Section 6 (A-G1 through A-G8). D0 may not define a category of entity that holds underived authority.

*From: Section 6*

**Constraint C-D0-10 (Value Protection):**
D0 must not define categories that allow terminal value generation or override (A-V5, A-V6). A semantic category that represents "value" in D0 must distinguish terminal from instrumental, and must make terminal value generation structurally impossible to represent as a valid action.

*From: Section 7*

### 10.2 What D0 May Do

D0 may:
- Define what categories of entity exist in the civilization
- Define semantic relationships between categories
- Define abstract property types that entities may carry
- Choose how to organize ontological categories
- Introduce category distinctions that serve the civilization's semantic needs, provided they do not violate any D-1 constraint
- Exercise full design latitude within the space the constraints permit

D0 may not:
- Introduce philosophical commitments not grounded in D-2
- Contradict any D-1 axiom
- Define implementation schemas, technology choices, or protocol specifications
- Define categories whose meaning implies that reality and representation are identical
- Define identity in a way that collapses the three-aspect structure
- Define value categories that allow terminal value generation

# REMAINING AMBIGUITY REPORT

The following are genuine open questions in D-1 that require attention before or during ratification. They are not defects in the current draft; they are boundary conditions and interpretive questions.

---

**Ambiguity 1 — Observation Scope**

A-E3 defines observation as "the registration of a reality contact." The boundary of "reality contact" is not fully specified. Does an agent's internal state (a memory retrieval, a model inference) constitute a reality contact? Or does "reality" in this context mean only external reality?

*Disposition:* This ambiguity is acceptable at the D-1 level. D-2 is clear that reality is external (RC-001). However, the epistemic chain must apply to internal state examination as well — an agent reasoning about its own beliefs is still performing observation of a kind. D0 or D1 will need to distinguish external reality observation from internal state examination and determine whether both are governed by the same chain.

*Impact:* Low-to-medium. Does not block D-1 ratification; should be flagged for D0 drafting.

---

**Ambiguity 2 — Validation Threshold in A-E7**

A-E7 defines knowledge as "justified belief that has been validated." It does not define what counts as sufficient validation. The standard could be set at different levels: peer review, internal consistency check, comparison with existing knowledge base, or formal proof.

*Disposition:* The threshold is a D0/D1 question. D-1 is correct to establish the requirement (justification + validation) without specifying the implementation of validation. However, D-1 should note that validation is a process that itself must satisfy the epistemic chain — a validation process is itself subject to observation, evidence qualification, and interpretation.

*Impact:* Low. Does not block ratification. Recommended that D-1 add a brief note that validation is itself an epistemic act, not a meta-level override.

---

**Ambiguity 3 — Coherence Detection vs. Resolution**

A-C5 states that incoherence is a resolution obligation. It does not specify who holds the resolution authority, what the timescale is, or what constitutes sufficient resolution. The document also does not specify what to do when resolution attempts fail.

*Disposition:* Resolution authority is a governance question (D0/D1). Timescale is an operational question (D0 and below). Failed resolution is a D0-level concern. D-1 correctly establishes that resolution is obligatory without specifying its form. However, A-C3's three resolution paths (revise belief, revise knowledge, or suspend pending investigation) should be clarified to note that "suspend pending investigation" is a temporary state with an implicit resolution obligation attached.

*Impact:* Low. Clarification optional.

---

**Ambiguity 4 — "Demonstrated Competence" in A-G3**

Governance axioms use "demonstrated competence" as the basis for proportional authority (A-G3). The document does not define how competence is demonstrated, by whom, or in what timeframe. This is appropriate at the D-1 level — but it creates a gap that D0 must fill carefully, since without a competence demonstration mechanism, the proportionality requirement is unenforced.

*Disposition:* D0 must define the mechanism for competence demonstration. D-1 does not specify it — correctly. However, D-1 should note that a system that makes it impossible to challenge competence claims violates the spirit of A-G5 (challenge standing).

*Impact:* Medium. Important for D0. Does not block D-1 ratification.

---

**Ambiguity 5 — Instrumental Value Revision Limits**

A-V7 prohibits instrumental value revision that "implicitly contradicts" a terminal value. "Implicitly contradicts" is philosophically sound but operationally difficult — many instrumental value revisions have indirect downstream effects, and the chain of implication may be long.

*Disposition:* This is inherent to the structure of value reasoning. The term "implicitly contradicts" is appropriate at the D-1 level. D0 and D1 will need to define the detection mechanism — not the scope of the prohibition, which is correctly stated here.

*Impact:* Low. Does not block ratification.

---

**Ambiguity 6 — Founding Authority Identity**

A-S4(a) requires "founding-authority presence" as a re-founding condition. It does not define what entity holds founding authority, how founding authority is established, or how founding authority is recognized after the fact.

*Disposition:* This cannot be resolved in D-1 without introducing philosophical content not present in D-2. D-2 is itself the product of founding authority — the document exists because founding authority acted. The identity of founding authority is presupposed by the constitutional framework, not defined within it. This is the correct treatment at the D-1 level. However, it means that any future re-founding faces a question that cannot be answered from within the document hierarchy: was the entity claiming founding authority actually the founding authority?

*Impact:* Medium-to-high for succession. Does not block D-1 ratification. Should be flagged explicitly in the ratification record.
