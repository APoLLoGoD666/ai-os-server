## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

D2 defined what a valid APEX object is. D3 defines the world those objects inhabit.

The Reality Fabric is the constitutional environment in which Universal Reality Objects (UROs) exist, relate, evolve, and collectively constitute APEX's representation of reality. It is not a storage layer. It is not a query system. It is not a graph database. It is the set of constitutional rules, structural invariants, topological constraints, and evaluation protocols that must hold for any collection of URO objects to qualify as a coherent reality-tracking system.

Without D3, APEX has valid objects but no world for them to inhabit. With D3, every URO object exists within a defined constitutional environment that enforces identity continuity, epistemic integrity, authority traceability, temporal preservation, and coherence evaluation — continuously, across the full lifecycle of the civilization.

D3 also performs a critical function that no prior document has performed: it maps the existing APEX architecture — its kernel, registry, memory system, governance system, intelligence engines, agents, and telemetry infrastructure — into the URO constitutional model. This mapping is the foundation of constitutional convergence. It makes visible what the existing systems already embody constitutionally, and where the gaps are.

## DEFINITIONS

**Reality Fabric (RF):** The totality of all constitutionally valid URO objects and all constitutionally valid URO-Relationships, evaluated continuously against the seven CoherenceRegisters, across the full temporal extent of the civilization's existence.

**Fabric Participation:** An object participates in the Reality Fabric when it satisfies all six URO Layers, is related to at least one other fabric object, and has at least one non-violated coherence register membership.

**Fabric Segment:** A topologically connected sub-graph of the Reality Fabric — a set of objects where every object is reachable from every other object through a finite chain of URO-Relationships.

**Observation Boundary:** The constitutional interface between external reality (RealitySegments, ExternalStates, Occurrences) and APEX representation. Observations are the only URO objects that may directly reference external reality.

**Epistemic Chain:** The ordered sequence of epistemic stages through which information must pass from Observation to ActionTrace. No stage may be skipped.

**Authority Chain:** The ordered sequence of AuthorityClaims tracing from a specific authority exercise back to the civilization's FoundingRatification.

**Fabric Coherence:** The property of the Reality Fabric that holds when all seven CoherenceRegisters evaluate to {Consistent} across all active objects and all active relationships.

**Temporal Fabric:** The constitutional structure governing how objects and relationships exist, change, and are preserved across time within the Reality Fabric.

**Coherence Register:** One of seven constitutional evaluation dimensions against which the coherence of every URO object and relationship is continuously assessed.

**Fabric Checkpoint:** A constitutionally preserved snapshot of the complete fabric state at a specific TemporalPosition, from which the fabric state can be reconstructed.

## PART 1: REALITY FABRIC — DEFINITION

### 1.1 What the Reality Fabric Is

The Reality Fabric is the constitutional environment in which all APEX representational objects participate. It has five defining properties:

**Property 1 — Totality:** The Reality Fabric contains every URO object that has ever existed, including objects in terminal states (superseded, historical, archived). Nothing is outside the fabric once it has entered. Entry is permanent.

**Property 2 — Relationality:** The Reality Fabric is constituted by relationships, not by objects alone. Objects without relationships are constitutional orphans — present but non-participatory. The fabric's meaning emerges from the pattern of connections between objects, not from the objects in isolation.

**Property 3 — Temporal Extent:** The Reality Fabric is not a snapshot. It is a temporally extended structure that includes all past states, all present states, and the constraints on future states. The fabric at any moment T is the full history of the civilization from founding to T, plus all provenance chains, plus all coherence evaluations, plus all historical records.

**Property 4 — Continuous Coherence:** The Reality Fabric is subject to continuous coherence evaluation. It is not merely stored — it is continuously assessed. The seven CoherenceRegisters operate as persistent evaluators that maintain a running assessment of whether the fabric is internally consistent.

**Property 5 — Reality Orientation:** The Reality Fabric is not self-contained. It is oriented toward external reality. Its purpose is to represent, track, and reason about external states, not to replace them. The fabric is constitutionally obligated to remain tethered to external reality through the Observation Boundary.

### 1.2 What the Reality Fabric Is Not

**Not a database:** A database stores records and retrieves them. The Reality Fabric defines constitutional rules for what may exist and how it may relate. A database is a possible implementation substrate for part of the fabric — but the fabric exists prior to and independently of any storage mechanism.

**Not a knowledge graph:** A knowledge graph is a semantic network of typed nodes and edges, typically designed for query and inference. The Reality Fabric includes semantic relationships but goes further — it enforces provenance chains, authority chains, epistemic constraints, temporal ordering, and coherence evaluation. Knowledge graphs are typically designed without constitutional constraints on what may be asserted or by what authority. The Reality Fabric is constitutionally constrained at every level.

**Not a memory system:** APEX has eight memory types (working, episodic, semantic, procedural, strategic, skill, decision, founder). These are operational subsystems that implement specific memory functions. The Reality Fabric is the constitutional environment in which the contents of all memory systems participate — not the memory systems themselves. Memory systems are implementations; the Reality Fabric is the constitution those implementations must satisfy.

**Not an ontology:** An ontology defines categories and relationships between categories. D0 and D1 are the APEX ontology. The Reality Fabric is the active, temporally extended instance of that ontology — populated with specific objects, specific relationships, and specific histories. The ontology is the schema; the Reality Fabric is the living instance.

**Not an object store:** An object store persists objects for retrieval. It does not evaluate coherence, enforce epistemic chains, trace authority, or preserve historical states constitutionally. The Reality Fabric is the constitutional obligation that any object store serving APEX must satisfy.

**Not a document repository:** Documents are APEX objects that may participate in the Reality Fabric. They are not the fabric itself.

### 1.3 The Constitutional Environment Concept

The Reality Fabric is a constitutional environment in the same sense that a legal system constitutes an environment for contracts. A contract exists within a legal system: the legal system defines what counts as a valid contract, what happens when contracts conflict, how they are enforced, what makes them invalid, and what their history means. Without the legal system, paper with words is just paper.

Similarly, a URO object exists within the Reality Fabric: the fabric defines what counts as a valid object, what happens when objects conflict, how authority chains are enforced, what makes an object constitutionally suspended, and what the historical record means.

This makes D3 not descriptive but prescriptive. D3 does not describe what APEX currently does. It prescribes what any valid APEX reality-tracking system must do — and thereby defines the standard against which the existing implementation will be audited and against which D4 will define its enforcement kernel.

### 1.4 The Existing Reality Fabric

The existing `lib/reality/fabric.js` implements a 13-stage claim lifecycle (potential → evolved) with 9-dimensional health scoring. This is the operational implementation of what will become the constitutionally governed Reality Fabric after D3 ratification. The existing implementation is constitutionally proto-compliant: it captures the epistemic progression concept (stages), the health evaluation concept (9 dimensions), and the temporal evolution concept (lifecycle). D3 does not invalidate this implementation — it provides the constitutional architecture that D4 and the implementation layer will use to make it fully compliant.

## PART 2: UNIVERSAL OBJECT GRAPH MODEL

### 2.1 The Graph

The Reality Fabric is constitutionally a directed, typed, temporally-versioned, coherence-evaluated graph. Its elements:

**Nodes:** Every URO object is a node. Nodes are typed by their D0/D1 category. Nodes have constitutional state: {pre-constitutional, active, suspended, superseded, historical, archived}. Pre-constitutional nodes are not in the graph — they are pending entry.

**Edges:** Every URO-Relationship is an edge. Edges are themselves URO objects (first-class nodes that also function as edges). An edge has a SourceObject, a TargetObject, and a RelationshipType from the D2 type hierarchy. An edge is directional: from Source to Target.

**Meta-edges:** Because edges are URO objects, they may themselves be related to other objects through further edges. This creates a constitutionally valid meta-structure. An EvidenceRecord (edge: grounds a Belief) may itself be grounded by (another edge pointing from another EvidenceRecord to this EvidenceRecord). Meta-edges are not recursive without limit — every edge must eventually trace to an Observation at the Observation Boundary.

**Hyperedges:** Some constitutional events involve more than two objects simultaneously (e.g., a ResolutionRecord resolves a CoherenceViolation by modifying the relationship between two objects). These are represented as hyperedges — URO objects with more than two related objects. Hyperedges are constitutionally valid but must themselves satisfy all six URO Layers.

### 2.2 Graph Invariants

The following invariants must hold for the Universal Object Graph at all times:

**GI-1 — No Isolated Active Nodes:** Every active URO object must have at least one outgoing and one incoming URO-Relationship. An object with zero relationships is constitutionally orphaned and must enter the {suspended} state until its relationships are established.

**GI-2 — No Undefined Edge Endpoints:** Every URO-Relationship must have both SourceObject and TargetObject pointing to existing URO objects (including objects in terminal states). An edge pointing to a non-existent object is constitutionally invalid.

**GI-3 — Acyclic Epistemic Chains:** The sub-graph formed by EpistemicRelationships (grounds, derives-from, validates, interprets) must be acyclic. An Observation may not be grounded by a Belief. A KnowledgeClaim may not validate its own evidence. Epistemic cycles are unconditional coherence violations in the Epistemic Register.

**GI-4 — Acyclic Provenance Chains:** The sub-graph formed by ProvenanceRelationships must be acyclic. An object cannot be derived from its own product. Provenance cycles are unconditional coherence violations in the Provenance Register.

**GI-5 — Single Authority Root:** The sub-graph formed by AuthorityRelationships must have a single root per CivilizationalEra: the FoundingRatification of that era. All authority chains in a given era must terminate at that era's FoundingRatification and nowhere else.

**GI-6 — Temporal Ordering Consistency:** For any two objects A and B where A.OriginatingOccasion precedes B.OriginatingOccasion in real time, no edge may assert that A depends on B (where dependency implies prior existence). The temporal ordering of the real world must be preserved in the graph's causal structure.

**GI-7 — Historical Completeness:** When an object enters a terminal state, a complete traversal of that object's sub-graph (all objects reachable from it through URO-Relationships) must remain reachable through the HistoricalAnchor. No terminal transition may orphan dependent historical records.

**GI-8 — Category Membership Stability:** The D0 CategoryMembership declared in an object's SC may not change after origination. An object's node type is permanently fixed at origination.

**GI-9 — Relationship Type Immutability:** The RelationshipType of a URO-Relationship may not change after origination. If the relationship between two objects changes in character, the old relationship is superseded and a new relationship with the correct type is created.

### 2.3 Graph Coherence Rules

Beyond the invariants (which must always hold), the following coherence rules govern the graph's semantic integrity:

**GCR-1 — Epistemic Chain Completeness:** Every active KnowledgeClaim must be reachable from at least one Observation through a complete, unbroken epistemic chain (EpistemicRelationships only, no skips). This rule is evaluated by the Epistemic Register.

**GCR-2 — Authority Chain Completeness:** Every active URO object's authority chain must be traceable to the current era's FoundingRatification through an unbroken sequence of AuthorityRelationships. This rule is evaluated by the Authority Register.

**GCR-3 — Provenance Chain Completeness:** Every active KnowledgeClaim and UnderstandingModel must have IsComplete = true in its ProvenanceChain. This rule is evaluated by the Provenance Register.

**GCR-4 — Temporal Causality:** For any edge of type {derived-from, generated-by, grounded-by, validated-by}, the TargetObject must have originated before the SourceObject. This rule is evaluated by the Temporal Register.

**GCR-5 — Identity Consistency:** For any two objects sharing a StructuralAnchor reference (specifically, ActorProfile sharing its SC with an Entity), no property of the shared SC may differ between the two objects. This rule is evaluated by the Identity Register.

**GCR-6 — Value Alignment:** No active CivilizationalDecision or CivilizationalAction may have a URO-Relationship of type {conflicts-with-value} to any TerminalValue without a corresponding ResolutionRecord. Unresolved value conflicts are CoherenceViolations in the Value Register.

**GCR-7 — Ontological Soundness:** No active object may be related through an EpistemicRelationship to an object of an incompatible category (e.g., an Interpretation may not directly ground a KnowledgeClaim — it must go through Belief first). This rule is evaluated by the Ontological Register.

### 2.4 How APEX Knows What Exists

APEX knows what exists through the constitutional principle of Fabric Presence: an entity exists within the Reality Fabric if and only if it satisfies all six URO Layers and is in an active or terminal (not pre-constitutional or suspended) state.

"Knowing what exists" means: being able to enumerate all objects in the fabric, organized by D0 category, filtered by LifecycleState, with full Layer content accessible.

The Registry system (lib/registry/) is the operational implementation of Fabric Presence tracking. Its entity graph, capabilities graph, and relationship graph collectively implement the constitutional requirement. The Registry Kernel (registry/kernel.js) is the operational surface for fabric interrogation.

### 2.5 How APEX Knows How Objects Relate

APEX knows how objects relate through the URO-Relationship structure: every relationship is a first-class URO object with explicit type, source, target, and full provenance. The graph is not implicit — no relationship may be inferred without being constitutionally represented.

"Knowing how objects relate" means: being able to enumerate all URO-Relationships between any two objects, with their types, timestamps, authorizations, and current lifecycle states.

The existing registry/relationships/ system is the operational implementation. Its reverse-relation queries and relationship discovery mechanisms implement the constitutional requirement.

### 2.6 How APEX Knows Whether Relationships Remain Valid

APEX knows whether relationships remain valid through continuous coherence evaluation: every active URO-Relationship is assessed by all relevant CoherenceRegisters. A relationship whose TargetObject has been superseded is not automatically invalid — it remains a valid historical record but must be assessed for current operational validity.

"Knowing whether relationships remain valid" means: being able to evaluate, for any relationship, whether its constitutional preconditions (authority, epistemic grounding, temporal ordering, value alignment, identity consistency) are still satisfied.

## PART 3: REALITY FABRIC AXIOMS

The following axioms are the constitutional constraints governing the Reality Fabric. They derive from D-1 axioms but are specific to the fabric as a constitutional environment.

**RF-A1 — Fabric Totality:**
Every valid URO object exists inside the Reality Fabric. An object that has passed through all six URO Layers and entered the {active} state is in the fabric permanently. Entry into the fabric is irrevocable.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-S6 (non-erasability), D2 URO-A1 (constitutional completeness)

**RF-A2 — Relational Meaning:**
No object is meaningful without relationships. An isolated URO object carries no representational function within the civilization. Constitutional existence requires both valid object structure (D2) and valid relational participation (D3).

*Derived from:* D-1 A-R5 (reality as relational), GI-1 (no isolated active nodes)

**RF-A3 — Relationship First-Class Status:**
All relationships are first-class constitutional objects. No relationship may exist as a bare pointer, implicit link, or structural convention. Every relationship is a URO object satisfying all six Layers.

*Derived from:* D2 URO-A2 (constitutional legibility), D2 Section 4.1

**RF-A4 — Temporal Integrity:**
The Reality Fabric must preserve temporal evolution without exception. Every object's history — from origination through all SM changes through terminal transition — must remain accessible within the fabric. The present state of the fabric is always recoverable from its temporal record.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-S5 (temporal ordering), D2 Layer 6 (Historical Continuity)

**RF-A5 — Continuous Coherence Evaluability:**
The coherence of the Reality Fabric must be continuously evaluable. At any moment, it must be possible to determine the coherence status of any object, any relationship, and any sub-graph against all seven CoherenceRegisters. The fabric may not enter a state where coherence evaluation is impossible.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-C1 through A-C6 (coherence axioms), D2 Layer 5

**RF-A6 — Observation Primacy:**
The first object in any epistemic chain must be an Observation. No EvidenceRecord, Interpretation, Belief, or higher-stage object may exist without a traceable antecedent Observation. The Observation Boundary is the only constitutional entry point for information from external reality into the Reality Fabric.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-E1 (observation as foundation), D-1 A-E2 (observation before evidence)

**RF-A7 — Authority Non-Implication:**
No authority is implicit within the Reality Fabric. Every authority exercise must be represented as an explicit AuthorityRelationship traceable to FoundingRatification. The fabric may not contain objects that exercise authority without an explicit, constitutionally valid authority chain.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-G1 through A-G3 (authority axioms)

**RF-A8 — Historical Inalienability:**
The historical record of the Reality Fabric may not be altered. ChangeRecords, HistoricalRecords, and ProvenanceChains may only be appended to — never modified, backdated, or deleted. The fabric's past is constitutionally fixed.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-S7 (provenance completeness), D2 Layer 3 (append-only)

**RF-A9 — Coherence Violation Specificity:**
Every coherence violation must be specific, located, and named. The fabric may not contain a general "something is wrong" state. Every violation names the specific object, the specific register, the specific violated rule, and the detection occasion.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-C4 (coherence violation detection), D2 Layer 5

**RF-A10 — Reality Orientation:**
The Reality Fabric is not self-contained. It is constitutionally required to maintain grounding in external reality through the Observation Boundary. A fabric that has no observations traceable to external reality (ExternalStates or Occurrences) has lost its reality-tracking function and is in a constitutional failure state.

*Derived from:* D-2 RC-001 (reality is external), D-2 RC-011 (observation-first epistemology)

**RF-A11 — Single Founding Root Per Era:**
For each CivilizationalEra, there is exactly one FoundingRatification that serves as the root of all authority and provenance chains in that era. Multiple simultaneous founding claims within a single era constitute an unconditional Ontological Register violation.

*Derived from:* D-1 A-G5 (founding uniqueness), D2 F-1 resolution

**RF-A12 — Fabric Segment Reachability:**
The Reality Fabric must be constitutionally navigable: from any active object, a constitutional path must exist to the FoundingRatification through some combination of ProvenanceRelationships and AuthorityRelationships. An object with no such path is constitutionally disconnected and must enter the {suspended} state.

*Derived from:* D2 D2-RC-01, GI-5

## PART 4: OBJECT RELATIONSHIP ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 Relationship Creation

A URO-Relationship comes into existence through a **Constitutional Relationship Event (CRE)**. A CRE is itself a URO object (category: Occurrence) that records the moment and circumstances of a relationship's origination.

For a relationship to be created:

**Step 1 — Authorization Check:** The actor proposing the relationship must hold an AuthorityClaim of scope sufficient to authorize a relationship of this type between these two objects. Creating an EpistemicRelationship requires epistemic competence. Creating an AuthorityRelationship requires authority-system competence.

**Step 2 — Object State Validation:** Both SourceObject and TargetObject must be in the {active} state. A relationship may not be created from or to an object in {suspended}, {superseded}, {historical}, or {archived} state. (Historical relationships may reference historical objects, but the relationship itself cannot be newly created; it can only be recorded as a historical fact.)

**Step 3 — Type Compatibility Validation:** The RelationshipType must be compatible with the D0 categories of both SourceObject and TargetObject. An {interprets} relationship must have an EvidenceRecord as SourceObject and an Interpretation as TargetObject — not the reverse.

**Step 4 — Coherence Pre-Check:** Creating the relationship must not create an immediate coherence violation of type {constitutively impossible} (e.g., creating an epistemic cycle). Relationships that would create {conditionally invalid} coherence violations may be created but are immediately flagged.

**Step 5 — Layer Population:** The new relationship object populates all six URO Layers. The OriginationRecord references the CRE. The ExistenceAuthorization references the actor's AuthorityClaim.

**Step 6 — Coherence Re-evaluation:** After creation, the CoherenceStatus of both endpoints (SourceObject and TargetObject) must be re-evaluated in all registers where the new relationship is relevant.

### 4.2 Relationship Validation

A relationship is constitutionally valid when:

- All six URO Layers are populated
- SourceObject and TargetObject are non-null and exist in the fabric
- RelationshipType is compatible with the categories of both endpoints
- The relationship's authority chain terminates at FoundingRatification
- The relationship does not introduce a constitutively impossible coherence violation

A relationship is constitutionally suspect (flagged for review) when:
- The relationship introduces a conditionally invalid coherence violation pending resolution
- The TargetObject has been superseded (the relationship is historically accurate but operationally stale)
- The authorizing AuthorityClaim has been challenged and the challenge is unresolved

A relationship is constitutionally invalid when:
- Any endpoint is null or non-existent
- The RelationshipType is incompatible with endpoint categories
- The authority chain is broken
- The relationship introduces an epistemic or provenance cycle

### 4.3 Relationship Modification

URO-Relationships may not be modified after origination in the following fields:
- SourceObject
- TargetObject
- RelationshipType
- OriginationRecord

The following fields may be modified:
- The SemanticProfile (the meaning or description of the relationship — but not its structural type)
- The CoherenceStatus (updated by coherence evaluation)
- The LifecycleRecord (lifecycle transitions)

Every modification to a modifiable field generates a ChangeRecord in the relationship's Layer 3.

### 4.4 Relationship Supersession

A relationship is superseded when:

1. The nature of the connection between SourceObject and TargetObject has changed such that the existing RelationshipType no longer accurately represents the connection (e.g., a {consistent-with} relationship becomes a {violates} relationship).

2. One or both endpoints have been superseded and the relationship must be re-established with their successors.

3. An authorizing actor's AuthorityClaim has been revoked, invalidating the relationship's authority chain.

Relationship supersession requires:
- An actor with sufficient authority to supersede relationships of this type
- A SupersessionLink on the relationship object pointing to its successor (if one exists)
- A ChangeRecord on the successor pointing back to the predecessor
- A coherence re-evaluation of all objects that had this relationship in their CoherenceObligations

**Critical:** A superseded relationship is not removed from the fabric. It becomes a historical record that this connection once held. Both the superseded relationship and its successor (if any) exist in the fabric simultaneously, with the superseded relationship in terminal state.

### 4.5 Relationship Historical Preservation

When a relationship enters a terminal state, all information about the relationship must be preserved:

- The full URO-Relationship object (all six Layers at terminal-state moment) is preserved as a HistoricalRecord
- The HistoricalAnchor records the terminal-state snapshot
- All objects that referenced this relationship retain their references — historical references are not retroactively removed
- The temporal fabric records the complete lifecycle of the relationship from origination to termination

### 4.6 The Constitutional Relationship Event as Architectural Record

Every relationship creation, modification, supersession, and termination produces a corresponding Constitutional Relationship Event (CRE) — an Occurrence in the fabric. This means the fabric contains not only objects and their relationships, but also the complete event history of how those relationships came into being and changed.

The CRE stream is one of the primary inputs to the Temporal Fabric (Part 8) and is the foundation of the Provenance Register's coherence evaluation.

## PART 5: REALITY SEGMENT INTERFACE

### 5.1 The Absolute Distinction

D-2 establishes that reality is external, independent, and not owned by the civilization. D3 enforces this through the Observation Boundary: the constitutional interface that separates external reality from APEX representation.

The distinction is not epistemological (APEX might be wrong about external reality). It is ontological: external reality and APEX representation are different kinds of things. External reality exists independently. APEX representation exists within the fabric. APEX representation may track external reality more or less accurately — but it never constitutes or controls it.

**The Observation Boundary is absolute.** No URO object other than an Observation may have a direct referential link to an ExternalState or Occurrence. All other epistemic objects (EvidenceRecord, Interpretation, etc.) reference the observation of external reality, not external reality itself.

### 5.2 How External Reality Enters the Reality Fabric

External reality enters the Reality Fabric through exactly one mechanism: **Observation**.

An Observation is the first URO object in every epistemic chain. It is created when an Observer (a constitutionally recognized Actor with ObserverCapability) perceives, receives, or registers an ExternalState or Occurrence.

The entry process:

**Step 1 — Contact:** An external state or occurrence becomes available to an Observer. "Available" means within the Observer's designated scope and sensitivity range. The availability itself is not yet a fabric event — it is still in external reality.

**Step 2 — Sensing Act:** The Observer performs a sensing act — a deliberate or automatic registration of the ExternalState or Occurrence. The sensing act is an internal event to APEX and produces an Occurrence (URO object).

**Step 3 — Observation Formation:** The Observer creates an Observation object. The Observation's ReferentialLink points to the ExternalReference (a handle to the ExternalState or Occurrence that remains outside the fabric). The Observation does not import the external state — it records that the sensing act occurred and points to what was sensed.

**Step 4 — Observation Constitutional Validation:** The Observation must satisfy all six URO Layers. Critically: the Observation's ExistenceAuthorization must trace to a FoundingRatification, establishing that this Observer has legitimate epistemic standing to make observations in this domain.

**Step 5 — Fabric Entry:** The constitutionally valid Observation enters the fabric. External reality remains outside. The connection between internal and external is maintained through the ExternalReference in the Observation's ReferentialLink — a pointer that acknowledges external reality without claiming to contain it.

### 5.3 RealitySegment Reference Model

A RealitySegment is an external-reality object: a bounded portion of external reality that the civilization is tracking. RealitySegments do not exist in the Reality Fabric. They exist in external reality and are referenced by APEX objects.

Within the Reality Fabric, RealitySegments appear as:
- **ExternalReference handles** in Observation ReferentialLinks
- **RealitySegment-type entries** in an RLReferentHandle — a constitutional placeholder that acknowledges the existence of an external referent

The RealitySegment Reference Model specifies:
```
RealitySegmentHandle {
    SegmentIdentifier    : ExternalReference    [1..1]
    SegmentScope         : ScopedDescription    [1..1]
    BoundaryDefinition   : ScopedDescription    [1..1]
    FirstObservedAt      : TemporalPosition     [1..1]
    LastObservedAt       : TemporalPosition     [1..1]
    ObservationCount     : integer              [1..1]
    GroundingQuality     : GroundingQuality     [1..1]
}
```

The RealitySegmentHandle is carried within the RL Layer of any Observation that references this RealitySegment. It does not represent the RealitySegment — it records APEX's knowledge of the RealitySegment's identity, scope, and observation history.

### 5.4 ExternalState Reference Model

An ExternalState is a condition that holds within a RealitySegment at a given time. ExternalStates are ephemeral — they are the "facts about the world right now" that APEX observes.

Within the Reality Fabric, ExternalStates appear only through Observations. An Observation captures that a specific ExternalState was perceived at a specific time by a specific Observer.

```
ExternalStateHandle {
    StateIdentifier      : ExternalReference    [1..1]
    ObservedAt           : TemporalPosition     [1..1]
    ObservingSegment     : RealitySegmentHandle [1..1]
    ObserverReference    : ActorProfile-ref     [1..1]
    SensedProperties     : [PropositionalContent] [1..*]
    ObservationUncertainty : ConfidenceLevel    [1..1]
}
```

The ExternalStateHandle is the constitutional record of what was observed. It does not claim to fully capture the ExternalState — it records what was perceived and how confidently.

### 5.5 Occurrence Reference Model

Occurrences are events that happen in external reality or at the boundary between external reality and APEX (including internal APEX events). The constitutional treatment differs:

**External Occurrences:** Events that happen in external reality (independent of APEX). These are observed by APEX exactly as ExternalStates are — through Observation acts that produce ExternalOccurrenceHandle records.

**Boundary Occurrences:** Events at the APEX-reality interface — the sensing act itself. These are URO objects (category: Occurrence, as defined in D0) and exist within the fabric.

**Internal Occurrences:** Events within APEX — agent decisions, task executions, governance acts. These are URO objects (various categories depending on their epistemic stage) and are fully within the fabric.

The key distinction:
- External Occurrences → referenced by Observations (not in fabric)
- Boundary Occurrences → URO Occurrences in fabric (the sensing events)
- Internal Occurrences → URO objects in fabric (decisions, actions, etc.)

### 5.6 Observer Infrastructure Constitutional Requirements

The existing observer-health system (lib/observer-health/index.js) implements sensor registry, calibration, and health measurement. Constitutionally, Observers are Actors with ObserverCapability. Their constitutional standing requires:

1. **Registration:** Each Observer must be a constitutionally valid Actor (Entity + ActorProfile) with an explicit ObserverCapability claim
2. **Scope Definition:** Each Observer must have a defined observation scope — which RealitySegments and ExternalState types it is authorized to observe
3. **Calibration Record:** Each Observer must maintain a CompetenceDemonstration record that includes calibration history — evidence that its observations are reliable within its declared scope
4. **Health Assessment:** The Observer's current reliability must be assessable through its AccountabilityRecord. A degraded Observer produces lower-GroundingQuality observations, which propagates uncertainty through the epistemic chain
5. **Authority Chain:** The Observer's authority to observe must trace to FoundingRatification

The 9-dimensional health scoring already implemented (coverage, accuracy, freshness, coherence, completeness, depth, evidence_quality, projection_alignment, gap_coverage) maps directly onto the Observer's CompetenceDemonstration and the GroundingQuality field of observations it produces.

## PART 6: EPISTEMIC FABRIC

### 6.1 Epistemic Topology

The epistemic fabric is the topological structure formed by all epistemic chain relationships in the Reality Fabric. It has the following mandatory properties:

**Property EP-T1 — DAG Structure:** The epistemic fabric is a directed acyclic graph. Epistemic cycles are unconditionally invalid.

**Property EP-T2 — Observation Roots:** Every connected component of the epistemic fabric must have at least one Observation as a root (an object with no epistemic predecessors). Components without an Observation root are constitutionally ungrounded.

**Property EP-T3 — Stage Monotonicity:** Moving along any epistemic path (following epistemic edges in their direction), the ChainPosition.Stage may only increase or stay the same (in the case of parallel epistemic paths that merge). It may never decrease. A KnowledgeClaim may not feed back into an EvidenceRecord.

**Property EP-T4 — Validation Gates:** The transition from Belief to KnowledgeClaim is a constitutionally mandatory validation gate. No object may be classified as a KnowledgeClaim without explicitly passing through the validation gate — a formal ValidationProcess that produces a ValidationStatus update in the object's L2.2 EpistemicGrounding.

**Property EP-T5 — Understanding Synthesis:** The transition from KnowledgeClaim to UnderstandingModel is a constitutionally mandatory synthesis gate. An UnderstandingModel must integrate multiple KnowledgeClaims — a single KnowledgeClaim may not alone constitute an UnderstandingModel. The minimum integration requirement is two KnowledgeClaims with documented synthesis rationale.

### 6.2 The Epistemic Chain — Stage Specifications

**Stage 1 — Observation**
- Origin: Sensing act by a constitutionally recognized Observer
- Grounding: ExternalStateHandle or ExternalOccurrenceHandle
- Uncertainty: Mandatory UncertaintyDeclaration. No Observation may claim 100% accuracy.
- Outputs: May ground EvidenceRecords (via {grounds} relationship)
- Forbidden inputs: May not receive epistemic inputs from higher-stage objects

**Stage 2 — EvidenceRecord**
- Origin: Qualifying judgment by an epistemic actor — assessing relevance and reliability of one or more Observations
- Grounding: Must cite at least one Observation in its JustificationPath
- Key distinction: Evidence-formation is relevance-assessment, not meaning-assignment (D-2 v1.2)
- Uncertainty: May not reduce uncertainty below the minimum of all cited Observations
- Outputs: May be interpreted (via {grounds} relationship to Interpretation)
- Forbidden: Direct grounding of Beliefs, KnowledgeClaims, Decisions, or Actions without passing through Interpretation

**Stage 3 — Interpretation**
- Origin: Meaning-assignment act — determining what the EvidenceRecord reveals about reality
- Grounding: Must cite at least one EvidenceRecord. May cite multiple EvidenceRecords.
- Key distinction: Interpretation introduces an InterpretiveFrame — a perspective, model, or schema that shapes the meaning assigned. The InterpretiveFrame must be explicitly declared.
- Uncertainty: May not reduce uncertainty below the minimum of all cited EvidenceRecords, adjusted for InterpretiveFrame confidence
- Outputs: May support Beliefs (via {grounds} relationship)

**Stage 4 — Belief**
- Origin: Commitment act — an actor commits to a proposition as representing their current best understanding
- Grounding: Must cite at least one Interpretation. May cite multiple conflicting Interpretations.
- Properties: BeliefStatus: {forming, held, challenged, revised, abandoned}. Revisions exceeding two in a short timeframe constitute a "dangerous belief state" (flagged by the existing beliefs system)
- Uncertainty: Explicit ConfidenceLevel required. Beliefs at ConfidenceLevel < 0.3 should not proceed to KnowledgeClaim without additional evidence
- Validation: Not yet required at this stage. The actor commits based on their epistemic assessment.
- Outputs: May support KnowledgeClaims (via {grounds} relationship) subject to validation gate

**Stage 5 — KnowledgeClaim (Validation Gate)**
- Origin: Validation act — a formal assessment confirming that a Belief meets the civilization's knowledge standards
- Grounding: Must cite at least one Belief AND a completed ValidationProcess
- ValidationProcess: Formal review by an Actor with epistemic validation authority. Must document: validation method, validation scope, validation outcome, and validator's AuthorityClaim
- Dual requirement: BOTH justification (epistemic chain) AND validation (independent review) are required. Neither alone is sufficient.
- KnowledgeStatus: {validating, validated, contested, superseded}
- Outputs: May contribute to UnderstandingModels (via {contributes-to} relationship) subject to synthesis gate

**Stage 6 — UnderstandingModel (Synthesis Gate)**
- Origin: Synthesis act — integrating multiple KnowledgeClaims into a coherent model of how a domain of reality works
- Grounding: Must cite minimum two KnowledgeClaims with documented synthesis rationale
- Synthesis requirement: UnderstandingModel must demonstrate integration — it must not be a mere concatenation of KnowledgeClaims. The integration must resolve tensions between claims, identify patterns across claims, and produce understanding not derivable from any single claim.
- The existing 6-dimensional scoring (structural, behavioral, relational, temporal, causal, predictive) maps directly to the UnderstandingModel's ModelCoherence field
- ModelCoherence: Evaluated across all six dimensions. Minimum viable coherence: 0.6 across all six dimensions
- Outputs: May ground CivilizationalDecisions

**Stage 7 — CivilizationalDecision**
- Origin: Deliberation act — formally selecting among considered alternatives within a PossibilitySet
- Grounding: Must cite at least one UnderstandingModel. Must have an associated PossibilitySet.
- PossibilitySet requirement: A CivilizationalDecision is constitutionally incomplete without a corresponding PossibilitySet. The PossibilitySet must have been open (accepting PossibleActions) before the Decision is made and closed (no further alternatives accepted) at Decision time.
- Authority requirement: CivilizationalDecisions require an explicit AuthorityClaim authorizing the decision in this domain
- Outputs: Authorizes CivilizationalActions (the selected PossibleAction becomes authorized)

**Stage 8 — CivilizationalAction**
- Origin: Execution act — carrying out what was decided
- Grounding: Must cite exactly one CivilizationalDecision
- Distinction from PossibleAction: A PossibleAction was considered; a CivilizationalAction is executed. Selection (in Decision) ≠ Execution (in Action). An action may fail to execute even if selected.
- Authority requirement: CivilizationalAction requires an explicit AuthorityClaim authorizing execution (which may differ from the decision authority)
- Outputs: Produces ActionEffects

**Stage 9 — ActionEffect**
- Origin: Effect occurrence — the actual changes produced in the environment by the CivilizationalAction
- Grounding: Must cite at least one CivilizationalAction
- Reality Bridge: ActionEffects are the point at which APEX action meets external reality. They reference ExternalStateHandle records capturing what actually changed in the world. This makes ActionEffect the "output boundary" — symmetric to Observation as the "input boundary."
- ActionEffects may be positive (intended changes occurred), negative (unintended changes occurred), or null (no changes occurred despite the action)
- Outputs: Are recorded in ActionTraces

**Stage 10 — ActionTrace**
- Origin: Recording act — the permanent record of what was done and what resulted
- Grounding: Must cite at least one ActionEffect and one CivilizationalAction
- Purpose: Closes the epistemic loop. ActionTraces are the material from which future Observations may be generated (when the civilization observes its own outputs), enabling the civilization to learn from its own actions.
- ActionTraces are always terminal in the epistemic chain — they are not themselves inputs to further epistemic stages. They are historical records.

### 6.3 Allowed and Forbidden Epistemic Transitions

**Allowed transitions (epistemic path edges):**

| From | To | Relationship Type |
|------|----|------------------|
| Observation | EvidenceRecord | grounds |
| Observation | EvidenceRecord (multiple) | grounds |
| EvidenceRecord | Interpretation | grounds |
| EvidenceRecord (multiple) | Interpretation | grounds |
| Interpretation | Belief | grounds |
| Interpretation (multiple) | Belief | grounds |
| Belief | KnowledgeClaim | grounds (subject to validation) |
| Belief (multiple) | KnowledgeClaim | grounds (subject to validation) |
| KnowledgeClaim | UnderstandingModel | contributes-to (subject to synthesis) |
| KnowledgeClaim (multiple) | UnderstandingModel | contributes-to (minimum two) |
| UnderstandingModel | CivilizationalDecision | grounds |
| CivilizationalDecision | CivilizationalAction | authorizes |
| CivilizationalAction | ActionEffect | produces |
| ActionEffect | ActionTrace | recorded-in |

**Constitutionally forbidden transitions:**

| Attempted Skip | Reason Forbidden |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Observation → Belief (skip Evidence and Interpretation) | Bypasses qualifying judgment and meaning-assignment |
| Observation → KnowledgeClaim (any skip) | Bypasses all intermediate processing |
| EvidenceRecord → Belief (skip Interpretation) | Evidence is not yet meaning — it requires an interpretive act |
| Interpretation → KnowledgeClaim (skip Belief) | Commitment act is constitutionally required before validation |
| Belief → UnderstandingModel (skip KnowledgeClaim) | Unvalidated beliefs cannot directly constitute understanding |
| KnowledgeClaim → CivilizationalDecision (skip Understanding) | Decisions must be grounded in integrated understanding, not individual claims |
| CivilizationalAction → ActionTrace (skip ActionEffect) | Actions must produce observable effects before they can be traced |
| ActionTrace → Observation (backward loop) | Epistemic chain is acyclic |

### 6.4 Epistemic Failure States

**EFS-1 — Ungrounded Object:** An epistemic object exists with no preceding stage in its epistemic chain. Resolution: Trace grounding chain; if unresolvable, reclassify as Belief (the lowest stage that permits partial grounding) and mark as suspect.

**EFS-2 — Stage Skip:** An object claims a ChainPosition.Stage but lacks the required priors for that stage. Resolution: Constitutionally invalid — object must be reclassified to the highest valid stage it can legitimately occupy.

**EFS-3 — Invalid Validation:** An object claims KnowledgeClaim status but its ValidationProcess was performed by an Actor without validation authority. Resolution: KnowledgeStatus reverts to {validating}; re-validation required with properly authorized validator.

**EFS-4 — Understanding Without Synthesis:** An UnderstandingModel cites only one KnowledgeClaim or provides no synthesis rationale. Resolution: Reclassify as KnowledgeClaim or provide additional claims and synthesis.

**EFS-5 — Decision Without PossibilitySet:** A CivilizationalDecision has no associated PossibilitySet. Resolution: Constitutionally incomplete. PossibilitySet must be retroactively reconstructed (with documented rationale for reconstruction) or the Decision must be reclassified.

**EFS-6 — Epistemic Cycle:** Objects A and B are in each other's epistemic chains (A grounds B, B grounds A). Resolution: The cycle is constitutionally impossible. One of the grounding claims is fabricated or erroneous. Audit required; the incorrect grounding must be removed and documented in a ChangeRecord.

## PART 7: AUTHORITY FABRIC

### 7.1 Authority Fabric Definition

The authority fabric is the sub-graph of the Reality Fabric formed by all AuthorityRelationships. It has a tree structure with FoundingRatification at the root (per era) and every authority exercise as a leaf.

Constitutional properties of the authority fabric:

**Property AU-F1 — Tree Structure Per Era:** The authority fabric for each CivilizationalEra is a tree (acyclic, connected, single-root). No directed cycles, no multiple roots.

**Property AU-F2 — Descending Authority:** Authority flows only downward in the tree — from FoundingRatification through AuthoritySources through AuthorityClaims to specific exercises. Authority may never flow upward or laterally.

**Property AU-F3 — Scope Constraint:** Authority exercised at any node in the tree may not exceed the scope of the authority held by the parent node. Scope narrows monotonically as authority is delegated downward.

**Property AU-F4 — Explicit Delegation:** Authority is never inherited by default or conferred by proximity. Every authority delegation must be an explicit URO-Relationship of type {delegates-to} with full constitutional documentation.

### 7.2 Authority Sources

AuthoritySources are the categories of legitimate authority within the civilization, established by FoundingRatification. The existing system recognizes:

- **Human authority:** The founder/human actor whose founding act constitutes the FoundingRatification
- **Delegated agent authority:** Authority delegated to specific Agents for specific domains, traceable to human authority
- **Role-based authority:** Authority attached to a Role (ARCHITECT, DEVELOPER, TESTER, REVIEWER, VALIDATOR) and inherited by any Actor filling that Role
- **System authority:** Authority held by constitutional system components (Registry Kernel, Memory Gateway, Kernel Chain) for executing their constitutional functions

The existing kernel's four-gate chain (resolveIdentity → resolveOwnership → checkAuthority → checkGovernance) is the operational implementation of authority source verification. The kernel resolves what authority the requesting Actor holds before any action proceeds.

### 7.3 Authority Propagation

Authority propagates through the fabric through explicit delegation chains:

```
FoundingRatification
  └── AuthoritySource (Human)
       ├── AuthorityClaim (domain: all — held by founder)
       │    └── AuthorityClaim (domain: intelligence — delegated to Intelligence agents)
       │         └── AuthorityClaim (domain: epistemic validation — delegated to Validator role)
       └── AuthorityClaim (domain: system — held by Registry Kernel)
            └── AuthorityClaim (domain: entity management — delegated to system agents)
```

Every node in this chain is a URO object. Every delegation edge is a URO-Relationship. The entire chain is navigable, auditable, and historically preserved.

### 7.4 Delegation Architecture

A delegation from Actor A to Actor B for scope S requires:

1. Actor A holds an AuthorityClaim for scope S (or a super-scope of S)
2. A constitutionally valid delegation event (URO Occurrence) is recorded
3. A new AuthorityClaim is created for Actor B with scope S
4. A {delegates-to} URO-Relationship connects A's AuthorityClaim to B's new AuthorityClaim
5. B's AuthorityClaim carries its parent reference (A's AuthorityClaim) and scope constraints
6. The delegation is recorded in both A's and B's AccountabilityRecords

Delegation does not transfer authority — it creates derived authority. A's AuthorityClaim remains active after delegation. B's authority is revocable by A without affecting A's authority.

### 7.5 Standing Approvals as Pre-Authorized Delegation

The existing standing approvals system (pgCreateStandingApproval) is the operational implementation of pre-authorized delegation. A standing approval is a constitutionally bounded AuthorityClaim granted in advance for a defined action pattern. Constitutionally:

- Each standing approval creates a bounded AuthorityClaim
- The scope is defined by the action pattern (eligible action types, resource types, cost limits)
- The standing approval is revocable (pgDisableAgentSchedule analog)
- Each use of a standing approval generates an AccountabilityRecord

### 7.6 Accountability Architecture

Every authority exercise — whether by human, agent, or system — generates a corresponding AccountabilityRecord. The AccountabilityRecord is a URO object that:

- Identifies the Actor who exercised authority
- Identifies the AuthorityClaim used
- Identifies the specific action taken
- Records the outcome
- Records any cost, risk, or quality scores
- Remains permanently in the fabric

The 40-domain governance system (lib/governance.js) is the operational implementation of accountability record generation. Its domains (execution graphs, agent decisions, memory versions, evidence chains, certifications, cost accounting, etc.) all correspond to accountability record types.

### 7.7 Challenge Mechanism

Any Actor with a legitimate epistemic interest may challenge an authority exercise by creating an AuthorityChallenge object. The challenge:

- Identifies the specific AuthorityClaim or delegation being challenged
- Provides the grounds for challenge (epistemic, procedural, scope violation, conflict of interest)
- Triggers an Authority Register coherence evaluation on the challenged claim and all objects authorized by it
- Remains in the fabric until either dismissed (with documented grounds) or upheld (triggering authority chain revision)

During an unresolved challenge, objects whose ExistenceAuthorization depends on the challenged AuthorityClaim enter a {contested} sub-state within {active}. They remain accessible but are flagged for review.

## PART 8: TEMPORAL FABRIC

### 8.1 Temporal Model

The temporal fabric is the constitutional structure governing how the Reality Fabric exists and changes across time. The temporal model has three registers:

**Register T1 — Present State:** The current state of all active URO objects and relationships. This is what most operational queries will access.

**Register T2 — Historical Record:** All past states of all URO objects and relationships, preserved through ChangeRecords, HistoricalRecords, and HistoricalAnchors. Every past state must be reconstructible.

**Register T3 — Constrained Future:** The set of constitutionally valid future states — states that do not violate any D3 axiom, any GI, any GCR, or any prior constitutional document. The temporal fabric does not predict the future; it defines the boundary of constitutionally valid futures.

### 8.2 Origination

Every URO object has a precise origination moment: the InstantiationOccasion in its StructuralAnchor. Origination is the moment when the object completes all six URO Layers and transitions from {pre-constitutional} to {active}.

The origination moment is:
- Immutable — may never be backdated or modified
- Unique — two different objects may not share an identical InstantiationOccasion (within the precision of the temporal system)
- Referenced by all subsequent events in the object's lifecycle

### 8.3 Evolution

An object evolves when its SemanticProfile changes — when the content it carries changes while its identity (SC) remains constant. Evolution is not a state transition — the object remains {active} throughout.

Every evolution event:
- Updates SemanticProfile.CurrentContent and LastUpdatedAt
- Appends a ChangeRecord to SemanticProfile.UpdateHistory
- Appends a ChangeRecord to ModificationHistory.Records (Layer 3)
- Triggers coherence re-evaluation in all relevant registers

The temporal fabric preserves evolution by making UpdateHistory append-only. Every prior state is recoverable by traversing the ChangeRecord chain backward.

### 8.4 Supersession

Supersession is the constitutional mechanism for identity change. When an object's SC must change (CategoryMembership change, ScopeDefinition change, etc.), supersession creates a new object and links the old to the new.

The temporal fabric records supersession through:
- The predecessor's SupersessionLink (Layer 6) pointing to successor
- The successor's OriginationRecord (Layer 3) citing the supersession event
- A HistoricalAnchor capturing the predecessor's complete state at supersession moment
- A HistoricalRelationship {supersedes} from successor to predecessor

The predecessor remains in the fabric permanently in {superseded} state. The temporal fabric preserves the complete lineage: current active object → its predecessor chain → the original founding object.

### 8.5 Historical State Preservation and Reconstruction

For any TemporalPosition T in the civilization's history, the state of the Reality Fabric at T must be reconstructible. Reconstruction requires:

1. Take all objects whose OriginationRecord.OriginatingOccasion ≤ T
2. For each object, traverse its ChangeRecord chain to find the state at T
3. For each object in terminal state before T, include the terminal state
4. For each relationship, apply the same logic

This reconstruction capability is what makes the temporal fabric more than a snapshot system. It is a constitutional requirement that the fabric's history is fully preserved and fully navigable.

The existing state/snapshot system (captureStateSnapshot, captureEnvironmentSnapshot in governance.js) implements periodic Fabric Checkpoints. Constitutionally, a Fabric Checkpoint is a constitutionally recognized full-state snapshot that makes reconstruction faster (you can start from the nearest checkpoint rather than from the beginning). Checkpoints do not replace the full reconstruction capability — they optimize it.

### 8.6 Causality Preservation

The temporal fabric enforces causal ordering: cause must precede effect. This is encoded in GCR-4 (temporal causality) and enforced by the Temporal Register.

Causal relationships in the fabric include:
- Epistemic grounding (EvidenceRecord grounds Interpretation; evidence must precede interpretation)
- Authority delegation (parent AuthorityClaim must precede child AuthorityClaim)
- Object origination (an object cannot reference a predecessor that doesn't exist yet)
- Action-effect (CivilizationalAction must precede its ActionEffect)

Causality violations (any edge from a later object to an earlier object where temporal precedence is constitutionally required) are Temporal Register coherence violations.

### 8.7 Era Management

A CivilizationalEra is a bounded temporal period with a single FoundingRatification. All objects created within an era carry that era's EraDesignation in their SC.

Era transitions occur through a refounding event. Constitutionally:
- The prior era closes when the new FoundingRatification is established
- All objects from prior eras retain their original EraDesignation — they do not get re-stamped
- Cross-era references are constitutionally valid for historical records
- Cross-era authority chains are not valid — authority does not persist across era boundaries without explicit re-ratification
- The fabric spans all eras in its historical record; the present-state registers contain only the current era's active objects

## PART 9: COHERENCE FABRIC

### 9.1 Coherence Architecture Overview

The coherence fabric is the continuous evaluation infrastructure that assesses whether the Reality Fabric satisfies its constitutional requirements. It operates across seven registers, each focused on a specific dimension of constitutional integrity.

Coherence evaluation is not a batch job. It is triggered by every constitutional event (object creation, modification, relationship creation, relationship supersession, lifecycle transition) and runs against all affected objects and relationships.

The existing cognitive/resolver.js and intelligence/contradiction-engine.js are operational implementations of coherence detection. D3 defines the constitutional architecture of what they detect and how.

### 9.2 Register Specifications

**Register 1 — Ontological Register**

Focus: Category membership, type compatibility, structural validity.

What it evaluates:
- Is every object's CategoryMembership a valid D0/D1 category?
- Are all relationship types compatible with the categories of their endpoints?
- Are all specialization constraints (from D2 Part 5) satisfied?
- Do any objects claim category properties they do not possess?

Coherence rules: GCR-7 (ontological soundness), GI-8 (category membership stability), GI-9 (relationship type immutability)

Suspension trigger: Any active object violating ontological soundness is immediately constitutionally suspended.

**Register 2 — Epistemic Register**

Focus: Epistemic chain integrity, stage validity, validation completeness.

What it evaluates:
- Is every epistemic object at the stage it claims?
- Are all required priors present for every stage?
- Is every KnowledgeClaim validated?
- Are there epistemic cycles?
- Does every UnderstandingModel have minimum two KnowledgeClaims with synthesis rationale?

Coherence rules: GI-3 (acyclic epistemic), GCR-1 (epistemic chain completeness), RF-A6 (observation primacy)

Suspension trigger: Any KnowledgeClaim or UnderstandingModel with broken epistemic chain is suspended.

**Register 3 — Authority Register**

Focus: Authority chain validity, delegation soundness, accountability completeness.

What it evaluates:
- Does every object's authority chain terminate at FoundingRatification?
- Are all delegations within scope?
- Do all authority exercises have AccountabilityRecords?
- Are there open unresolved AuthorityChallenges affecting active objects?

Coherence rules: GI-5 (single authority root), GCR-2 (authority chain completeness), RF-A7 (authority non-implication)

Suspension trigger: Any object whose authority chain is broken or whose exercise is unchallenged (unresolved) is flagged for review.

**Register 4 — Value Register**

Focus: Value alignment, TerminalValue integrity, value derivation soundness.

What it evaluates:
- Do any active CivilizationalDecisions or CivilizationalActions conflict with TerminalValues without resolution?
- Are all InstrumentalValues properly derived?
- Have any new TerminalValues been asserted without FoundingRatification authorization?

Coherence rules: GCR-6 (value alignment), D-1 A-V1 through A-V8

Suspension trigger: Any object asserting a new TerminalValue without FoundingRatification is suspended immediately.

**Register 5 — Temporal Register**

Focus: Temporal ordering, causality, era consistency.

What it evaluates:
- Does every causal relationship have the cause preceding the effect?
- Are all objects' EraDesignations consistent with their InstantiationOccasion?
- Are cross-era authority chains properly bounded?
- Are there impossible timelines (effect before cause)?

Coherence rules: GCR-4 (temporal causality), GI-6 (temporal ordering consistency)

Suspension trigger: Objects involved in impossible timelines are suspended pending temporal reconstruction.

**Register 6 — Identity Register**

Focus: Three-aspect consistency, SC immutability, ActorProfile consistency.

What it evaluates:
- Has any object's SC been modified after origination?
- Do all ActorProfiles share SC correctly with their underlying Entities?
- Are RL grounding claims consistent with the evidence they cite?
- Are there identity conflicts (two active objects with identical SC)?

Coherence rules: GCR-5 (identity consistency), GI-8 (category membership stability)

Suspension trigger: Objects with modified SC are immediately suspended — SC modification is constitutionally impossible, so detection means either corruption or fabrication.

**Register 7 — Provenance Register**

Focus: Provenance chain completeness, non-backdating, append-only enforcement.

What it evaluates:
- Are all KnowledgeClaims' and UnderstandingModels' provenance chains complete?
- Have any ChangeRecords been modified after creation?
- Are there provenance cycles?
- Are any ProvenanceLinks backdated (linking an object to a prior object whose origination postdates the link's creation)?

Coherence rules: GI-4 (acyclic provenance), GCR-3 (provenance chain completeness), RF-A8 (historical inalienability)

Suspension trigger: Objects with modified ChangeRecords are immediately suspended — this indicates historical record tampering.

### 9.3 Detection

Coherence violations are detected through two mechanisms:

**Triggered Detection:** Every constitutional event (object creation, modification, relationship creation, relationship supersession, lifecycle transition) triggers coherence evaluation in all registers relevant to the affected objects. The evaluation examines the affected objects and all objects in their CoherenceObligations.

**Continuous Scan:** A background constitutional evaluation process periodically scans all active objects in all registers. This catches violations that emerge from time-based conditions (e.g., an object's ValidUntil has elapsed; an authority chain has reached its ReviewPeriod without review; an UnderstandingModel's source KnowledgeClaims have been superseded).

### 9.4 Propagation

When a coherence violation is detected, it propagates according to the following rules:

**EP-P1 — Immediate Propagation to Dependents:** When object A enters a CoherenceViolation state, every object that lists A in its CoherenceObligations.DependsCoherentlyOn must re-evaluate its coherence status. If the dependency is violated, the dependent object also enters CoherenceViolation.

**EP-P2 — Authority Chain Propagation:** When an AuthorityClaim enters CoherenceViolation, all objects whose ExistenceAuthorization.AuthorityChain includes this claim must re-evaluate. This can cascade through large sub-graphs.

**EP-P3 — Epistemic Chain Propagation:** When an Observation or EvidenceRecord enters CoherenceViolation, all epistemic objects downstream in the chain must re-evaluate. A corrupted observation can invalidate the entire epistemic chain above it.

**EP-P4 — Bounded Propagation:** Propagation stops when it reaches an object whose coherence evaluation is unaffected by the violation (e.g., an object that does not depend on the violated object). Propagation does not cascade infinitely.

**EP-P5 — Historical Non-Propagation:** Violations in active objects do not propagate to historical objects. Historical objects are constitutionally frozen; past coherence evaluations are preserved as they were.

### 9.5 Resolution

A CoherenceViolation is resolved through a ResolutionRecord — a URO object that documents how the violation was addressed.

Resolution types:

**RT-1 — Repair:** The violated constraint is satisfied by correcting the object (e.g., providing a missing epistemic prior, completing a provenance chain). The CoherenceViolation enters {resolved-by-repair} state. The object's CoherenceStatus returns to {Consistent}.

**RT-2 — Reclassification:** The object is reclassified to a lower epistemic stage or different category that it legitimately occupies. This requires a Supersession (new object at correct classification) and a ResolutionRecord explaining the reclassification.

**RT-3 — Supersession:** The violated object is superseded by a constitutionally valid successor. The predecessor remains in the fabric with its CoherenceViolation recorded as part of its historical state.

**RT-4 — Dismissal:** The violation is determined to be a false positive — the evaluation rule was incorrectly applied. The ResolutionRecord must document the specific reason for dismissal. The CoherenceViolation enters {dismissed} state with documented grounds.

**RT-5 — Unresolvable Documentation:** The violation cannot be resolved (e.g., a provenance cycle in a founding-era record) but cannot be corrected without violating historical inalienability. The CoherenceViolation is documented as {known-unresolvable} and the affected objects are flagged permanently. This does not affect the object's active status unless the violation also prevents constitutional completeness.

### 9.6 Suspension and Recovery

**Suspension** occurs when an object's coherence violation in the Ontological or Identity Register is of severity {constitutively invalid} — the object cannot operate without resolving the violation.

Suspended objects:
- Remain in the fabric
- May not be referenced by new objects
- May not authorize new actions
- Are visible for audit purposes
- Are not deleted or archived

**Recovery** from suspension requires resolution via RT-1, RT-2, or RT-4. After resolution:
- The CoherenceViolation is marked {resolved}
- The object's CoherenceStatus returns to {Consistent}
- The object exits suspension
- All objects that were blocked from referencing this object may proceed
- The suspension period is recorded in the LifecycleRecord as a transition (active → suspended → active)

## PART 10: REALITY FABRIC FAILURE MODES

### FM-1 — Orphan Objects

**Scenario:** A URO object exists in the fabric with no relationships — zero incoming and zero outgoing edges.

**Detection:** GI-1 violation (no isolated active nodes). Detected by identity scan during object origination or by continuous scan.

**Constitutional consequence:** The object is constitutionally non-participatory. It satisfies URO structure (six Layers) but fails the relational completeness requirement of the Reality Fabric.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. Object enters {suspended} state immediately upon detection
2. Originating actor is notified via AccountabilityRecord
3. Actor has a defined ReviewPeriod to establish at least one constitutionally valid relationship
4. If no relationship established within ReviewPeriod, the object's LifecycleRecord transitions to {archived} with a documented rationale: "no fabric participation established"
5. A ResolutionRecord of type RT-5 (unresolvable for participation but preserved) is created

---

### FM-2 — Disconnected Sub-Graphs

**Scenario:** A group of objects forms a constitutionally valid sub-graph (all internally consistent, all related to each other) but is disconnected from the main fabric — no path exists from any object in the group to FoundingRatification.

**Detection:** RF-A12 violation (fabric segment reachability). Detected by authority chain traversal check during registration.

**Constitutional consequence:** The entire disconnected sub-graph is constitutionally unrooted — it has no legitimate authority anchor.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. All objects in the disconnected sub-graph enter {suspended} state
2. Audit: determine whether the sub-graph has a legitimate authority chain that was simply not recorded
3. If legitimate: establish the missing ProvenanceRelationship and AuthorityRelationship connecting the sub-graph to the main fabric. All objects exit suspension.
4. If illegitimate (sub-graph was constructed outside the constitutional framework): each object is individually assessed. Constitutionally valid objects with repairable chains are repaired. Constitutionally invalid objects are superseded by valid replacements or archived.

---

### FM-3 — Contradictory Objects

**Scenario:** Two active objects (A and B) make mutually exclusive claims about the same subject — both cannot be constitutionally correct simultaneously.

**Detection:** Contradiction detected by intelligence/contradiction-engine.js (operational) or by Epistemic Register coherence evaluation when both objects are cited in the same epistemic chain.

**Constitutional consequence:** Neither object is constitutionally suspended automatically by contradiction alone. Contradiction triggers a formal CoherenceViolation in the Epistemic Register for both objects and any object that depends on both.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. CoherenceViolation is created citing both contradicting objects
2. A deliberation is triggered: a PossibilitySet is created for the question "which claim is epistemically better supported?"
3. The PossibilitySet has two PossibleActions: maintain A and supersede B; maintain B and supersede A
4. Resolution via validation: additional evidence is sought or existing evidence is re-evaluated
5. Upon resolution: the less-supported claim is superseded with a ResolutionRecord documenting the grounds for rejection
6. The contradiction's history (both claims, the deliberation, the resolution) is permanently preserved

### FM-4 — Circular Provenance

**Scenario:** Object A's ProvenanceChain references Object B, and Object B's ProvenanceChain references Object A.

**Detection:** GI-4 violation (acyclic provenance). Detected by graph cycle analysis on the provenance sub-graph.

**Constitutional consequence:** Both objects' provenance claims are constitutionally invalid. Neither can establish legitimate origination.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. Both objects enter CoherenceViolation state in the Provenance Register
2. Temporal audit: examine the InstantiationOccasion of both objects to determine which actually originated first
3. The later-originating object's ProvenanceChain is the one with the invalid reference (it cannot be derived from something that didn't exist before it)
4. The invalid ProvenanceLink is superseded (historical record preserved: "this link was circular and invalid")
5. The later object's ProvenanceChain is reconstructed from its actual legitimate antecedents
6. Both objects exit CoherenceViolation; ResolutionRecord documents the audit and correction

### FM-5 — Impossible Timelines

**Scenario:** An object's OriginatingOccasion postdates an Occurrence that the object claims to have caused (effect precedes cause in timestamp).

**Detection:** GCR-4 violation (temporal causality). Detected by Temporal Register evaluation.

**Constitutional consequence:** The causal claim is constitutionally invalid. The Temporal Register enters violation state for the affected relationship.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. The affected URO-Relationship enters CoherenceViolation state in the Temporal Register
2. Investigation: is the timestamp of one of the objects incorrect (e.g., a clock error, a backdating error)?
3. If timestamp error: the OriginationRecord's timestamp cannot be changed (immutable), but a ChangeRecord documents the error and a corrected InstantiationOccasion is recorded in the object's SemanticProfile as a known discrepancy
4. If the causal claim is simply false: the URO-Relationship is superseded (historical record preserved: "this causal claim was found to be temporally impossible") and a correct causal claim or no claim is established
5. The Temporal Register violation is resolved via ResolutionRecord

### FM-6 — Authority Violations

**Scenario:** An action is taken under an AuthorityClaim whose authority chain is broken — either revoked, challenged, or never validly established.

**Detection:** Authority Register coherence violation. Detected by kernel chain Gate 3 (operational) or by Authority Register continuous scan.

**Constitutional consequence:** The action is constitutionally unauthorized. All objects created under this unauthorized action have compromised ExistenceAuthorizations.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. The unauthorized action's CivilizationalAction object enters CoherenceViolation (Authority Register)
2. All objects that cite this action in their ProvenanceChain or ExistenceAuthorization are flagged for review
3. If the authority chain can be repaired (e.g., a missing delegation was simply not recorded): establish the missing AuthorityRelationship with retroactive documentation of the original authorization
4. If the action was genuinely unauthorized: the CivilizationalAction is superseded as {unauthorized-action}; all produced ActionEffects are assessed for reversal (where reversible) or documented as {unauthorized-effects} (where irreversible)
5. AccountabilityRecord is generated for the Actor who took unauthorized action
6. If appropriate, an AuthorityChallenge is created against the Actor's AuthorityClaim

### FM-7 — Epistemic Shortcuts

**Scenario:** An actor creates a KnowledgeClaim that cites only Observations — bypassing EvidenceRecord, Interpretation, and Belief.

**Detection:** Epistemic Register coherence violation. ChainPosition.RequiredPriors check fails.

**Constitutional consequence:** The KnowledgeClaim is constitutionally at the wrong stage. It does not satisfy the epistemic chain requirements.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. KnowledgeClaim enters CoherenceViolation (Epistemic Register)
2. The object is reclassified via Supersession to the highest legitimate stage it actually occupies (likely Belief, if it represents a commitment based on observations without formal evidence assessment)
3. If the missing stages (EvidenceRecord, Interpretation) can be retroactively constructed from existing material, they are created with OriginationRecords noting the retroactive construction
4. Once the chain is complete, a new KnowledgeClaim (requiring validation) is created as the successor
5. ResolutionRecord documents the shortcut and the correction

### FM-8 — Identity Corruption

**Scenario:** An object's StructuralAnchor (SC) has been modified after origination — one of the four OriginalContext fields has changed.

**Detection:** Identity Register coherence violation. SC immutability check fails.

**Constitutional consequence:** This is the most severe failure mode. SC modification is constitutionally impossible by design. Detection means either data corruption or deliberate tampering.

**Resolution protocol:**
1. Object immediately enters {suspended} state — Identity Register violation of severity {constitutively invalid}
2. Emergency audit: compare current SC against the original OriginationRecord and any Fabric Checkpoints preceding the detected modification
3. If data corruption (storage layer error): restore the correct SC from checkpoint or OriginationRecord; document the corruption event in an Incident record
4. If deliberate tampering: treat as a security incident. The tampered object is superseded by a restored version. All objects created after the tampering that depended on the corrupted identity are reviewed. AccountabilityRecord and AuthorityChallenge are created for the actor responsible.
5. Fabric Checkpoint integrity audit is performed to determine scope of corruption

---

### FM-9 — Relationship Corruption

**Scenario:** A URO-Relationship's SourceObject, TargetObject, or RelationshipType has been modified after origination.

**Detection:** Ontological Register or Identity Register coherence violation. Relationship fields are checked against OriginationRecord.

**Constitutional consequence:** Same as identity corruption — field modification after origination is constitutionally impossible.

**Resolution protocol:** Same as FM-8 — audit, restore from checkpoint, investigate for tampering, generate Incident record.

---

### FM-10 — Historical Inconsistency

**Scenario:** A ChangeRecord has been modified after creation, or a HistoricalRecord does not match the state reconstructed from the ChangeRecord chain.

**Detection:** Provenance Register coherence violation. Append-only enforcement check.

**Constitutional consequence:** Historical record tampering undermines the constitutional guarantee of RF-A8 (historical inalienability).

**Resolution protocol:**
1. Provenance Register violation is created
2. The inconsistent records are quarantined (accessible for audit but not operationally referenced)
3. The correct historical state is reconstructed from the most recent valid Fabric Checkpoint
4. The tampered record is documented as {tampered} in a forensic ResolutionRecord
5. Security audit is performed to determine origin and scope of tampering
6. All objects that depended on the tampered historical record are re-evaluated against the correct history
