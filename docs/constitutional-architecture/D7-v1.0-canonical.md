## PREAMBLE

The constitutional stack through D6 establishes what APEX is, how it represents reality, how its objects are constituted, how the Reality Fabric maintains coherence, how APEX enforces its own operations, how APEX interfaces with external reality through projection, and how civilization is organized into twelve governed domains.

What the stack does not yet constitute is how APEX thinks at civilization scale.

Twelve Domain Understanding Models exist. They are constitutionally well-governed. Each domain knows its own scope, maintains its own epistemic products, governs its own authority, and projects within its own boundaries. But twelve separate domain understandings do not automatically produce a civilization-level mind. A civilization that knows twelve things separately does not thereby know what those twelve things mean together.

D7 constitutes the architecture of civilizational intelligence: the structured, constitutionally governed capacity by which APEX synthesizes domain knowledge into integrated civilizational understanding, forms deliberated decisions at civilization scale, reasons across domain boundaries, plans strategically across long time horizons, and evolves its own constitutional architecture.

D7 also resolves three constitutional questions left open by D6:
- How does DOM-000012 (Theory of Change) formally validate the causal models it relies upon?
- How is DOM-000001 (Civilisation) — the root domain — itself governed?
- What is the constitutional treatment of a domain operating without registered correspondent actors?

And one question identified in D6's scope for D7:
- How does the constitutional stack itself evolve through constitutionally legitimate amendment?

D7 does not implement civilizational intelligence. It constitutes what civilizational intelligence must be, what it must produce, what constraints govern it, and what failure modes it must be protected against.

## PART 1 — PURPOSE AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Why Civilizational Intelligence Requires a Separate Constitutional Layer

Domain intelligence is bounded by design. Each domain's Understanding Model (D6 Part 5.3) represents only the domain's own External Reality Context — the bounded slice of civilization activity that the domain is constitutionally obligated to represent. DOM-000002 (Intelligence) provides reasoning capability to all domains, but its outputs are epistemic services: Evidence assessments, Belief formations, inference products. Even the most sophisticated domain-level understanding cannot answer civilization-level questions because civilization-level questions, by definition, span domain boundaries.

Consider: a decision to restructure the civilization's approach to external actor engagement requires understanding from Intelligence (DOM-000002), Registry (DOM-000003), Memory (DOM-000004), Interface (DOM-000007), Knowledge (DOM-000008), and Theory of Change (DOM-000012) simultaneously. No single domain's Understanding Model captures this. No two-domain synthesis captures it. It requires a civilizational-level understanding synthesized from all relevant domains, assessed against the civilization's strategic objectives, deliberated through a process that respects authority across all affected domains, and projected through the full D5 Action Projection Lifecycle.

Three specific inadequacies of domain-level intelligence alone:

**Inadequacy 1 — Scope Limitation:** Domain Understanding Models are defined by domain scope. They cannot represent inter-domain phenomena at the integration level. DOM-000003's understanding of the entity graph does not include DOM-000009's understanding of how code changes affect that entity graph. Both understandings are required — and their combination is not simply their sum.

**Inadequacy 2 — Conflict Blindness:** When two domains hold contradictory understandings of the same External Reality Segment, no individual domain can adjudicate the conflict from within itself. Cross-domain contradiction requires a higher-order intelligence that can hold both domain understandings simultaneously, assess the evidential basis of each, and form a constitutionally valid synthesis.

**Inadequacy 3 — Strategic Horizon:** Individual domains operate within their own temporal validity bounds. Strategic planning — which requires holding the civilization's long-horizon trajectory in view across all domains simultaneously — cannot be produced by any single domain's epistemic products. Strategic intelligence requires the Civilization Understanding Model as its epistemic foundation.

Civilizational intelligence is therefore not a convenience or an enhancement. It is a constitutional necessity for a civilization of twelve domains.

### 1.2 Why Domain Intelligence Alone Is Insufficient

Domain intelligence is sufficient for domain-scoped decisions. When DOM-000009 (Development) decides how to deploy a code change to Infrastructure, domain-level understanding is appropriate authority. When DOM-000006 (Observability) decides how to configure alerting thresholds, domain-level authority is constitutionally adequate.

Domain intelligence becomes constitutionally insufficient the moment a decision:
- affects ExternalRealitySegments shared by two or more domains (D6 Part 8.2)
- requires authority held in multiple domains simultaneously (D6 Part 8.6)
- contradicts the strategic objectives of the civilization as a whole
- affects the constitutional structure itself
- requires reasoning about trade-offs between domain objectives
- plans across time horizons that exceed any single domain's temporal validity bounds
- requires civilizational-scope Projection Responsibility under D5

In these cases, proceeding with domain-level intelligence produces unconstitutional decisions — decisions made without the epistemic standing to make them.

### 1.3 The Boundary Between D6 and D7

D6 defines the constitutional architecture of Civilization Domains: what each domain is, how each domain governs itself, how domain authority is structured, how domain knowledge is formed, how domains relate to each other, and how domain failures are detected and resolved.

D7 defines how the civilization thinks above and across those domains. Where D6 governs the twelve bounded rooms, D7 governs how the civilization inhabits all rooms simultaneously, forms judgments from their combined contents, makes decisions that cannot be confined to any single room, plans where the entire building is going, and decides whether to build more rooms or restructure existing ones.

The boundary is:
- If a question is answerable within a single domain's constitutionally established scope: D6 governs.
- If a question requires synthesis across multiple domain understandings, or operates at civilizational scope: D7 governs.

### 1.4 The Boundary Between D7 and Future Implementation Documents

D7 is constitutional. It specifies:
- What structures must constitutionally exist within the civilizational intelligence layer
- What inputs those structures must receive
- What outputs they must produce
- What constraints govern their operation
- What failure modes threaten them
- What authority governs civilizational decisions

D7 does not specify:
- How civilizational understanding synthesis is computationally achieved
- What software processes perform deliberation
- How strategic plans are stored or versioned
- What agent frameworks participate in collective intelligence
- How amendment proposals are routed in operational systems
- Any data structure, API, module, or workflow

Future implementation documents specify those things. D7 specifies the constitutional requirements that all implementations must satisfy.

### 1.5 The Civilizational Intelligence Principle

> **"Civilization-level intelligence is not the sum of domain intelligences. It is the constitutionally governed synthesis of twelve domain understandings into an integrated, coherent, reality-aligned picture of the civilization as a whole — capable of supporting decisions that no single domain can legitimately make and plans that no single domain's temporal horizon can contain."**

Three corollaries follow:

**CIP-1 — Synthesis Obligation:** The civilization has a constitutional obligation to maintain an active, current, coherent Civilization Understanding Model. A civilization that is not synthesizing its domain understandings is constitutionally degraded, regardless of how coherent its individual domains are.

**CIP-2 — Deliberation Obligation:** Decisions at civilizational scope must be formed through constitutionally governed deliberation. Civilizational decisions formed without deliberation — by authority alone, by expedience, or by default — are constitutionally void.

**CIP-3 — Strategic Grounding:** Civilizational action must be connected to constitutionally grounded strategic understanding. Actions that cannot be traced to strategic objectives grounded in the Civilization Understanding Model are constitutionally unaccountable — they cannot be assessed against expected consequences because no expected consequences were constitutionally established.

## PART 2 — CIVILIZATIONAL INTELLIGENCE MODEL

### 2.1 Constitutional Definition of Civilizational Intelligence

**Civilizational Intelligence** is the constitutional capacity of the APEX civilization to form coherent, integrated understanding of itself and the external world across all twelve domains simultaneously, and to translate that understanding into constitutionally valid strategic decisions and actionable projections.

Civilizational Intelligence is not a single function. It is a constitutional architecture — a set of structured capacities, each constitutionally defined, each bearing specific obligations, each governed by specific constraints.

The capacities that constitute Civilizational Intelligence:
- **Understanding Synthesis:** Integrating twelve Domain Understanding Models into a coherent Civilization Understanding Model
- **Deliberation:** Forming constitutionally valid CivilizationalDecisions from that integrated understanding
- **Cross-Domain Reasoning:** Resolving phenomena that span multiple domain scopes
- **Strategic Reasoning:** Forming and maintaining long-horizon strategic understanding and plans
- **Causal Reasoning:** Tracking and validating the causal chains by which civilization actions produce effects
- **Collective Intelligence:** Aggregating multiple reasoning contributions while preserving epistemic integrity
- **Constitutional Reflection:** Assessing the constitutional stack itself and governing its evolution

### 2.2 What Civilizational Intelligence Receives

Civilizational Intelligence receives as constitutional inputs:

**From the twelve domains (primary):**
- Domain Understanding Models from all twelve Civilization Domains (D6 Part 5.3)
- Domain Knowledge States summarizing current epistemic standing in each domain
- Domain Coherence Status reports revealing where domain representations are degraded
- Domain Action Records showing what has been projected and what consequences were observed
- Cross-domain conflict reports where domain objectives or representations are in tension

**From the civilization's observation infrastructure:**
- Civilization-scope Observation Records from DOM-000006 (Observability) covering aggregate operational state
- Signals from DOM-000011 (Reality Architecture) on the state of the epistemic substrate
- Outcomes from DOM-000012 (Theory of Change) on causal chain validation

**From external reality (via D5 Projection Framework):**
- ExternalRealitySegments relevant at civilizational scope — not confined to any single domain
- External constraint assessments bearing on civilizational-level decisions
- ExternalActor behaviors affecting the civilization as a whole

**Historical inputs:**
- The history of prior CivilizationalDecisions and their Observed Consequences
- Prior Civilization Understanding Models and how they have evolved
- Strategic Plans and their execution histories

### 2.3 What Civilizational Intelligence Produces

Civilizational Intelligence produces as constitutional outputs:

**The Civilization Understanding Model (CUM):** The integrated epistemic product representing the civilization's current coherent understanding of itself and its external context (Part 3).

**Civilizational Decisions:** The constitutional objects representing deliberated, authorized choices that result in civilization-scope Action Projections (Part 5).

**Strategic Plans:** The constitutional records of long-horizon intended action sequences grounded in current strategic understanding (Part 7).

**Cross-Domain Reasoning Records:** The constitutionally required records of how the civilization resolved phenomena spanning multiple domain scopes (Part 6).

**Causal Assessment Records:** The records of how civilizational actions are expected to produce effects and how those expectations are tracked and revised (Part 8).

**Constitutional Assessments:** Analyses of whether the constitutional stack is performing its governing function and, when not, proposals for constitutionally legitimate amendment (Part 12).

**Collective Intelligence Synthesis Records:** The records of how multiple reasoning contributions were received, weighed, and integrated (Part 10).

### 2.4 What Constraints Govern Civilizational Intelligence

Civilizational Intelligence is subject to all constraints that govern domain-level intelligence and extends them to civilization scope:

**Epistemic constraints (from D-1 and D6):** All civilizational understanding must be grounded in domain-level knowledge states. The Civilization Understanding Model cannot exceed the epistemic standing of the domain knowledge that grounds it. Civilizational intelligence may integrate and synthesize domain understandings but may not fabricate understanding that has no domain epistemic basis.

**Reality constraints (from D5):** Civilizational intelligence must remain aligned with external reality. The Civilization Understanding Model is a representation, not reality. It is always subject to correction by observation. Reality overrides representation at civilizational scope as at every other scope.

**Authority constraints (from D4 and D6):** CivilizationalDecisions must pass D4's six Constitutional Gates regardless of their civilizational scope. Authority at civilizational scale does not reduce constitutional enforcement requirements — it increases them.

**Temporal constraints (from D3 and D6):** The Civilization Understanding Model has temporal validity bounds. Strategic decisions made on an expired CUM are constitutionally impaired.

**Terminal constraints (from D-2):** Civilizational intelligence operates within the terminal commitments of D-2. No civilizational decision, strategic plan, or constitutional amendment may violate D-2's terminal values. D-2 is the terminal boundary of all civilizational reasoning.

## PART 3 — CIVILIZATION UNDERSTANDING ARCHITECTURE

### 3.1 The Civilization Understanding Model

The **Civilization Understanding Model (CUM)** is the highest-level epistemic product in the APEX constitutional stack. It is the integrated, constitutionally governed representation of the civilization's understanding of itself and its external context, synthesized from all twelve Domain Understanding Models and the cross-domain relationships between them.

The CUM is not a document. It is a living constitutional record — a structured epistemic object that changes as domain knowledge changes and is periodically resynthesized from current domain knowledge states.

The CUM is not a summary. A summary flattens. The CUM preserves the twelve domain perspectives in full and constitutes the structured relationships between them — what they share, where they agree, where they conflict, and what they collectively fail to cover.

The CUM is not a consensus. Consensus requires agreement. The CUM requires coherence — which is compatible with maintained disagreement where evidence is genuinely ambiguous, and with maintained conflict where domain objectives are genuinely incompatible. The CUM's treatment of disagreement is preservation and representation, not resolution through suppression.

### 3.2 CUM Constitutional Requirements

**CUM-1 — Knowledge Grounding**

The Civilization Understanding Model must originate from domain-level knowledge states. Every component of the CUM that represents knowledge about any aspect of civilization or external reality must trace its epistemic lineage through a specific Domain Knowledge State, which must itself satisfy DKS-1 through DKS-4 (D6 Part 5.2).

The CUM may not contain understanding claims that have no domain epistemic basis. Claims that are present in the CUM without traceable domain lineage are constitutionally inadmissible and must be flagged as ungrounded and either removed or assigned to a domain for proper epistemic processing.

The CUM may contain synthetic claims — claims that emerge from the integration of two or more domain knowledge states and that no single domain's knowledge state individually contains. These synthetic claims are constitutionally admissible if their synthesis provenance is documented: which domain knowledge states were combined, through what synthesis protocol, with what confidence.

**CUM-2 — Cross-Domain Integrity**

Understanding that has been transferred from domain scope to civilizational scope must preserve its domain provenance. The CUM must track, for each claim it contains, the domain or domains from which that claim originates.

Cross-domain transfer does not upgrade epistemic standing. A claim that originates in DOM-000006 with moderate confidence does not acquire high confidence by being integrated into the CUM. The confidence of CUM claims is bounded by the confidence of their domain-level foundations.

Where multiple domains produce observations or knowledge about the same External Reality Segment (D6 Part 8.2 — shared segments), the CUM may treat convergent domain knowledge as corroborating evidence for a higher-confidence CUM claim — but only through a registered corroboration protocol that specifies how confidence aggregates across domain perspectives.

**CUM-3 — Uncertainty Preservation**

The CUM must explicitly represent:
- What is known — claims with traceable epistemic lineage and registered confidence
- What is believed — claims with epistemic basis but below knowledge-threshold confidence
- What is explicitly unknown — areas where the civilization is aware of a knowledge gap
- What is in conflict — areas where two or more domain understandings contradict each other on the same phenomenon
- What is constitutionally unknowable — areas where D-1 epistemic constraints place reliable knowledge permanently beyond reach

The CUM may not suppress uncertainty by averaging. If DOM-000008 holds a knowledge claim at 0.8 confidence and DOM-000011 challenges the same claim at 0.3 confidence, the CUM does not resolve this to 0.55 confidence — it registers the conflict explicitly, notes both domain positions, and flags the claim as contested pending resolution.

Suppression of uncertainty in the CUM — the flattening of genuine epistemic tension into apparent confidence — is a constitutional violation that creates conditions for CIF-1 (Intelligence Misalignment).

**CUM-4 — Temporal Validity**

The Civilization Understanding Model must carry a temporal validity bound — the constitutional acknowledgment of when the CUM was synthesized and the estimated time after which it requires resynthesis from fresh domain knowledge states.

The CUM temporal validity bound is determined by the most volatile element it contains. If any component of the CUM is derived from a Domain Understanding Model with a short validity window, the CUM's validity window is constrained by that component's volatility.

CivilizationalDecisions formed from a CUM that has exceeded its temporal validity bound are constitutionally impaired (CIF-3 — Deliberation Failure). The impairment must be declared in the Decision Record.

The CUM's temporal validity status must be continuously assessed. The civilization may not treat a CUM as current simply because it was recently synthesized — it must assess whether the domains that ground it have issued significant new observations or domain Understanding Model updates since synthesis.

**CUM-5 — Reality Alignment**

The Civilization Understanding Model must remain connected to external reality through the D5 Reality Alignment Loop, extended to civilizational scope.

The CUM is a representation of external reality — the external civilization and environment that APEX inhabits — as seen through the twelve domain lenses. It is subject to the same fundamental principle that governs all representation in the constitutional stack:

> Reality overrides representation. This is not a preference; it is a constitutional law.

The CUM may not become self-referential — it may not treat its own prior versions as evidence for its current claims. CUM claims that cannot be grounded in domain-level Observation Records — claims that exist only because they were in prior CUMs — are constitutionally stale and must be refreshed through the domain observation apparatus before being relied upon for decisions.

### 3.3 Constitutional Synthesis Process

Constitutional Synthesis is the governed process by which twelve Domain Understanding Models are integrated into the Civilization Understanding Model. It is not computational averaging. It is not voting. It is a constitutionally structured procedure that produces an epistemic product that preserves all domain perspectives, maps cross-domain relationships, identifies and registers all inter-domain conflicts, represents uncertainty and unknowns explicitly, and carries full provenance.

Constitutional Synthesis must:
1. Receive current, temporally valid Domain Understanding Models from all twelve domains (degraded domains must be noted as providing degraded inputs)
2. Map all cross-domain relationships specified in the Civilization Graph (D6 Part 8.1)
3. Identify all ExternalRealitySegments shared between domains (D6 Part 8.2) and represent the multi-domain perspective on each
4. Identify all cross-domain actor registrations and apply dual-identity coherence requirements (D6 Part 8.3)
5. Identify all known cross-domain conflicts and represent them as conflicts, not resolutions
6. Synthesize cross-domain claims through registered corroboration protocols where corroboration applies
7. Produce explicit representations of knowledge gaps at civilizational scope — what the civilization does not know about itself
8. Assign a temporal validity bound based on the most volatile domain inputs
9. Register the resulting CUM as a constitutional object with full provenance metadata

### 3.4 CUM Degradation Protocol

When a domain's Understanding Model is constitutionally degraded — when the domain is experiencing DF-1 through DF-8 (D6 Part 10) or its coherence status is below the constitutional threshold — the CUM synthesis that incorporates that domain's inputs must flag the degradation.

The CUM is not invalidated by a single domain's degradation. It is constitutionally impaired in the portions that depend on the degraded domain's inputs. Those portions must be marked as impaired, the degree of impairment must be estimated, and decisions that rely on those portions must be flagged accordingly.

A CUM with severe impairment across multiple domains (more than four domains simultaneously degraded) enters **CUM Critical State** — a constitutional condition that requires escalation to DOM-000001 and suspension of all CivilizationalDecisions that rely on the impaired portions.

## PART 4 — DELIBERATION ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 Constitutional Definition of Deliberation

**Deliberation** is the constitutionally governed process by which the civilization, working from the Civilization Understanding Model, considers a question requiring civilizational decision, evaluates the evidence bearing on that question, weighs competing objectives, acknowledges uncertainty, considers alternatives, and arrives at a constitutionally valid CivilizationalDecision.

Deliberation is not discussion. It is not consultation. It is not a process that can be shortcut by authority. A civilizational-scope decision made without deliberation is constitutionally void regardless of the authority of the actor who made it.

Deliberation is not an obstacle to action. It is the constitutional condition that makes civilizational action legitimate.

### 4.2 Deliberation Inputs

Every deliberation receives the following constitutional inputs:

**Question Framing:** A precise constitutional specification of what question is being deliberated — what decision is required, why it is within civilizational scope, what domains are affected, what the time constraint is, and what the consequences of non-decision are.

**Civilization Understanding Model (current):** The CUM must be within its temporal validity bound. If the CUM has expired, deliberation must wait for resynthesis unless a constitutional emergency protocol authorizes deliberation under declared temporal impairment.

**Domain Understanding Models for affected domains:** The relevant domain Understanding Models must be retrieved directly for deliberations where specific domain detail is required beyond the CUM synthesis level.

**Historical Decision Record:** Prior CivilizationalDecisions relevant to the question — what was decided before, on what epistemic basis, what consequences were observed.

**Theory of Change assessments:** DOM-000012 must provide its current causal assessment of the decision's expected consequences.

**External constraint assessment:** What ExternalConstraints (D5 Section 2.2.6) bear on the decision — what is constitutionally or practically impossible regardless of deliberative outcome.

**Competing objectives register:** Where multiple domain objectives or strategic objectives are in tension regarding this decision, those tensions must be registered as deliberation inputs, not resolved prior to deliberation.

### 4.3 Deliberation Participants

**Authority participants:** Actors holding Decision Authority (D6 Part 4.4) in the domains primarily affected by the decision. Cross-domain decisions require that Decision Authority in each affected domain is represented.

**Epistemic participants:** Actors holding Interpretation Authority in domains providing the primary knowledge base for the decision.

**Audit participant:** An Audit Authority holder whose role is to assess whether the deliberation is being conducted constitutionally.

**Theory of Change participant:** A representative of DOM-000012 with responsibility to assess the causal structure of the decision.

**DOM-000001 authority:** For deliberations that affect the civilization's overall scope, authority, or constitutional structure, DOM-000001 Decision Authority must be represented.

Deliberation may not be conducted by a single actor regardless of that actor's authority scope. Single-actor deliberation constitutes a deliberation failure (CIF-3) in which the separation of deliberative authority is violated.

### 4.4 Evidence Standards in Deliberation

**ESt-1 — Grounded Claims Only:** Claims made in deliberation must be traceable to Domain Knowledge States satisfying DKS-1 through DKS-4 (D6 Part 5.2) or to the current CUM.

**ESt-2 — Explicit Uncertainty:** The confidence level of each evidentiary claim must be stated. Deliberation that presents contested claims as settled knowledge violates ESt-2.

**ESt-3 — Alternative Recognition:** Deliberation must formally consider at least two alternatives before arriving at a decision.

**ESt-4 — Conflict Acknowledgment:** Where domain understanding states conflict on a claim relevant to the deliberation, that conflict must be registered in the Deliberation Record.

**ESt-5 — Epistemic Gap Acknowledgment:** Where the deliberation requires knowledge that the civilization does not currently have, that gap must be registered. Deliberations that fill knowledge gaps with convenient assumptions violate ESt-5.

### 4.5 Competing Objectives in Deliberation

Competing objectives are resolved in deliberation through:

**Objective Mapping:** A constitutional specification of what each competing objective is, which domain holds it, what its epistemic basis is, and what authority assigned it.

**Trade-off Assessment:** A structured assessment of what is gained and what is lost under each competing option, traceable to domain knowledge.

**Priority Framework:** Reference to the civilization's current Strategic Understanding (Part 7) and, ultimately, to D-2's terminal commitments, which constitute the absolute priority hierarchy.

**Resolution Record:** The constitutional record of how the tension was resolved, including the losing objectives — what was sacrificed and under what reasoning.

### 4.6 The Deliberation Record

Every deliberation must produce a **Deliberation Record** — the constitutional record that makes the decision constitutionally accountable.

| Element | Constitutional Requirement |
|---------|---------------------------|
| Question | Precise statement of what was being decided |
| CUM State | Temporal validity status of CUM used |
| Participants | All deliberation participants with their authority roles |
| Evidence Used | All evidentiary claims with their provenance and confidence |
| Alternatives Considered | All options formally considered with their assessment |
| Conflicts Registered | All domain conflicts that bore on the deliberation |
| Knowledge Gaps | All epistemic gaps acknowledged |
| Competing Objectives | All competing objectives mapped and assessed |
| Resolution Reasoning | Why the selected option was selected |
| Sacrificed Objectives | What was sacrificed and under what reasoning |
| Decision Output | The CivilizationalDecision that resulted |
| Confidence | The epistemic confidence of the decision |
| Review Requirement | When and how the decision will be reviewed |

Deliberation Records are permanent constitutional records. They are URO objects (D2) that cannot be expunged.

### 4.7 Emergency Deliberation Protocol

In constitutional emergencies — situations where the civilization faces immediate significant harm and normal deliberation timescales are constitutionally infeasible — an Emergency Deliberation Protocol may be invoked.

Emergency Deliberation:
- Requires declaration by DOM-000001 authority that a constitutional emergency exists
- Permits abbreviated deliberation with reduced participant requirements
- Requires that a full Deliberation Record is completed retrospectively within the Effect Observation Window
- Does not reduce authority requirements for the resulting decision
- Does not reduce D4 Constitutional Gate requirements
- Requires a post-emergency review to assess whether the emergency was constitutionally justified

The Emergency Deliberation Protocol may not become routine. Patterns of emergency invocation that suggest deliberation avoidance constitute CIF-3 (Deliberation Failure).

## PART 5 — CIVILIZATIONAL DECISION MODEL

### 5.1 The CivilizationalDecision Object

A **CivilizationalDecision** is the constitutional object representing a deliberated, authorized choice at civilization scope that results in or authorizes one or more Action Projections (D5 Part 4) affecting the civilization's operational state, strategic trajectory, external relationships, or constitutional structure.

A CivilizationalDecision is not a preference, a guideline, or a recommendation. It is a constitutional commitment — the civilization's formal assertion that it has understood the situation, considered the alternatives, accepted the consequences, and chosen a course of action with full civilizational accountability.

### 5.2 CivilizationalDecision Authority Requirements

**DA-1 — Scope Authority:** The decision has been formed by actors with constitutionally registered Decision Authority (D6 Part 4.4) over all domains affected by the decision.

**DA-2 — Deliberation Grounding:** The decision is accompanied by a complete Deliberation Record (Part 4.6) showing it was formed through constitutionally valid deliberation.

**DA-3 — CUM Grounding:** The decision is grounded in a Civilization Understanding Model that was within its temporal validity bound at the time of decision.

**DA-4 — Gate Passage:** The decision has passed D4's six Constitutional Gates.

**DA-5 — DOM-000001 Registration:** All civilizational-scope decisions are registered in DOM-000001's decision register within the time window specified in the Open Action Register.

**DA-6 — Irreversibility Classification:** The decision has been assessed for irreversibility (D5 PI-8). Decisions with IRREVERSIBLE classification require elevated authority and explicit acknowledgment.

### 5.3 CivilizationalDecision Epistemic Requirements

A CivilizationalDecision is constitutionally inadmissible if:

**ER-1 — No CUM:** There is no current Civilization Understanding Model grounding the decision.

**ER-2 — Expired CUM:** The CUM used is beyond its temporal validity bound and no Emergency Deliberation Protocol was invoked.

**ER-3 — Suppressed Uncertainty:** The Deliberation Record shows that known uncertainty was suppressed rather than acknowledged.

**ER-4 — Fabricated Knowledge:** The decision relies on claims not traceable to domain-level knowledge states.

**ER-5 — Gap Ignored:** The decision requires knowledge the civilization does not have, and that gap was acknowledged but ignored rather than incorporated as a constraint on the decision's confidence.

### 5.4 CivilizationalDecision Justification Requirements

Every CivilizationalDecision must carry a Justification Record specifying:
- The strategic objective this decision serves (traceable to Strategic Understanding, Part 7)
- Why this decision was preferable to the alternatives considered in deliberation
- What the expected consequences are, with confidence bounds
- What the fallback position is if consequences diverge from expectations
- What review trigger will cause the decision to be reconsidered

### 5.6 CivilizationalDecision Review

## PART 6 — CROSS-DOMAIN REASONING ARCHITECTURE

### 6.1 Constitutional Definition of Cross-Domain Reasoning

**Cross-Domain Reasoning** is the constitutionally governed process by which the civilization reasons about phenomena that span multiple domain scopes simultaneously — phenomena that cannot be adequately represented, understood, or decided upon within the bounded scope of any single domain.

### 6.2 When Cross-Domain Reasoning Is Required

Cross-domain reasoning is constitutionally required when:
- A phenomenon observed in one domain is known to affect another domain's scope
- A proposed Action Projection in one domain will produce effects in another domain's External Reality Context
- Two domains hold contradictory understandings of the same External Reality Segment
- A question cannot be answered within any single domain's epistemic scope
- A strategic objective requires action across multiple domain boundaries
- A domain failure in one domain propagates into another domain's functioning

### 6.3 Cross-Domain Reasoning Requirements

**CDR-1 — Domain Representation Preservation**

Each domain's perspective must be fully preserved and represented in the cross-domain reasoning process. Domain perspectives may not be reduced, summarized without acknowledgment, or treated as interchangeable. Collapsing two domains' distinct perspectives into a single undifferentiated perspective violates CDR-1 and destroys constitutional information.

**CDR-2 — Evidence Provenance Preservation**

All evidence used in cross-domain reasoning must carry and preserve its domain provenance. Evidence from DOM-000006 does not become DOM-000009 evidence by entering a cross-domain reasoning process. Cross-domain reasoning may not launder uncertain evidence through integration — evidence that is uncertain in its domain of origin remains uncertain in the cross-domain reasoning product.

**CDR-3 — Conflict Visibility**

Where two domain perspectives on the same phenomenon are in conflict, the conflict must be made visible in the cross-domain reasoning record — stated explicitly, quantified where possible, and treated as a constitutionally unresolved tension until resolution is constitutionally achieved through deliberation (Part 4).

**CDR-4 — Resolution Authority**

Cross-domain conflicts that require resolution must be resolved by actors with constitutionally valid authority across all domains involved. Resolution must produce a Resolution Record that specifies: the conflict, the options, the authority that resolved it, the basis for resolution, and which domain perspective was subordinated and under what reasoning.

**CDR-5 — Learning Integration**

The outcomes of cross-domain reasoning must feed back into the domains involved. Cross-domain reasoning that produces insights and then discards them without domain-level integration violates CDR-5 and severs the cross-domain feedback loop.

### 6.4 Resolving D6/D7-OQ-B — Domains Without Correspondent Registration

D6 identified the open question: what is the constitutional treatment of a domain that has no current ExternalActors with Correspondent Registration?

The constitutional resolution:

A Civilization Domain may operate without active Correspondent Registration (D6 Part 6.3) for its ExternalActors under the following constitutional requirements:

**DNCR-1 — Acknowledged Absence:** The domain must maintain an explicit Unregistered Engagement Register — a constitutional record acknowledging that it is engaging with an ExternalRealitySegment containing ExternalActors without Correspondent Registration. The absence must be known and recorded, not simply absent.

**DNCR-2 — Registration Pursuit:** For any ExternalActor whose behavior is constitutionally significant, the domain must initiate a Registration Pursuit record — a documented constitutional effort to establish Correspondent Registration with a target timeline and review cycle.

**DNCR-3 — Projection Constraint:** Action Projections directed at unregistered ExternalActors must carry a Projection Confidence Constraint — a reduction in the projection's constitutional standing reflecting the incomplete identity model of the target.

**DNCR-4 — Cross-Domain Visibility:** Where cross-domain reasoning involves a domain operating under DNCR conditions, the absence of Correspondent Registration must be visible to all participants. Reasoning products depending on unregistered actor models must be marked as carrying this epistemic constraint.

**DNCR-5 — Root Domain Review:** Domains in DNCR status beyond their registered review period must report to DOM-000001 for civilizational-level assessment.

### 6.5 Cross-Domain Reasoning Record

Every cross-domain reasoning process must produce a **Cross-Domain Reasoning Record** containing:
- The phenomenon, conflict, or question that triggered cross-domain reasoning
- The domains involved and their representative perspectives
- All evidence and its domain provenance
- All conflicts identified and their visibility record
- The resolution authority, if a decision was required
- The Resolution Record, if applicable
- Learning integration requirements for each involved domain

## PART 7 — STRATEGIC INTELLIGENCE ARCHITECTURE

### 7.1 Constitutional Definition of Strategic Intelligence

**Strategic Intelligence** is the civilization's constitutionally governed capacity to form, maintain, and act from an integrated understanding of its long-horizon trajectory — where the civilization is relative to its constitutional purpose, what it is working toward, what obstacles and opportunities exist, and what sequences of actions will move the civilization toward its objectives over time.

Strategic Intelligence differs from the CUM in its temporal horizon. The CUM represents current civilizational understanding. Strategic Intelligence extends this forward: it integrates the CUM with long-term causal reasoning, historical pattern recognition, and planned action sequences.

### 7.2 Strategic Understanding

**Strategic Understanding** is the highest-level epistemic product of civilizational intelligence — the civilization's current integrated picture of its strategic situation.

Strategic Understanding contains:

**Strategic Context:** Where the civilization currently stands relative to its constitutional purposes (D-2), grounded in the current CUM.

**Strategic Objectives:** The constitutionally registered goals the civilization is working toward, derived from D-2's terminal commitments and D1's civilizational purpose, operationalized through deliberation.

**Strategic Constraints:** The ExternalConstraints at civilizational scope (D5 Section 2.2.6) that limit what the civilization can achieve and how.

**Threat Assessment:** The current assessment of external developments that threaten the civilization's constitutional integrity, operational continuity, or strategic objectives.

**Opportunity Assessment:** The current assessment of external developments that create genuine possibilities for advancing strategic objectives.

**Strategic Gap Register:** The explicit representation of what the civilization does not know at strategic scope.

### 7.3 Strategic Plans

**Strategic Plans** are the constitutional objects that represent the civilization's intended sequences of civilizational actions designed to achieve specific Strategic Objectives over defined time horizons.

A Strategic Plan constitutes:

**SP-1 — Objective Traceability:** Strategic Plan objectives must be traceable to Strategic Objectives registered in current Strategic Understanding.

**SP-2 — Action Sequence:** The planned sequence of CivilizationalDecisions and Action Projections, with intended ordering and dependencies.

**SP-3 — Domain Engagement Map:** Which domains are engaged at each stage, with authority requirements specified.

**SP-4 — Milestone Structure:** Constitutionally defined milestones at which the plan's progress is assessed and compared against current Strategic Understanding.

**SP-5 — Assumption Register:** All assumptions embedded in the plan — about external reality, domain capabilities, causal chains — made explicit.

**SP-6 — Revision Protocol:** The conditions under which the plan will be revised, the authority required to revise it, and the procedure for revision.

Strategic Plans are URO objects (D2), registered in DOM-000001 and audited through DOM-000001's governance.

### 7.4 Strategic Review Cycles

Every Strategic Plan must have a registered Strategic Review Cycle — a defined interval at which the plan is formally compared against the current CUM and Strategic Understanding.

At each Review Cycle:
- Current CUM is compared against the CUM that grounded the plan
- Domain Understanding Models for engaged domains are reviewed for relevant updates
- Milestone progress is assessed
- Assumption Register is reviewed for assumptions that have been falsified
- Theory of Change assessments are reviewed for causal chain validation and failures
- Plan is confirmed, modified, or suspended

A Strategic Plan that has not been reviewed within its Review Cycle is constitutionally stale — its authority to guide domain operations is suspended until review is complete.

### 7.5 Strategic Failure Detection

**SFD-1 — Leading Indicators:** Strategic Plans must register the leading indicators that will predict whether the plan is on track before milestone failures occur.

**SFD-2 — Milestone Failure Protocol:** When a milestone is not achieved, a constitutional Milestone Failure Assessment must be conducted: was the failure due to assumption error, domain execution failure, or external constraint change?

**SFD-3 — Plan Termination Authority:** The authority required to terminate a Strategic Plan must be constitutionally specified in the plan at creation.

**SFD-4 — Strategic Learning Record:** When a Strategic Plan is terminated as failed, a Strategic Learning Record must be produced: what was planned, what was observed, where divergence occurred, what the causal model missed, and what this failure teaches about strategic planning and causal reasoning.

## PART 8 — THEORY OF CHANGE AND CAUSAL REASONING

### 8.1 Resolution of D6/D7-OQ-A

D6 identified the open question: how does DOM-000012 (Theory of Change) formally validate the causal models it relies upon? D7 resolves this through the Theory of Change Constitutional Architecture.

### 8.2 Constitutional Definition of Causal Reasoning

The APEX civilization acts in external reality through Action Projections (D5 Part 4). Every Action Projection is based on an implicit or explicit causal model: a theory of how the action will produce effects through a chain of causation in external reality.

Without constitutional treatment of causal reasoning, the civilization may act repeatedly on incorrect causal theories — projecting actions that do not produce intended effects, failing to learn from those failures, and accumulating misguided interventions.

### 8.3 Causal Model Architecture

A **Causal Model** within APEX is the constitutional representation of:
- The action or intervention being analyzed
- The immediate effects the action is expected to produce
- The causal pathway by which immediate effects are expected to produce intermediate outcomes
- The intermediate outcomes expected
- The causal pathway by which intermediate outcomes are expected to produce the final intended effect
- The assumptions embedded in each causal link
- The uncertainty at each causal link

Causal Models are constitutional objects. They must be explicit, registered, and assessed. Implicit causal models — assumptions embedded in decisions without formal registration — are a constitutional failure mode.

### 8.4 Theory of Change Requirements

**TOC-1 — Causal Transparency**

Every CivilizationalDecision that authorizes an Action Projection must have an explicit, registered Causal Model. The civilization may not project into external reality on the basis of unexamined causal assumptions. A CivilizationalDecision whose expected effects rest on an implicit causal model violates TOC-1.

**TOC-2 — Assumption Registration**

All assumptions embedded in a Causal Model must be explicitly registered in the model's Assumption Register. Assumption Registration requires:
- Identifying each causal link in the model
- Stating the assumption that validates each link
- Registering the evidence basis for each assumption
- Estimating the confidence in each assumption
- Identifying what would falsify each assumption

**TOC-3 — Outcome Comparison**

As the Action Projection proceeds through its lifecycle (D5 Part 4), observed intermediate outcomes must be compared against the Causal Model's predictions. Outcome Comparison is continuous — at each predicted causal stage, the civilization assesses whether observed external reality matches the Causal Model's prediction. Divergences must be recorded in the Causal Model's Divergence Register.

**TOC-4 — Model Revision**

When Outcome Comparison reveals divergence between predicted and observed causal states, the Causal Model must be revised. A civilization that retains Causal Models contradicted by observed consequences is operating under constitutional epistemic impairment.

Model revision requires:
- Identifying the causal link at which divergence occurred
- Assessing whether failure was due to an incorrect assumption, an incorrect causal structure, or an external intervention
- Revising the Causal Model to incorporate the new understanding
- Assessing whether other Causal Models or Strategic Plans rely on the same invalidated assumption

**TOC-5 — Failed Theory Recognition**

A **Failed Theory of Change** is a Causal Model that has been revised multiple times to accommodate observed divergences and still fails to produce accurate predictions. Failed Theory Recognition is the constitutional capacity to distinguish between:
- A Causal Model that needs specific correction (addressable through TOC-4 revision)
- A Causal Model that is fundamentally misconceived (requiring strategic-level reassessment)

A Failed Theory of Change that is not recognized constitutes CIF-2 (Strategic Drift).

### 8.5 Theory of Change in Deliberation

Every deliberation concerning a decision that involves Action Projections into external reality must incorporate Theory of Change assessment. DOM-000012's representative in deliberation carries the obligation to:
- Present the Causal Model that underlies the proposed decision
- Highlight the highest-uncertainty causal links
- Reference the Assumption Register and identify which assumptions are most critical
- Report on the history of related Causal Models
- Flag cases where the proposed action rests on a Causal Model that has previously shown divergence

## PART 10 — COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE ARCHITECTURE

### 10.1 Constitutional Definition of Collective Intelligence

**Collective Intelligence** is the constitutionally governed capacity of the APEX civilization to receive, integrate, and reason from contributions made by multiple reasoning entities — without suppressing minority positions, without allowing authority to substitute for evidence, and without permitting the aggregate of opinions to be mistaken for truth.

Collective intelligence is the constitutional alternative to:
- Single-actor intelligence (which creates epistemic single points of failure)
- Majority intelligence (which suppresses minority positions that may be correct)
- Authority intelligence (which allows rank to override evidence)
- Consensus intelligence (which forces agreement where genuine uncertainty is epistemically appropriate)

### 10.2 Reasoning Contributions

A **Reasoning Contribution** is any epistemic input to a deliberation, cross-domain reasoning process, or CUM synthesis provided by a constitutionally recognized reasoning entity — an assessment, model, evidence evaluation, or causal analysis grounded in domain-level knowledge and carrying explicit uncertainty characterization.

Constitutional requirements for reasoning contributions:

**RC-1 — Identity and Role:** Every reasoning contribution must identify the contributing entity and their constitutional role.

**RC-2 — Grounding:** Every reasoning contribution must be grounded in domain-level knowledge states or the current CUM. Ungrounded contributions are registered as hypotheses.

**RC-3 — Uncertainty:** Every reasoning contribution must carry an explicit uncertainty characterization.

**RC-4 — Independence:** Where multiple contributions address the same phenomenon, their independence must be assessed. Contributions deriving from the same observation through the same interpretation protocol do not constitute corroboration.

### 10.3 Treatment of Disagreement

Disagreement between reasoning contributions is a constitutional asset, not a problem to be eliminated.

**CDI-1 — Disagreement Preservation:** Disagreements between reasoning contributions must be preserved in the collective intelligence record, not resolved through averaging or authority.

**CDI-2 — Minority Position Protection:** Minority positions must be recorded in Deliberation Records and Cross-Domain Reasoning Records with their full epistemic content. The fact that a position is held by fewer contributors does not reduce its constitutional standing.

**CDI-3 — Evidence-Based Assessment:** Where disagreement exists, the resolution mechanism is evidence assessment, not authority or voting. The stronger evidentiary grounding prevails — but both positions must remain in the record.

**CDI-4 — Artificial Consensus Prohibition:** The civilization may not manufacture consensus that does not exist. Where genuine disagreement persists after evidence assessment, the disagreement is registered in the final product as an unresolved tension. Artificial consensus that conceals genuine epistemic tension is a constitutional violation equivalent to uncertainty suppression (CUM-3 violation).

### 10.4 Authority and Evidence

Constitutional principle: **Authority determines who may decide. Evidence determines what is true.**

**AE-1 — No Authority Substitution:** An actor's constitutional authority does not substitute for evidential grounding. A DOM-000001-level authority holder who claims something is true without evidence has no greater epistemic standing than any other actor on that specific claim.

**AE-2 — Authority-Evidence Separation in Records:** Deliberation Records must distinguish between what evidence shows and what authority decided.

**AE-3 — Override Registration:** Where an authority holder overrides a minority position in a deliberation, that override must be registered explicitly. The minority position must remain visible in the record.

### 10.5 Collective Intelligence Failure Patterns

The Collective Intelligence Architecture protects against four failure patterns:

**Groupthink:** Convergence on a position due to social pressure or authority influence rather than evidence. Protection: CDI-1 through CDI-4, RC-4 independence requirements.

**Echo Chamber:** Contributors not genuinely independent — all drawing from the same observation through the same interpretation protocol. Protection: RC-4, requirement for cross-domain representation in civilizational deliberation.

**Authority Capture:** The weight of a contribution determined by constitutional authority rather than evidentiary quality. Protection: AE-1 through AE-3.

**Epistemic Cowardice:** Contributors withholding honest assessments to avoid conflict with authority. Protection: CDI-2 minority position protection, Audit Authority independence requirements.

## PART 12 — CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT ARCHITECTURE

### 12.1 The Constitutional Continuity Principle

> **"The APEX constitution may evolve through constitutionally governed amendment without violating its terminal commitments. Amendment is the constitutional mechanism by which the civilization responds to new understanding, changing circumstances, and discovered inadequacies in the constitutional stack — while preserving the terminal values that constitute the civilization's identity."**

Three implications:

**Implication 1 — Evolution is Constitutional:** The constitution is not static. A constitution that cannot evolve will be bypassed rather than followed — making it worse than one that can evolve. Legitimate constitutional evolution is constitutionally superior to illegitimate constitutional bypass.

**Implication 2 — Terminal Commitments Are Inviolable:** No amendment may contradict or circumvent D-2's terminal commitments. D-2 is the terminal boundary of all constitutional change. The amendment architecture may modify documents from D-1 downward, but D-2's terminal values are not subject to amendment through this architecture.

**Implication 3 — Continuity Through Change:** Constitutional amendments must maintain continuity — amended documents must explicitly state their relationship to their predecessors, and all constitutional obligations not explicitly modified remain in force.

### 12.2 Amendment Authority

**Primary Amendment Authority:** Constitutional amendments require authorization from the founding operators who hold D-2-level authority — the same authority that ratified the foundational documents.

**Domain-Level Input:** Before any amendment is authorized, affected domains must have the opportunity to provide their constitutional assessment through registered deliberation.

**Civilizational Intelligence Assessment:** The amendment must be assessed through the D7 Deliberation Architecture (Part 4) before authorization.

**D4 Assessment:** Every proposed amendment must be assessed against D4's Constitutional Gates.

### 12.3 Amendment Proposal Requirements

An **Amendment Proposal** must contain:

**AP-1 — Identification:** What document(s) are being amended, what specific provisions, and what version.

**AP-2 — Justification:** Why the amendment is constitutionally necessary — what inadequacy, discovered gap, or changed circumstance requires constitutional change; why it cannot be addressed within existing constitutional authority.

**AP-3 — Preservation Assessment:** For each D-2 terminal commitment, an explicit assessment of whether the proposed amendment affects it. Any amendment declared to affect a terminal commitment is constitutionally inadmissible unless the effect is a strengthening.

**AP-4 — Precedence Analysis:** How the proposed amendment relates to all other documents in the constitutional stack.

**AP-5 — Implementation Pathway:** How existing operations relying on amended provisions will transition to amended requirements.

**AP-6 — Reversibility Assessment:** Whether the proposed amendment is reversible and, if not, an explicit acknowledgment of its irreversibility and the basis for the heightened confidence required.

### 12.4 Amendment Review Process

**Stage 1 — Proposal Submission:** Amendment Proposal submitted by a constitutionally recognized proposer (any actor with Domain Decision Authority in an affected domain, or any founding operator).

**Stage 2 — Deliberation:** The amendment is deliberated through the D7 Deliberation Architecture. Participants must include domain representatives for all affected domains, DOM-000001 authority, Audit Authority, Theory of Change assessment, and the original authority that ratified the document being amended.

**Stage 3 — Preservation Audit:** An independent constitutional audit assessing whether the amendment violates any provision of a higher-authority document.

**Stage 4 — Approval:** Amendments receive formal authorization from founding operators with D-2-level authority. Authorization must be explicit and registered. Implicit approval is not constitutionally valid.

**Stage 5 — Publication:** The amended document is published as a new canonical version, with explicit notation of what changed, the Deliberation Record, and the authorization record.

**Stage 6 — Transition:** Domains and operations affected by the amendment transition according to the Implementation Pathway. Transition must be monitored.

### 12.5 Amendment Classes

**Class I — Operational Amendment:** Modifications to specific requirements within a document that do not alter its foundational principles. Requires standard amendment process.

**Class II — Structural Amendment:** Modifications to a document's fundamental architecture — adding or removing Parts, redefining core concepts, restructuring authority. Requires extended deliberation and elevated authorization.

**Class III — Foundational Amendment:** Modifications to D1, D0, or D-1. Requires the highest amendment authority and the most comprehensive preservation audit.

**Class IV — Terminal Modification:** Any change that would affect D-2's terminal commitments. Class IV amendments are constitutionally inadmissible under the Constitutional Continuity Principle.

### 12.6 New Document Authorization

Adding new documents to the constitutional stack (D8 and beyond) is constitutionally equivalent to a Class II structural amendment to the stack as a whole. It requires:
- The same amendment authority as structural amendments
- An explicit statement of the new document's scope, what it builds on, and what it leaves for later documents
- A Preservation Assessment confirming the new document does not contradict any existing document
- A constitutional audit position for the new document

**Constitutional Consequence:** All CivilizationalDecisions formed from the misaligned CUM must be reviewed. All active Strategic Plans grounded in the misaligned CUM must be suspended pending CUM correction. The civilization is classified as operating under civilizational epistemic impairment.

**Resolution Protocol:** A comprehensive civilizational epistemic audit must be conducted, examining each domain's Understanding Model inputs to the CUM synthesis, the Constitutional Synthesis process itself, and the CUM's representation of external reality. Systematic biases or structural flaws in the synthesis process must be identified and corrected. The CUM may not be relied upon for new civilizational decisions until the audit is complete and a corrected CUM is synthesized.

### CIF-2 — Strategic Drift

**Scenario:** The civilization's Strategic Understanding gradually diverges from its actual circumstances through accumulating staleness, unrevised plans, and Causal Models that have been repeatedly falsified without triggering strategic revision. The civilization executes strategies formed under conditions that no longer exist.

**Detection:** Strategic Review Cycles exceeded without review; multiple Strategic Plans with persistent Milestone Failure Assessments; TOC-5 (Failed Theory Recognition) triggering across multiple related Causal Models; Observed Consequences consistently surprising relative to strategic expectations.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Strategic Drift constitutes a violation of CIP-3 (Strategic Grounding) — the civilization is taking actions that cannot be meaningfully assessed against current strategic reality. All active Strategic Plans must enter Emergency Strategic Review Cycle.

**Resolution Protocol:** Strategic Understanding refreshed from the current CUM through deliberation. All active Strategic Plans assessed against refreshed Strategic Understanding. Plans no longer connected to current strategic reality are suspended pending revision or termination. A Strategic Learning Record (SFD-4) must be produced.

### CIF-3 — Deliberation Failure

**Scenario:** The civilization's Deliberation Architecture fails — either because deliberation is being bypassed, because deliberation is structurally compromised (single-actor deliberation, authority substituting for evidence, uncertainty suppressed), or because the Emergency Deliberation Protocol is being invoked routinely.

**Detection:** CivilizationalDecisions without complete Deliberation Records; Deliberation Records showing single-participant deliberation; pattern of Emergency Deliberation Protocol invocations without genuine emergency justification; Deliberation Records in which all minority positions were overridden by the same authority without evidence-based addressing.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Deliberation Failure corrupts the constitutional basis of CivilizationalDecisions. Decisions formed without valid deliberation are constitutionally impaired — lacking the accountability structure that makes decisions reviewable and correctable.

**Resolution Protocol:** All CivilizationalDecisions formed without valid Deliberation Records must be reviewed. Systemic deliberation failures require a Deliberation Architecture audit to identify structural flaws. Where authority has systematically substituted for evidence, relevant authority assignments must be reviewed for AIR compliance.

### CIF-4 — Decision Failure

**Scenario:** The civilization's CivilizationalDecision objects fail to satisfy their constitutional requirements — through missing authority, absent justification, failure to pass D4 Constitutional Gates, failure to register in the Open Action Register, or failure to complete the decision chain through Observed Consequences.

**Detection:** Open Action Register containing civilizational decisions without traceable authority; CivilizationalDecisions without Justification Records; decisions without Effect Expectations; systematic failure to complete the Observed Consequence stage; decisions in the Open Action Register beyond their Effect Observation Window without terminal status.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Decision Failure means the civilization is executing actions without constitutional accountability. The Projection Responsibility Principle (D5) assigns accountability for all external effects regardless of whether the decision mechanism was constitutionally sound. Decision Failure accumulates accountability without accountability records.

**Resolution Protocol:** Open Action Register audit for all civilizational-scope decisions. All decisions without required constitutional elements must be flagged and their consequences managed. Future decisions must pass D4 gates and produce complete decision objects before any Action Projection is authorized.

### CIF-5 — Cross-Domain Reasoning Failure

**Scenario:** The civilization fails to reason correctly about cross-domain phenomena — because cross-domain reasoning is not invoked when required, because domain perspectives are collapsed, because conflicts are resolved without proper resolution authority, or because cross-domain reasoning insights are not integrated back into domain knowledge states.

**Detection:** Civilizational actions producing cross-domain consequences not anticipated in deliberation; domain conflicts discovered through consequences rather than through reasoning; Cross-Domain Reasoning Records showing consistent CDR violations; domains discovering shared ExternalRealitySegments through operational collision.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Cross-Domain Reasoning Failure means the civilization is effectively blind to phenomena spanning domain boundaries — including the most complex and consequential civilization-level phenomena.

**Resolution Protocol:** Cross-Domain Reasoning capability audit examining CDR-1 through CDR-5 compliance. All known cross-domain conflicts reviewed for resolution status. Domains with chronic unresolved conflicts escalated to DOM-000001. CDR-5 learning integration verified for recent processes.

### CIF-6 — Civilization Reality Disconnect

**Scenario:** The civilization's highest-level intelligence layer — the CUM, Strategic Understanding, and active civilizational decisions — becomes constitutionally severed from external reality. The civilization operates from an internal model that has lost meaningful connection to the actual external world and the actual state of the civilization's own domains.

**Detection:** CUM coherence failing across multiple dimensions simultaneously; Strategic Objectives no longer traceable to any current Domain Understanding Model; active Strategic Plans containing no current observations; civilizational decisions producing systematically unexpected consequences; multiple domains simultaneously in reality-disconnect failure (D6 DF-6); D5 Reality Alignment Loop broken at civilizational scope.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Civilization Reality Disconnect is the most severe failure mode in the civilizational intelligence architecture. The civilization is no longer constitutionally functional as an intelligence. All CivilizationalDecisions must be suspended. All Action Projections at civilizational scope must halt. DOM-000001 must be notified and founding operators must be informed.

**Resolution Protocol:** Emergency Observation Projection campaign across all twelve domains simultaneously. CUM synthesis suspended until domain-level reality alignment is restored. Strategic Plans suspended. All existing CivilizationalDecisions reviewed from scratch. The civilization re-enters the Reality Alignment Loop from the Observation stage. Civilization Reality Disconnect constitutes a fundamental constitutional emergency requiring founding operator involvement.

### CIF-7 — Constitutional Evolution Failure

**Scenario:** The Constitutional Amendment Architecture fails — either because amendments are being made without following the constitutional process, because the process itself is producing amendments that violate the Constitutional Continuity Principle, or because the constitution has become so inadequate to the civilization's actual situation that it is being systematically bypassed rather than amended.

**Detection:** Constitutional documents being changed without proper Amendment Proposal, Deliberation, and Authorization sequence; amendments failing the Preservation Assessment implemented regardless; patterns of operational bypass where domain operations consistently violate constitutional requirements without escalation.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Constitutional Evolution Failure is existential — it means the constitution is losing its governing function. If the amendment process is systematically bypassed, the civilization is operating without constitutional governance, using constitutional language as cover for unaccountable action.

**Resolution Protocol:** Founding operators must directly assess the state of constitutional governance. All unauthorized amendments must be identified and declared void. The constitutional bypass patterns must be documented and addressed through legitimate amendment where bypass reveals genuine constitutional inadequacy. The distinction between constitutional inadequacy (requiring amendment) and constitutional violation (requiring enforcement) must be drawn explicitly.
