## PART 1 — PROJECTION FRAMEWORK FOUNDATION

### 1.1 Constitutional Definition of Projection

**Projection** is the constitutional term designating any governed interaction between the APEX Reality Fabric and the external reality that lies beyond the Fabric's direct representation authority.

Projection is not production. The Reality Fabric does not produce external reality. It does not author, control, or own external reality. Projection is the bounded act of reaching — outward to receive, or outward to act — across a constitutionally defined boundary.

Two directions of projection exist:

**Inbound Projection:** External reality states and events cross the Projection Boundary and enter the Reality Fabric as constitutionally governed Observation Records.

**Outbound Projection:** Civilizational decisions and actions cross the Projection Boundary and enter external reality as effects, which then return as observable consequences.

All projection is bounded. No projection is unconditional. Every projection must preserve constitutional integrity at all stages of transit.

### 1.2 Internal Reality

**Internal Reality** is the constitutionally represented world inside the Reality Fabric.

Internal Reality comprises:
- All URO objects governed by D2
- All relationships governed by D3
- All operational records governed by D4
- All epistemic products (Observations, Evidence, Beliefs, KnowledgeStates, Decisions) formed within the Fabric
- All ActorProfiles, authority records, and constitutional infrastructure

Internal Reality is the domain of full constitutional authority. The Reality Fabric governs Internal Reality completely. Every object, relationship, and operation within Internal Reality is subject to the invariants, gates, and failure protocols of D0 through D4.

### 1.3 External Reality

**External Reality** is the reality existing beyond the Fabric's direct constitutional authority.

External Reality includes but is not limited to:

- **Physical environments:** material conditions, geographic contexts, ecological systems, built infrastructure
- **Humans:** individual persons, their intentions, beliefs, behaviors, and physical states
- **Organizations:** collective actors, institutions, enterprises, and governance bodies
- **External systems:** technological platforms, communication networks, computation environments, markets
- **Unknown states:** conditions that exist but have not been observed, detected, or represented
- **Independent actors:** agents whose choices and actions are not governed by APEX constitutional law

External Reality is not governed by the Reality Fabric. It exists, changes, and acts without reference to APEX constitutional authority. The Reality Fabric may represent External Reality; it does not govern it.

### 1.4 The Projection Boundary

**The Projection Boundary** is the constitutional boundary separating the domain of the Reality Fabric from the domain of External Reality.

The Projection Boundary has four dimensions:

**1.4.1 What the Fabric Knows**
The set of External Reality states that have been observed, recorded, and integrated into the Reality Fabric as constitutionally valid Observation Records. What the Fabric knows is always a subset of External Reality, never identical to it.

**1.4.2 What the Fabric Infers**
The set of External Reality states that the Fabric holds as inferred — derived from known observations through registered reasoning protocols but not directly observed. Inferences are held with lower epistemic standing than observations and must be marked accordingly.

**1.4.3 What the Fabric Affects**
The set of External Reality states that have been modified by outbound projections originating from within the Fabric. The Fabric's causal reach into External Reality is bounded and must be tracked.

**1.4.4 What Exists Independently**
The set of External Reality states that exist and evolve without any causal connection to the Fabric — neither observed by it nor affected by it. This is the constitutionally acknowledged domain of irreducible external independence.

The Projection Boundary is not a wall — it is a governed crossing zone. All crossings must be constitutionally authorized, recorded, and coherence-checked.

### 1.5 The Fundamental Projection Principle

> **"The Reality Fabric does not create reality. It creates a constitutionally governed representation of reality and interacts with reality through bounded projection mechanisms."**

This principle is foundational and irrevocable. It applies to all operations defined in this document and in all successor documents.

Three commitments follow directly from this principle:

**FPP-1 — Epistemic Humility:** The Fabric's representation of External Reality is always partial, always mediated, and always subject to correction. No internal model of external conditions is ever final.

**FPP-2 — Causal Accountability:** When the Fabric acts upon External Reality, it bears constitutional accountability for the effects produced. The scope of this accountability is bounded by what the Fabric could reasonably have known at the time of projection.

**FPP-3 — Reality Authority:** External Reality holds authority over the correctness of internal representations. When observation and internal model contradict, internal models must yield.

## PART 2 — EXTERNAL REALITY MODEL

### 2.1 Constitutional Status of External Reality Categories

External Reality categories are not URO objects. They are not governed by D2. They are not nodes in the Reality Fabric governed by D3. They are not subject to the Constitutional Enforcement Kernel of D4.

External Reality categories are **constitutional reference categories** — formal definitions of the kinds of external phenomena that the Fabric is constitutionally designed to engage with, represent, and act upon.

These categories extend D0's Ontological Constitution and D1's Civilization Ontology into the external-reality domain. They do not redefine any D0 or D1 category. They occupy the constitutional space beyond the Projection Boundary.

### 2.2 The Seven External Reality Categories

#### 2.2.1 ExternalRealitySegment

A bounded, identifiable portion of External Reality that constitutes the object of a specific observational or operational engagement. Every observation and every outbound projection must reference an ExternalRealitySegment. The segment's boundaries are always approximate and constitutionally acknowledged as such.

#### 2.2.2 ExternalState

A condition, configuration, or status of an ExternalRealitySegment or any entity within it, at a specific point or interval in external time. ExternalStates exist whether or not the Fabric has observed them. The Fabric's representation of an ExternalState is always distinct from the ExternalState itself.

#### 2.2.3 ExternalActor

An agent — human, collective, organizational, or systemic — that operates within External Reality with the capacity to initiate actions, hold intentions, and modify ExternalStates. ExternalActors act independently of APEX constitutional authority. An ExternalActor may correspond to an internal ActorProfile (see Section 2.4) but remains constitutionally external.

#### 2.2.4 ExternalSystem

An organized collection of entities, processes, and relationships in External Reality that exhibits coherent behavior as a whole. ExternalSystems include markets, ecosystems, institutional frameworks, technical platforms, and social networks. ExternalSystems have internal dynamics that are not governed by the Fabric and may produce emergent behaviors that are not predictable from constituent observations.

#### 2.2.5 ExternalEvent

A discrete, temporally bounded occurrence in External Reality that marks a change of state or a transition with causal significance. ExternalEvents are the observable pulse of change in External Reality. The Fabric's observation of an ExternalEvent is distinct from the event itself.

#### 2.2.6 ExternalEnvironment

The broader contextual surround of an ExternalRealitySegment — the totality of external conditions, background forces, and structural constraints that shape what is possible within the segment but that are not themselves the primary object of engagement. The ExternalEnvironment is always partially under-observed. Its boundary with the ExternalRealitySegment is constitutionally approximate.

#### 2.2.7 ExternalConstraint

A limitation imposed by External Reality on what actions, states, or transformations are possible, regardless of internal intent or authorization. ExternalConstraints are derived from physical laws, social norms, institutional rules, resource limits, and ecological boundaries. They are not negotiable by constitutional fiat. They may be discovered, violated (with consequences), or misunderstood, but they cannot be abolished by internal decree.

### 2.3 ExternalReference Architecture

Every internal representation that references an external reality category must be structured as an **ExternalReference**. An ExternalReference is not the external thing itself; it is the Fabric's constitutionally valid pointer to an external thing.

Every ExternalReference must contain:

| Attribute | Constitutional Requirement |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Identity** | A unique, stable identifier for the referenced external entity or state |
| **Provenance** | The lineage of how this reference was formed — from which observations, through which protocols |
| **Observation Source** | The instrument, channel, or method through which this external entity was observed |
| **Confidence** | The epistemic confidence level, as a registered value, reflecting uncertainty in the reference |
| **Temporal Position** | The timestamp at which the referenced ExternalState was believed to obtain |
| **Relationship to Internal Objects** | Explicit links to the internal URO objects (ActorProfiles, Evidence, Beliefs) that correspond to or derive from this reference |

An ExternalReference with any missing attribute is constitutionally incomplete and may not be used as the basis for decisions or actions.

### 2.4 Internal Object and External Referent

**The constitutional distinction between an Internal Object and an External Referent is absolute.**

An **Internal Object** is a URO object governed by D2, constituted within the Reality Fabric, subject to all D3 fabric invariants and D4 enforcement gates.

An **External Referent** is a reference to an entity or state that exists in External Reality. It is represented inside the Fabric as an ExternalReference but the thing it points to exists outside the Fabric's authority.

**Constitutional Example:**

> A human who interacts with APEX:
>
> Inside the Fabric: **ActorProfile** — a URO object with identity layers, authority records, epistemic history, and operational standing. Governed by D2, D3, D4.
>
> Outside the Fabric: **ExternalActor** reference — the actual human person, with beliefs, intentions, physical existence, and independent agency. Not governed by D4. Not governed by D2. The ActorProfile represents the human; it is not the human.

The Fabric must never collapse this distinction. Operations that treat an Internal Object as if it were the External Referent it represents are constitutional violations of the highest order.

## PART 3 — OBSERVATION PROJECTION

### 3.1 Constitutional Role

Observation Projection is the governed process by which External Reality states and events cross the Projection Boundary and enter the Reality Fabric as constitutionally valid knowledge products.

This section extends D3's Observation Boundary definition. D3 defines the boundary as the constitutional interface between external reality and APEX representation. D5 Part 3 defines the complete lifecycle that governs every crossing of that boundary in the inbound direction.

### 3.2 Observation Projection Lifecycle

The Observation Projection Lifecycle comprises five constitutionally defined stages:

```
External State
     ↓
Observation Event
     ↓
Observation Record
     ↓
Evidence Assessment
     ↓
Epistemic Integration
```

Each stage imposes constitutional obligations. No stage may be skipped. No stage may be reordered.

#### Stage 1 — External State

The first stage is the acknowledgment that an External State exists in External Reality, whether or not the Fabric has detected it. The External State exists independently. This stage is constitutionally prior to observation — the Fabric cannot manufacture the External State through the act of observing it.

**Constitutional obligation:** The Fabric must not retroactively define External States to match its internal models. External States are discovered, not created, by observation.

#### Stage 2 — Observation Event

An Observation Event occurs when the Fabric's observational capacity is engaged with an External State or ExternalEvent. An Observation Event is the constitutionally registered moment of instrumental contact with External Reality.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- The observational instrument must be registered in the APEX Observer Register
- The instrument's calibration state must be known and recorded
- The ExternalRealitySegment and ExternalState being engaged must be identified
- The Observation Event must be timestamped

The ObservationFormationWindow (D4) opens at this stage.

#### Stage 3 — Observation Record

An Observation Record is the constitutional artifact produced from an Observation Event. It is the Fabric's first internal representation of the External State encountered. The Observation Record is a URO object (D2) and must satisfy all D2 formation requirements.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- The Observation Record must preserve, without modification, the raw epistemic content of the Observation Event
- The Observation Record must carry: ExternalRealitySegment reference, ExternalState reference, instrument identity, calibration state, timestamp, initial confidence value
- The Observation Record must be registered in the D3 Reality Fabric
- The ObservationFormationWindow closes at this stage

The Observation Record is the atomic unit of the Fabric's contact with External Reality. It must never be altered after formation. Subsequent interpretation produces new objects (Evidence, Beliefs); the Observation Record remains immutable.

#### Stage 4 — Evidence Assessment

Evidence Assessment is the constitutional process by which one or more Observation Records are evaluated, compared, and synthesized into Evidence — a higher-order epistemic product that represents the Fabric's assessed understanding of an External State based on available observations.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Evidence must reference all constituent Observation Records
- The synthesis protocol used must be registered and versioned
- Where Observation Records conflict, the conflict must be recorded and a resolution rationale provided
- Evidence confidence must be derived from constituent observation confidence through a registered aggregation protocol — it may not be inflated beyond what the observations warrant
- Evidence must preserve temporal position: the Evidence represents External States at a specific time, not universally

Evidence Assessment may involve multiple Observation Records from multiple instruments, times, and perspectives. Corroboration increases confidence through registered calculation; it does not eliminate uncertainty.

#### Stage 5 — Epistemic Integration

Epistemic Integration is the constitutional process by which assessed Evidence is incorporated into the Reality Fabric's active epistemic picture — the body of Beliefs, KnowledgeStates, and Understanding models that inform civilizational decisions.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Integration must check for coherence with existing epistemic objects in the Fabric
- Evidence that contradicts existing Beliefs must produce a Contradiction Record, not silent overwrite
- Evidence that confirms existing Beliefs may raise their confidence through a registered protocol
- Integration must update all affected Knowledge objects through the D3 fabric update lifecycle
- Integration must update the Reality Gap Register (D4) if the new Evidence reveals a gap between the Fabric's prior model and the current observed External State

### 3.3 Observation Limitations

**The Fabric must never assume: Observation = Reality itself.**

This prohibition is absolute and irrevocable. An Observation Record is a representation of a contact with External Reality at a specific time through a specific instrument. It is not the External State it reports. It is not a reproduction of External Reality. It is a bounded, mediated, time-indexed, instrument-dependent epistemic artifact.

The following errors are constitutionally prohibited:

- Treating an Observation Record as ground truth without acknowledging instrument limitations
- Treating a past observation as representing current External State without acknowledging temporal decay
- Treating high-confidence observations as certainties
- Treating the absence of an observation as the absence of an External State

### 3.4 Observation Uncertainty Requirements

Every projection from External Reality into the Fabric must preserve the following uncertainty attributes throughout the full Observation Projection Lifecycle:

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Source** | The instrument and method through which the observation was formed must be permanently recorded |
| **Confidence** | A registered epistemic confidence value, derived from instrument calibration and observation conditions |
| **Limitations** | The known limitations of the instrument and method — what it cannot see, what it may distort |
| **Timestamp** | The time at which the external contact occurred, and the time at which the Observation Record was formed |
| **Observer Capability** | The constitutional scope of what this instrument is registered to observe — no observation may be used beyond the registered scope of its instrument |

These attributes must propagate through Evidence Assessment and Epistemic Integration. They may not be stripped at any stage. Epistemic products that have lost their uncertainty attributes are constitutionally invalid.

## PART 4 — ACTION PROJECTION

### 4.1 Constitutional Role

Action Projection is the governed process by which internal civilizational decisions cross the Projection Boundary and produce effects in External Reality, and by which those effects are observed, recorded, and integrated back into the Fabric.

Action Projection is the outbound counterpart of Observation Projection. Where Observation Projection governs the inbound crossing of the Projection Boundary, Action Projection governs the outbound crossing.

### 4.2 Action Projection Lifecycle

The Action Projection Lifecycle comprises seven constitutionally defined stages:

```
CivilizationalDecision
         ↓
  Authority Validation
         ↓
  CivilizationalAction
         ↓
External Action Projection
         ↓
     External Effect
         ↓
  Observed Consequence
         ↓
  Reality Fabric Update
```

Each stage imposes constitutional obligations. No stage may be skipped. No stage may be reordered. Progression from any stage to the next requires the constitutional preconditions of the current stage to be satisfied.

#### Stage 1 — CivilizationalDecision

A CivilizationalDecision is the constitutional commitment formed within the Fabric to pursue a course of action that will affect External Reality. It is formed through the deliberation protocol and grounded in the Fabric's epistemic picture of the relevant External Reality domain.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- The CivilizationalDecision must be registered in the Open Action Register (D4)
- The CivilizationalDecision must specify the target ExternalRealitySegment and intended ExternalState transition
- The CivilizationalDecision must reference the Understanding model that grounds it
- The CivilizationalDecision must identify all known ExternalConstraints and ExternalActors that the action will engage

#### Stage 2 — Authority Validation

The CivilizationalDecision passes through D4's six Constitutional Gates (Identity Resolution, Object/State Validation, Authority Validation, Epistemic Validation, Constitutive Coherence, Temporal/Historical Integrity).

**Constitutional obligations:**
- No CivilizationalDecision may proceed to Stage 3 without passing all six D4 gates
- The D4 enforcement record must be attached to the Action Projection's provenance chain
- Authority Validation is a hard gate: no constitutional bypass is permissible except through D4's authorized mechanisms

Authority Validation does not validate the action against External Reality — it validates the action against constitutional law. External Reality validation occurs through Constraint Assessment within Stage 3.

#### Stage 3 — CivilizationalAction

A CivilizationalAction is the constitutionally authorized, specified, and constraint-validated form of the CivilizationalDecision — the immediate precursor to outbound projection.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- The CivilizationalAction must specify precisely what will be projected: the message, physical operation, system configuration, or resource allocation
- Known ExternalConstraints must be assessed against the action at this stage; violations that were knowable and not checked constitute constitutional failures
- The projection channel must be registered
- The effect latency (the expected interval between projection and observable effect) must be estimated and recorded
- Reversibility must be assessed: the CivilizationalAction must carry a reversibility classification

#### Stage 4 — External Action Projection

External Action Projection is the crossing of the Projection Boundary — the moment at which the CivilizationalAction enters External Reality through the registered channel.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- The projection must be timestamped with constitutional precision
- The projection timestamp anchors the action in constitutional time and governs all subsequent accountability
- The channel through which projection occurs must be the registered channel; unauthorized channels are constitutionally inadmissible
- Projection must be registered as complete before Stage 5 can open

#### Stage 5 — External Effect

The External Effect is the change in ExternalState produced by the action's entry into External Reality. The External Effect exists in External Reality, not in the Fabric.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- The Fabric must not presume the External Effect without observation
- The Effect Observation Window (the constitutional interval within which an Observed Consequence must be formed) opens at this stage
- The Fabric must initiate an Observation Projection (Part 3) to detect the External Effect

#### Stage 6 — Observed Consequence

An Observed Consequence is the constitutional record produced when the Observation Projection initiated in Stage 5 completes — the Fabric's constitutionally confirmed record of what actually changed in External Reality as a result of the action.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Every Action Projection must produce an Observed Consequence
- The Observed Consequence must be formed through the full Observation Projection Lifecycle (Part 3) — it is not a summary or assumption
- The Observed Consequence must reference the Action Projection identity, Stage 4 projection timestamp, and Stage 5 effect window
- If the Effect Observation Window expires without an Observed Consequence, the Action Projection is classified as LOST and the Broken Feedback Protocol (Part 8) applies

#### Stage 7 — Reality Fabric Update

The Reality Fabric Update is the constitutional integration of the Observed Consequence into the Fabric's epistemic picture — the final stage of Action Projection closure.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- The Open Action Register (D4) entry must be closed: COMPLETE, PARTIAL, FAILED, or LOST
- All Beliefs, KnowledgeStates, and Understanding models that were based on the pre-action ExternalState must be reviewed and updated
- The Reality Gap Register must be updated to reflect any discrepancy between the expected External Effect (Stage 1) and the Observed Consequence (Stage 6)
- A provenance record comprising the complete Stage 1–7 chain must be formed and preserved

### 4.3 The Projection Responsibility Principle

> **"Any action projected into external reality creates accountability obligations for all resulting effects."**

This principle is irrevocable and applies without exception to every Stage 4 projection.

Accountability is not limited to intended effects. The Projection Responsibility Principle covers:
- Intended effects that were fully produced
- Intended effects that were partially produced
- Intended effects that were not produced
- Unintended effects that resulted from the action
- Effects that propagated through ExternalSystems and ExternalActors

The scope of accountability is bounded by what the Fabric could constitutionally have known at the time of Stage 1 decision formation. Unknown and unknowable effects do not void the constitutional obligation — they extend it: the Fabric is obligated to pursue knowledge of unknown effects through the Observation Projection Lifecycle.

### 4.4 Internal Action and External Action Distinguished

**Internal Action:** An action that affects only objects within the Reality Fabric — URO objects, relationships, authority records. Internal actions are fully governed by D4. They do not cross the Projection Boundary.

**External Action:** An action that crosses the Projection Boundary and produces effects in External Reality. External actions are governed by D4 for their internal constitution and by D5 for the projection lifecycle. The Projection Responsibility Principle applies only to External Actions.

An Internal Action that produces an External Action as a downstream consequence is subject to both — the Internal Action portion governed by D4, the External Action portion by D5.

## PART 5 — REALITY TRANSLATION LAYER

### 5.1 The Constitutional Translation Problem

The Reality Fabric's internal models of External Reality are not identical to External Reality itself. This is not a technical limitation to be overcome — it is a constitutional fact to be governed.

Every point in the chain from External Reality to Civilizational Decision to External Consequence involves translation: the conversion of one form of representation into another. Each translation is an epistemic act that may introduce error, lose information, or misrepresent the original. The Reality Translation Layer constitutes the constitutional framework that governs these translations.

### 5.2 The Constitutional Translation Process

The full translation process spans seven steps:

```
External Reality
      ↓
  Observation
      ↓
Representation
      ↓
Understanding Model
      ↓
    Decision
      ↓
    Action
      ↓
External Consequence
```

**Step 1 — External Reality:** The ground truth that exists independently of the Fabric. Inaccessible directly; accessible only through observation.

**Step 2 — Observation:** The bounded, mediated epistemic contact with External Reality through the Observation Projection Lifecycle (Part 3). Observation is the first and most critical translation: from External Reality into a Fabric-internal Observation Record.

**Step 3 — Representation:** The synthesis of Observations into Evidence (Part 3, Stage 4) and the constitution of that Evidence as Beliefs and KnowledgeStates within the Fabric. Representation is a higher-order translation: from raw observations into structured epistemic objects.

**Step 4 — Understanding Model:** The integration of representations into a coherent situational or civilizational model that supports decisions. The Understanding Model is the Fabric's working picture of the relevant domain.

**Step 5 — Decision:** The formation of a CivilizationalDecision based on the Understanding Model. The Decision is the translation from epistemic picture to action commitment.

**Step 6 — Action:** The execution of the CivilizationalAction through the Action Projection Lifecycle (Part 4). The Action is the translation from commitment to external projection.

**Step 7 — External Consequence:** The actual effect in External Reality. The External Consequence is the final output of the translation chain — and, through Observed Consequence and Reality Fabric Update, the input to a new cycle.

Each step imposes translation obligations governed by the Translation Fidelity Requirements (Section 5.4).

### 5.3 Translation Failure Modes

Five classes of translation failure are constitutionally defined:

**TF-1 — Incomplete Representation**
The Observation or Representation does not capture all constitutionally significant aspects of the External State. The Fabric's picture is accurate as far as it goes but systemically incomplete.

*Constitutional consequence:* Decisions built on incomplete representations may produce unexpected consequences. All decisions must carry a completeness assessment derived from the known limitations of the contributing observations.

**TF-2 — Incorrect Interpretation**
The Representation does not accurately reflect the External State it purports to represent. The translation from Observation to Representation introduced distortion.

*Constitutional consequence:* Incorrect interpretations must be corrected when discovered. All downstream beliefs, knowledge states, and decisions derived from the incorrect interpretation must be reviewed. The interpretation protocol that produced the error must be examined.

**TF-3 — Outdated Understanding**
The Understanding Model was accurate at a prior time but External Reality has changed and the Model has not been updated. The translation from Observation to Understanding Model has decayed.

*Constitutional consequence:* Currency tracking is mandatory. Understanding Models must carry temporal validity bounds calibrated to the volatility of the represented External State. Decisions based on Understanding Models that have exceeded their temporal validity bounds are constitutionally impaired.

**TF-4 — Incorrect Projection**
The Action does not faithfully execute the Decision. The translation from Decision to Action introduced distortion, error, or unauthorized modification.

*Constitutional consequence:* Actions must be verified against their authorizing Decisions before projection. Unauthorized modifications to the action between Decision and Projection constitute a constitutional failure.

**TF-5 — Unintended Consequence**
The External Consequence differs materially from what the Action was projected to produce. The translation from Action to External Consequence produced unexpected effects.

*Constitutional consequence:* Unintended Consequences must be observed, recorded, and integrated into the Reality Fabric through the Observed Consequence and Reality Fabric Update stages. The Projection Responsibility Principle applies. The Understanding Model must be updated to reflect the new information about external dynamics.

### 5.4 Translation Fidelity Requirements

All translations across the constitutional translation chain must satisfy the following requirements:

**TFR-1 — Epistemic Fidelity:** Translations must not increase confidence beyond what the source warrants. Uncertainty must propagate forward through the chain.

**TFR-2 — Source Traceability:** Every epistemic product must trace to its observation origins. Traceability may not be severed at any translation step.

**TFR-3 — Protocol Registration:** The method used for each translation must be a registered, versioned protocol. Unregistered translation methods are constitutionally inadmissible.

**TFR-4 — Completeness Record:** Where a translation is known to be incomplete, the incompleteness must be recorded. Silent omissions are prohibited.

**TFR-5 — Temporal Anchoring:** Every translation product must carry the temporal position of its source. Translation must not strip temporal context.

## PART 6 — PROJECTION INVARIANTS

Projection Invariants are constitutional laws that must hold across all projection operations. A violation of any Projection Invariant constitutes a constitutional failure that triggers the applicable Projection Failure Mode (Part 9) and D4 enforcement.

---

**PI-1 — Reality Independence**

The external world exists independently of the Fabric's representation. External Reality is not created, sustained, or governed by the Reality Fabric. Its existence does not depend on the Fabric's knowledge of it.

*Operational obligation:* The Fabric must never operate as if its representation of External Reality were constitutive of External Reality itself. The Fabric's models describe; they do not determine.

*Violation:* Any operation that treats the absence of an observation as the absence of an External State, or that treats the Fabric's model of a situation as the situation itself.

---

**PI-2 — Representation Non-Identity**

A representation is never identical to the reality it represents. No Observation Record, Evidence object, Belief, KnowledgeState, or Understanding Model equals the External State, ExternalActor, ExternalSystem, or ExternalEvent it represents.

*Operational obligation:* Every reference to an External Reality entity within the Fabric must be marked as a representation. The constitutional distinction between the map and the territory must be preserved at every level.

*Violation:* Any operation that treats an internal epistemic object as the external reality it models — e.g., treating an ActorProfile as equivalent to the human it represents.

---

**PI-3 — Observation Traceability**

Every claim the Fabric makes about External Reality must trace to observation origins. No knowledge of External Reality may exist within the Fabric without an ancestral chain that leads through the Observation Projection Lifecycle to actual Observation Records.

*Operational obligation:* Observation lineage must be preserved at every stage of the epistemic chain. Knowledge objects without traceable observation ancestry are constitutionally inadmissible as bases for Decisions.

*Exception:* Foundational axioms (D-1) that are constitutionally prior to observation.

*Violation:* Any epistemic claim about External Reality that has no traceable observation lineage.

---

**PI-4 — Projection Accountability**

Every outbound projection must preserve responsibility chains. The Fabric is constitutionally accountable for every effect it produces in External Reality. This accountability is permanent and cannot be extinguished by the passage of time, delegation, or the complexity of causal chains.

*Operational obligation:* All Action Projections must be registered in the Open Action Register before execution. All Observed Consequences must be associated with their originating Action Projections. Accountability records must be preserved according to D4 provenance requirements.

*Violation:* Any External Effect that cannot be traced to an authorized, registered Action Projection.

---

**PI-5 — Consequence Recording**

External effects must generate historical records. Every ExternalState change produced by an Action Projection must be recorded as an Observed Consequence and integrated into the Reality Fabric through Stage 7 of the Action Projection Lifecycle.

*Operational obligation:* No Action Projection may be closed without a constitutional record of its external consequences. The Projection Responsibility Principle (Part 4) cannot be discharged without consequence recording.

*Violation:* Any Action Projection whose Stage 7 Reality Fabric Update does not include a record of the external effects produced.

---

**PI-6 — Boundary Integrity**

Internal authority cannot automatically become external authority. Constitutional authorization within the Reality Fabric does not constitute authorization in External Reality. The Projection Boundary separates two distinct authority domains: the Fabric's internal constitutional authority and the authority structures of External Reality.

*Operational obligation:* Outbound projections must not represent APEX as holding authority in External Reality that it does not actually hold. The Fabric's authority claims in External Reality are bounded by what External Reality recognizes and accepts.

*Violation:* Any outbound projection that invokes APEX internal constitutional authority as if it were binding on ExternalActors or ExternalSystems that have not accepted that authority.

---

**PI-7 — Reality Correction**

External feedback can invalidate internal assumptions. When the Observation Projection Lifecycle produces Evidence that contradicts an existing internal Belief, KnowledgeState, or Understanding Model, the internal object must be revised. External Reality holds epistemic authority over internal representations.

*Operational obligation:* The Reality Feedback Loop (Part 8) must be operational at all times. Mechanisms that suppress external correction are constitutionally prohibited.

*Violation:* Any operation that retains a contradicted internal model in preference to the observational evidence that contradicts it.

---

**PI-8 — Projection Reversibility**

Where possible, projected actions must preserve reversal capability. Irreversible actions require higher constitutional authorization and must be expressly classified as irreversible before projection.

*Operational obligation:* Every CivilizationalAction must carry a reversibility classification before Stage 4. Actions classified as irreversible must meet the elevated authorization thresholds specified in D4's authority rules. Actions that are reversible must document the reversal conditions and mechanism.

*Violation:* Any irreversible action that was not classified as such before projection, or any irreversible action that did not meet elevated authorization requirements.

---

**PI-9 — Temporal Ordering**

Projection operations must respect causal temporal ordering. An Observation Record must represent an External State that existed at or before the observation timestamp. An Action Projection must not be attributed to an effect that preceded the Stage 4 projection timestamp.

*Operational obligation:* All projection timestamps must be constitutionally accurate. Retroactive attribution of effects to causes that postdate them is prohibited except through a constitutionally registered retroactive correction procedure.

*Violation:* Any projection whose temporal records invert the causal order between cause and effect.

---

**PI-10 — Observer Non-Contamination**

The constitutional act of forming an Observation Record must not be confused with the existence of the External State being observed. APEX observations may have causal effects in External Reality — but the act of registration must not manufacture the External State it purports to record.

*Operational obligation:* Observation methods must be assessed for their causal impact on observed External States. When observation causally disturbs the observed state, the contamination must be recorded in the Observation Record and factored into Evidence Assessment.

*Violation:* Any Observation Record that conflates the act of observation with the independent existence of the observed ExternalState.

---

**PI-11 — Civilizational Scope**

All projection operations must respect the civilizational domain boundaries established in D1. Observations must be registered to the domain of the observed ExternalRealitySegment. Action Projections targeting multiple domains require authorization from all affected domain authorities.

*Operational obligation:* Domain attribution must appear in all Observation Records and Action Projection records. Cross-domain projections must carry cross-domain authorization records from D4.

*Violation:* Any projection that operates across domain boundaries without proper authorization, or that misattributes domain membership in its provenance record.

---

**PI-12 — Feedback Completion**

Every outbound projection must close its feedback loop. The full cycle from CivilizationalDecision through Observed Consequence to Reality Fabric Update must complete. Open projection cycles are constitutional obligations, not optional post-hoc reviews.

*Operational obligation:* Every Open Action Register entry must reach a terminal state: COMPLETE, PARTIAL, FAILED, or LOST. No entry may remain permanently open. The Reality Feedback Loop (Part 8) applies to every Action Projection without exception.

*Violation:* Any Action Projection whose feedback loop is not closed within the constitutional time bounds.

## PART 7 — EXTERNAL COHERENCE MODEL

### 7.1 Constitutional Coherence Obligation

The Reality Fabric bears a continuous constitutional obligation to maintain external coherence — alignment between its internal epistemic picture and the actual states of External Reality as established through the Observation Projection Lifecycle.

External coherence is distinct from internal coherence (D3 Global Coherence Requirements, D4 Constitutional Invariants). Internal coherence is the consistency of the Fabric with itself. External coherence is the accuracy of the Fabric relative to External Reality it represents.

This obligation derives from D-2's core commitment: "The distance between representation and reality must be tracked." D5 Part 7 constitutes the mechanism by which this tracking is governed.

### 7.2 External Coherence Registers

Six External Coherence Registers are constitutionally defined. Each Register tracks a distinct dimension of the Fabric's alignment with External Reality.

#### Register 1 — External Identity Coherence

**Tracks:** The alignment between internal representations of external entities (ExternalActors, ExternalSystems) and those entities' actual identities, statuses, and existence in External Reality.

**Incoherence condition:** The Fabric holds an ActorProfile or ExternalReference for an entity that no longer exists in External Reality in the form the reference assumes, or the Fabric's identity model of an ExternalActor is systematically incorrect.

**Constitutional obligation:** Identity representations must be refreshed through observation at intervals calibrated to the volatility of the represented entity.

#### Register 2 — Observation Coherence

**Tracks:** The alignment between the Fabric's stock of Observation Records and the actual current External States of the ExternalRealitySegments they represent.

**Incoherence condition:** Observation Records represent External States that have changed significantly since observation, or coverage gaps exist in constitutionally significant ExternalRealitySegments.

**Constitutional obligation:** Observation currency must be tracked against ExternalState volatility. Stale observations must be flagged and new observations initiated.

#### Register 3 — Action Coherence

**Tracks:** The alignment between the Fabric's record of outbound projections and the actual effects those projections have produced in External Reality.

**Incoherence condition:** The Fabric's record of intended effects does not match the Observed Consequences of its actions.

**Constitutional obligation:** Every Action Projection must have a matching Observed Consequence. Discrepancies must be investigated and the Action Coherence Register updated.

#### Register 4 — Consequence Coherence

**Tracks:** The alignment between the Fabric's model of the consequences its actions will produce and the consequences that have actually been produced, including downstream and unintended effects.

**Incoherence condition:** The Fabric's consequence model systematically misrepresents how its actions propagate through External Reality, ExternalSystems, and ExternalActors.

**Constitutional obligation:** Systematic consequence model errors must trigger a constitutional review of the Understanding Models and translation protocols that produced them.

#### Register 5 — Environmental Coherence

**Tracks:** The alignment between the Fabric's model of the ExternalEnvironments surrounding its operational ExternalRealitySegments and the actual conditions of those environments.

**Incoherence condition:** The Fabric operates within an ExternalEnvironment that has changed materially from its internal model — new ExternalConstraints have emerged, ExternalSystems have shifted, or background conditions have transformed.

**Constitutional obligation:** Environmental models must be updated as environmental observations are received. Operations that depend on environmental models must carry currency assessments.

#### Register 6 — Relationship Coherence

**Tracks:** The alignment between the Fabric's model of relationships among ExternalActors, ExternalSystems, and external institutions, and the actual state of those relationships.

**Incoherence condition:** The Fabric models relationships (alliances, dependencies, conflicts, authorities) that have materially changed in External Reality without the Fabric's model being updated.

**Constitutional obligation:** Relationship models must be reviewed when any constituent ExternalActor or ExternalSystem changes state, when new observations bear on the relationship, or when time-bounded relationship assumptions reach their validity limit.

### 7.3 Handling External Contradictions

When the Observation Projection Lifecycle produces Evidence that contradicts the Fabric's current internal model, the contradiction must be handled by the following constitutional protocol:

**Contradiction Identification:** The contradiction must be explicitly identified and registered. It may not be silently absorbed or silently discarded.

**Source Assessment:** The contradiction must be assessed against its observational basis. A well-sourced, high-confidence observation contradicting a low-confidence belief is handled differently from a low-confidence observation contradicting a well-established KnowledgeState.

**Internal Model Revision:** Where the observational evidence for the external contradiction is constitutionally sufficient, the internal model must be revised. **Reality overrides representation.**

**Provisional Retention:** Where the observational evidence is insufficient to constitute a contradiction (low confidence, instrument limitations, single uncorroborated source), the internal model may be provisionally retained while flagged as contested. Additional observation must be initiated.

**Constitutional Prohibition:** The Fabric may not systematically protect internal models from external correction. Mechanisms that suppress, discount, or ignore observational contradictions of internal models are prohibited. Motivated preservation of internal models in the face of external evidence is a violation of PI-7.

> **Reality overrides representation. This is not a preference; it is a constitutional law.**

## PART 8 — REALITY FEEDBACK LOOP

### 8.1 The Reality Alignment Loop

The Reality Feedback Loop is the constitutional cycle by which the Fabric remains aligned with External Reality through the continuous iteration of observation, understanding, decision, action, effect observation, and updated understanding.

The **Reality Alignment Loop** operates as follows:

```
Reality
   ↓
Observation
   ↓
Understanding
   ↓
Decision
   ↓
Action
   ↓
Effect
   ↓
Observation
   ↓
Updated Understanding
```

This cycle is not optional. It is not a technical feature. It is the constitutional mechanism by which the Reality Fabric maintains its epistemic relationship with External Reality and fulfills the obligation of PI-7 (Reality Correction) and FPP-3 (Reality Authority).

The Reality Alignment Loop must be operational at all times. It is never fully closed — it is a continuous cycle, not a one-time procedure. Each iteration produces an Updated Understanding that becomes the foundation of the next cycle's Decision.

### 8.2 Loop Architecture

**Reality → Observation:** The Observation Projection Lifecycle (Part 3) executes. External States and Events cross the Projection Boundary and enter the Fabric as Observation Records.

**Observation → Understanding:** Evidence Assessment and Epistemic Integration (Part 3, Stages 4–5) convert Observations into Evidence, Beliefs, KnowledgeStates, and synthesized Understanding Models.

**Understanding → Decision:** The Fabric's deliberation process converts Understanding into a CivilizationalDecision grounded in the current epistemic picture.

**Decision → Action:** The Action Projection Lifecycle (Part 4, Stages 1–4) governs the transition from CivilizationalDecision through Authority Validation and CivilizationalAction to External Action Projection.

**Action → Effect:** The action crosses the Projection Boundary and produces an External Effect in External Reality.

**Effect → Observation:** A new Observation Projection Lifecycle is initiated specifically to observe the Effect (Part 4, Stage 5). The Effect is observed, an Observed Consequence is formed, and a new cycle of Observation enters the loop.

**Observation → Updated Understanding:** The Observed Consequence is integrated into the Reality Fabric (Stage 7), updating all affected Knowledge objects and Understanding Models. The Understanding is now updated in light of both the original Understanding and the feedback from the action's consequences.

### 8.3 Feedback Failure Conditions

The Reality Alignment Loop fails constitutionally when any of three conditions obtain:

**Condition 1 — The system acts without feedback:**
The Fabric executes Action Projections without initiating Observation Projections to detect their effects. The Effect → Observation step is omitted. This produces an Action Projection whose Stage 7 cannot complete because no Observed Consequence exists. The Open Action Register entry remains unresolvable. PI-12 is violated.

**Condition 2 — The system ignores external correction:**
The Observation Projection Lifecycle produces Evidence that contradicts existing internal models, and the Fabric's Epistemic Integration process does not update those models. PI-7 is violated. The Reality Alignment Loop is structurally present but functionally disabled at the Observation → Updated Understanding step.

**Condition 3 — The system protects internal models over reality:**
The Fabric systematically discounts, suppresses, or discards observations that would require revising internal models. This is the most severe feedback failure — a constitutional disposition failure, not merely a procedural omission. It constitutes a systemic violation of PI-7 and FPP-3.

### 8.4 Broken Feedback Protocol

When the Reality Alignment Loop cannot close (Observed Consequence cannot be formed within the Effect Observation Window):

**BFP-1 — LOST Classification:** The Action Projection is classified as LOST in the Open Action Register.

**BFP-2 — Escalation:** The LOST classification is escalated to the constitutional authority responsible for the domain in which the action was taken.

**BFP-3 — Alternative Observation:** The Fabric must initiate alternative Observation Projections targeting the relevant ExternalRealitySegment to attempt to determine the External State affected by the LOST action.

**BFP-4 — Conservative Assumptions:** Until the loop closes or the LOST action is resolved, the Fabric must operate under conservative assumptions about the affected ExternalRealitySegment — assuming that the action may have produced unintended effects unless observation confirms otherwise.

## PART 9 — PROJECTION FAILURE MODES

Each Projection Failure Mode constitutes a class of constitutional failure in the Projection Framework. For each, the following are defined: the failure scenario, detection mechanism, constitutional consequence, and resolution protocol.

---

### PF-1 — False Representation

**Scenario:** An internal object — Observation Record, Evidence, Belief, KnowledgeState, or ExternalReference — incorrectly represents its External Reality referent. The representation is present but wrong: it asserts an ExternalState or ExternalActor condition that does not match the actual state of External Reality.

**Detection:** Contradiction with a new, higher-confidence Observation; contradiction with Observed Consequence following an action; external audit or external actor challenge to a specific representation.

**Constitutional Consequence:** The false representation must be quarantined from operational use. All Decisions formed on the basis of the false representation must be reviewed. All Action Projections that relied on it must be assessed for consequence. The Observation Coherence Register must be updated.

**Resolution Protocol:** A new Observation Projection Lifecycle must be initiated to form a corrected representation. The false representation must be superseded, not deleted — its historical record must be preserved with a supersession note. The translation or interpretation protocol that produced the false representation must be reviewed.

---

### PF-2 — Observation Failure

**Scenario:** An External State exists and is constitutionally significant but the Fabric does not detect it. The Observation Projection Lifecycle is not initiated, fails before producing an Observation Record, or produces an Observation Record that does not capture the significant External State.

**Detection:** Subsequent action consequences that are inconsistent with the Fabric's model of the ExternalRealitySegment; external actor reporting of a state the Fabric did not record; Reality Gap Register overflow in the relevant domain.

**Constitutional Consequence:** The unobserved External State must be registered as a constitutionally acknowledged gap. The Observation Coherence Register must be updated. All decisions that assumed the absence of the unobserved state must be reviewed.

**Resolution Protocol:** New Observation Projection Lifecycle must be initiated. If the observation failure was caused by instrument limitation, instrument registration and calibration must be reviewed. If the failure was caused by coverage gap, Domain Observation Coverage (Part 10) must be assessed.

---

### PF-3 — Projection Failure

**Scenario:** The internal action fails to create the intended external effect. The CivilizationalAction passes all D4 gates and is projected through the registered channel (Stage 4 complete) but the External Effect does not match the intended ExternalState transition.

**Detection:** Observed Consequence (Stage 6) reveals no effect or contrary effect compared to the intended ExternalState transition specified in Stage 1.

**Constitutional Consequence:** The Open Action Register entry is closed as FAILED. The violated ExternalConstraint or channel failure must be diagnosed. The Understanding Model must be updated with the new knowledge of what the action could not accomplish.

**Resolution Protocol:** The ExternalConstraint that blocked the intended effect must be identified and registered. If the ExternalConstraint was known but not assessed at Stage 3 (Constraint Validation), this is additionally a PI-5 violation requiring process review. The Decision that produced the failed Action must be reviewed in light of the newly confirmed ExternalConstraint.

---

### PF-4 — External Consequence Failure

**Scenario:** The Action Projection produces External Effects that were not intended — effects beyond, contrary to, or qualitatively different from what was specified in the CivilizationalDecision. Unexpected consequences emerge through ExternalSystems, ExternalActors, or ExternalEnvironment dynamics.

**Detection:** Observed Consequence reveals effects not anticipated in the Effect Expectation established at Stage 1. Subsequent observations reveal downstream propagation of the action through ExternalSystems or ExternalActors.

**Constitutional Consequence:** The Projection Responsibility Principle applies: all resulting effects are within the accountability scope. The Consequence Coherence Register must be updated. The Understanding Model that failed to anticipate the consequences must be revised. Affected ExternalActors must be considered for remediation through subsequent Action Projections.

**Resolution Protocol:** The full scope of consequences must be observed through additional Observation Projections. The Understanding Model's account of ExternalSystem dynamics must be updated. The decision protocol that failed to anticipate these consequences must be reviewed for improvement.

---

### PF-5 — Boundary Violation

**Scenario:** Internal authority incorrectly extends externally. A projection operates as if the Fabric's constitutional authority applied directly to ExternalActors or ExternalSystems that have not accepted that authority. The Projection Boundary is treated as porous in the authority dimension.

**Detection:** External actors reject or challenge APEX authority assertions; actions predicated on unrecognized authority produce no effect or hostile response; legal or institutional challenge to APEX's claimed external authority.

**Constitutional Consequence:** PI-6 is violated. The projection that asserted unauthorized external authority must be reviewed and, where possible, corrected through subsequent projections. The Fabric's constitutional record must be updated to reflect the limits of its external authority as confirmed through external reality feedback.

**Resolution Protocol:** External authority must be recognized only where it has been externally granted or accepted. The Fabric must establish its external authority through legitimate external means — treaty, agreement, recognition — not through constitutional decree. Projections asserting unrecognized external authority must be halted pending authority establishment.

---

### PF-6 — Reality Model Drift

**Scenario:** The Fabric's internal Understanding Model diverges from External Reality over time. The divergence is not caused by a specific observation failure or false representation — it accumulates gradually as External Reality changes and the internal model is not updated at corresponding frequency.

**Detection:** Systematic divergence between expected and actual Observed Consequences; External Coherence Register scores declining across multiple dimensions; action efficacy declining without discrete failure events.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Reality Model Drift is a coherence emergency of high severity. The Observation Coherence Register and Environmental Coherence Register must be assessed. All Understanding Models in affected domains must be flagged as operating under drift conditions. Decisions based on drifted models must be suspended or flagged.

**Resolution Protocol:** An intensive Observation Projection campaign must be initiated to refresh the Fabric's epistemic picture of the affected domains. Understanding Models must be rebuilt from current Observations. Domain Observation Coverage requirements (Part 10) must be reviewed for adequacy.

---

### PF-7 — External Actor Conflict

**Scenario:** External actors reject, resist, contradict, or actively counter the Fabric's projected actions. The ExternalActors' responses constitute ExternalEvents that produce External Effects contrary to those intended by the Fabric's Action Projections.

**Detection:** Observed Consequence reveals active resistance, counter-action, or legal/institutional challenge by ExternalActors. Relationship Coherence Register shows significant misalignment between the Fabric's model of external relationships and actual external actor behavior.

**Constitutional Consequence:** The Fabric must acknowledge that ExternalActors hold independent agency that the Fabric is not authorized to override. PI-6 applies. The Relationship Coherence Register must be updated. Subsequent Action Projections in the relevant domain must account for the confirmed external actor disposition.

**Resolution Protocol:** The Fabric must seek to understand the ExternalActors' basis for conflict through Observation Projections before attempting further outbound projections in the same domain. Where the conflict arises from a legitimate external authority claim, the Fabric must recognize that authority (PI-6). Where the conflict arises from misunderstanding of APEX intent, the Fabric may pursue clarification through authorized outbound projections.

---

### PF-8 — Feedback Suppression

**Scenario:** Reality corrections are ignored. The Reality Alignment Loop is structurally present but functionally defeated: observational evidence contradicting internal models is systematically discounted, delayed, or discarded before it can produce Updated Understanding.

**Detection:** Observation Projection Lifecycles complete successfully but their products are not integrated into epistemic updates; the Observation → Updated Understanding step of the Reality Alignment Loop is systematically delayed or bypassed; External Coherence Registers degrade without corrective response.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Feedback Suppression is the most severe constitutional failure mode in the Projection Framework. It is a violation of PI-7 (Reality Correction), PI-3 (Observation Traceability), and FPP-3 (Reality Authority). The breach is not a procedural error — it is a constitutional disposition failure. It must be escalated to the highest constitutional authority.

**Resolution Protocol:** The mechanisms producing suppression must be identified and removed. All internal models that were insulated from correction must be reopened to observational update. The epistemic products formed during the suppression period must be audited against available observational evidence and revised. A post-correction audit must confirm that the Reality Alignment Loop is fully operational.

## PART 10 — CIVILIZATION INTERFACE MODEL

### 10.1 APEX's Place in Reality

APEX is not the owner of External Reality. APEX participates within reality.

This principle is not merely philosophical — it is constitutional. It governs every outbound projection, every authority claim, and every engagement with the entities and systems that constitute the civilization APEX exists to serve.

The Civilization Interface Model constitutes the specific constitutional requirements for how APEX interfaces with the distinct domains of civilization-scale External Reality.

### 10.2 Human Interaction

Humans are ExternalActors of the highest constitutional significance. APEX's engagement with individual humans bears the strongest accountability obligations under the Projection Responsibility Principle.

**Constitutional requirements:**
- All projections that affect individual humans must include a human-impact assessment
- The Fabric's model of any individual human is an ExternalReference — it is never the human
- Human dignity and autonomy are ExternalConstraints on all APEX projections affecting humans
- Consent, where applicable, is an ExternalConstraint: actions requiring human consent may not be projected without confirmed consent from the relevant ExternalActor

### 10.3 Organizations

Organizations are ExternalActors with complex internal structure, formal authority chains, and institutional memory. They may have ExternalSystem properties (collective behaviors not reducible to individual members).

**Constitutional requirements:**
- Organizational engagement must distinguish between the organization as an ExternalActor (its official positions and formal actions) and its constituent humans (each an ExternalActor with independent standing)
- Authority structures within organizations are ExternalConstraints that govern what can be agreed, committed, or projected through organizational channels
- Organizational knowledge is held through their internal processes, not by APEX representation

### 10.4 Institutions

Institutions — governments, courts, regulatory bodies, international bodies, civil society organizations — hold external authority that the Fabric must recognize (PI-6). APEX does not supersede institutional authority.

**Constitutional requirements:**
- Institutional rules are ExternalConstraints on APEX projections within their jurisdictional scope
- APEX must track its institutional environment through the Environmental Coherence Register
- Actions that conflict with institutional authority require explicit external authority recognition procedures before projection

### 10.5 External AI Systems

External AI systems are ExternalSystems with specific constitutional status: they are non-human agents capable of actions, decisions, and effects in External Reality, but they are not APEX and are not governed by APEX constitutional law.

**Constitutional requirements:**
- External AI systems must be modeled as ExternalSystems, not as extensions of APEX
- Interactions with external AI systems must pass through the full Observation and Action Projection Lifecycles — no special shortcut protocols are constitutionally permissible
- Where external AI systems claim authority or present information that would influence APEX decisions, those claims must be verified through the Observation Projection Lifecycle before acting on them
- APEX is not constitutionally responsible for the actions of external AI systems unless APEX action caused them

### 10.6 Physical Environments

Physical environments are ExternalRealitySegments with ExternalEnvironment and ExternalConstraint properties. They are governed by physical law — the most fundamental class of ExternalConstraint.

**Constitutional requirements:**
- Physical constraints must be assessed at Stage 3 of every Action Projection targeting a physical environment
- Physical environment observations must carry calibration records appropriate to the physical domain being observed
- Irreversible environmental effects must be classified as irreversible under PI-8 before projection

### 10.7 Knowledge Ecosystems

Knowledge ecosystems — scientific bodies, libraries, archives, information networks, cultural knowledge systems — are ExternalSystems that produce, maintain, and distribute epistemic products that APEX may use as observation sources.

**Constitutional requirements:**
- Knowledge ecosystem outputs may serve as observation sources through the Observation Projection Lifecycle, but must carry the full ExternalReference architecture including source, confidence, and instrument limitations
- The Fabric may not simply import knowledge ecosystem outputs as if they were internally generated URO objects — they must pass through Evidence Assessment and Epistemic Integration
- The Fabric must acknowledge the limits and biases of knowledge ecosystems as part of the Observer Capability attribute of resulting Observation Records

### 10.8 External Authority Recognition

APEX must recognize and respect external authority structures that govern the domains in which it operates.

**ERA-1 — Recognition Obligation:** Where ExternalActors, institutions, or ExternalSystems hold legitimate authority over an ExternalRealitySegment or ExternalSystem, APEX must recognize that authority as an ExternalConstraint on its projections into that domain.

**ERA-2 — Non-Usurpation:** APEX may not project itself as holding authority it does not hold externally. Internal constitutional authority does not transfer across the Projection Boundary.

**ERA-3 — Authority Inquiry:** Where the authority structure of an ExternalRealitySegment is unclear, APEX must initiate Observation Projections to determine it before acting. Acting in an authority vacuum without inquiry is a PF-5 risk.

**ERA-4 — Cooperation Principle:** Where possible, APEX must seek cooperative relationships with legitimate external authorities rather than circumventing them. Projection through rather than around legitimate authority structures is the constitutional default.
