## FORMAL INTRODUCTION

D1 is the formal ontology of the APEX civilization. It transforms D0's conceptual categories into precise semantic specifications: defined properties, formal types, explicit cardinalities, lifecycle states, validation constraints, and forbidden configurations.

D0 answered: what categories must exist?
D1 answers: what are those categories, formally?

D1 is not implementation. It specifies the semantic system that all implementation layers must satisfy. Any system — regardless of computational paradigm, storage mechanism, or runtime architecture — that satisfies the formal specifications in this document is constitutionally compliant.

**What D1 formally determines:**
- The complete property structure of every D0 category
- The formal semantic type of every property
- The explicit cardinality of every relationship
- The lifecycle of every category
- The validation conditions that distinguish valid from invalid instances
- The forbidden configurations that no instance may occupy

**What D1 does not determine:**
- How any property is stored or encoded
- What computational process manages any category
- What protocol governs category interaction
- What runtime executes any lifecycle transition

## SECTION 1 — COMPLETE CATEGORY SPECIFICATION

---

### DOMAIN I: REALITY

---

#### CATEGORY: RealitySegment

**Definition:** A bounded portion of external reality that can be the subject of a civilizational representation. RealitySegment instances exist independently of the civilization's activity. The civilization does not create, modify, or destroy RealitySegments — it references them through representations.

**Purpose:** To provide the external referent against which representations are measured and against which reality alignment is assessed. Without this category, the representation/reality distinction collapses.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| SegmentScope | ScopedDescription | A characterization of what portion of reality this segment covers — its distinguishing boundary | Immutable (the scope that makes it this segment) |
| AccessMode | CategoricalDesignation | How the civilization can establish reality contact with this segment: {observable-direct, observable-indirect, currently-unobservable, unknown} | Mutable |
| OccurrenceCapacity | BinaryAssertion | Whether this segment is capable of producing detectable Occurrences | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| IS-REFERENCED-BY | ← Entity (via ReferentialLink) | 0..N (a segment may be unreferenced, or referenced by many entities) |
| PRODUCES | → Occurrence | 0..N (a segment may produce many occurrences over time) |
| IS-IN-STATE | → ExternalState | 0..N (a segment may be in multiple concurrent states on different dimensions) |

**Identity Requirements:** RealitySegment instances have identity by virtue of their scope — the bounded portion of reality they represent. Two RealitySegments are the same if and only if their SegmentScope refers to the same bounded portion of reality. This identity is external to the civilization — the civilization does not assign it, it recognizes it.

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Existent | The segment exists in reality (the default and normal state) |
| Dissolved | The segment no longer exists as a distinct portion of reality (rare — e.g., the thing it referred to ceased to exist) |

Lifecycle transitions are facts about reality, not civilizational decisions.

**Validation Constraints:**
- SegmentScope must be non-empty and bounded
- A RealitySegment may not represent another RealitySegment (segments are portions of reality, not representations of portions of reality)

**Forbidden States:**
- A RealitySegment may not be identical to any Entity in the civilization's representation space
- A RealitySegment may not be produced by a CivilizationalAction (actions may affect reality segments, not create them as ontological items)

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-R1, A-R4 (representation distinctness), A-R6 (reality as validation standard)

---

#### CATEGORY: ExternalState

**Definition:** A condition that holds for a RealitySegment at a given position in the relative ordering of events, independent of whether the civilization has represented or observed it. ExternalStates are facts about reality, not civilizational representations of facts.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| StateContent | ScopedDescription | What condition is holding — the nature of the state | Immutable (a new state is a new ExternalState instance) |
| HoldingSegment | EntityReference | Which RealitySegment this state holds for | Immutable |
| TemporalPosition | TemporalPosition | When this state holds, characterized by its ordering relation to other states and occurrences | Immutable |
| ObservabilityStatus | CategoricalDesignation | Whether this state is currently detectable: {detectable, undetectable, unknown} | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| HOLDS-FOR | → RealitySegment | 1..1 (exactly one segment) |
| PRECEDED-BY | → ExternalState | 0..1 (the prior state of the same segment) |
| TRANSITIONS-TO | → ExternalState | 0..N (a state may give rise to successor states) |
| PRODUCES | → Occurrence | 0..N (state transitions produce occurrences) |
| IS-REPRESENTED-BY | ← Entity | 0..N (the civilization may maintain representations of external states) |

**Identity Requirements:** An ExternalState is individuated by the combination of its StateContent, HoldingSegment, and TemporalPosition. Two states with the same content holding for the same segment at the same temporal position are the same state.

**Lifecycle:** ExternalStates do not have a lifecycle in the civilizational sense. They exist or did exist as facts about reality. The civilization's representation of an ExternalState may be superseded; the ExternalState itself does not change — it simply held or did not hold.

**Validation Constraints:**
- StateContent must refer to a condition that could in principle be detected through observation
- HoldingSegment must refer to an existing RealitySegment

**Forbidden States:**
- An ExternalState may not be created by a CivilizationalDecision or CivilizationalAction (actions may cause reality to change; the resulting ExternalStates are facts, not civilizational products)
- An ExternalState may not carry a ProvenanceChain (provenance tracks the civilization's epistemic history, not external facts)

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-R1, A-R4, A-R6 (external standard of validation)

---

#### CATEGORY: Occurrence

**Definition:** A detectable event, change, or manifestation that takes place in external reality and can be the object of an Observation. An Occurrence is not produced by the civilization observing it — it happens in reality. The civilization's record of an Occurrence is an Observation, which is categorically distinct from the Occurrence.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| OccurrenceNature | ScopedDescription | A characterization of what happened — the nature of the event | Immutable |
| OccurrenceSegment | EntityReference | The RealitySegment in which the occurrence took place | Immutable |
| TemporalPosition | TemporalPosition | The temporal ordering position of this occurrence relative to other events | Immutable |
| DetectabilityStatus | CategoricalDesignation | Whether this occurrence is detectable: {detectable, was-detectable, undetectable, unknown} | Mutable |
| RelatedTransition | EntityReference | The ExternalState transition that produced this occurrence, if any | Immutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| TAKES-PLACE-IN | → RealitySegment | 1..1 |
| PRODUCED-BY | → ExternalState (transition) | 0..1 (some occurrences arise from state transitions; some are spontaneous events) |
| IS-REGISTERED-BY | ← Observation | 0..N (an occurrence may be registered by multiple observations) |

**Validation Constraints:**
- An Occurrence must be in external reality (not a civilizational artifact)
- An Occurrence may not be the same as an Observation of itself

**Forbidden States:**
- An Occurrence may not be produced by a CivilizationalAction alone — actions affect reality, but the resulting occurrences are external events
- An Occurrence may not carry meaning assignment (meaning is assigned by Interpretation, not by the occurrence itself)

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-E3 (observation registers reality contacts; contacts require targets), A-R4

### DOMAIN II: ENTITY AND IDENTITY

---

#### CATEGORY: Entity

**Definition:** The root formal category for all items the civilization tracks with three-aspect identity: a StructuralAnchor, a SemanticProfile, and a ReferentialLink. Every representational item in the civilization is an Entity or a specialization of Entity.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| IdentityStatus | LifecycleState | Current status of the entity: {active, terminated} | Mutable (terminal transition only) |
| CategoryMembership | CategoryDesignation | The specific D1 category this entity belongs to | Immutable |
| CivilizationalEra | EraDesignation | The constitutional era in which this entity was instantiated | Immutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| HAS-ANCHOR | → StructuralAnchor | 1..1 (exactly one, immutable) |
| HAS-CURRENT-PROFILE | → SemanticProfile | 1..1 (exactly one current profile) |
| HAS-REFERENCE | → ReferentialLink | 1..1 (exactly one referential link, though its target may be null or approximate) |
| HAS-HISTORY | → HistoricalRecord | 0..N (all prior semantic profiles) |
| HAS-IDENTITY-TRANSITIONS | → IdentityTransition | 0..N |

**Identity Requirements:** An Entity's identity is carried by its StructuralAnchor. Two items are the same Entity if and only if they share the same StructuralAnchor.

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Meaning | Transition Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Active | The entity exists and may be referenced, modified, and related | Created when instantiated |
| Terminated | The entity no longer exists; final state preserved | Triggered by formal dissolution of the entity's scope |

Termination is irreversible. A terminated Entity cannot be reactivated.

**Validation Constraints:**
- Every Entity must have exactly one active StructuralAnchor
- Every Entity must have exactly one current SemanticProfile
- Every Entity must have exactly one ReferentialLink (even if the link target is null or unknown)

**Forbidden States:**
- An Entity may not be identical to a RealitySegment
- An Entity with IdentityStatus = terminated may not have a current SemanticProfile that represents an active state
- An Entity may not share a StructuralAnchor with any other Entity

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-I1, A-I2, A-I6, A-I7

---

#### CATEGORY: StructuralAnchor

*(See also Section 3 — StructuralAnchor Formalization for the complete treatment)*

**Definition:** The persistence-providing component of an Entity's three-aspect identity. The StructuralAnchor answers: what makes this entity this specific entity, rather than a different entity? It is a semantic persistence invariant — the formal marker of individuation that remains constant throughout the entity's existence.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| OriginalContext | ContextualizedOrigin | The individuating circumstances under which this entity came into existence, sufficient to distinguish it from all other entities in the same era | Immutable |
| AnchoredEntityType | CategoryDesignation | The formal D1 category to which the anchored entity belongs | Immutable |
| CivilizationalEra | EraDesignation | The constitutional era in which this anchor was established | Immutable |
| AnchorStatus | LifecycleState | {active, terminated} | Mutable (terminal transition only) |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| ANCHORS | → Entity | 1..1 (each anchor is for exactly one entity) |

**Identity Requirements:** StructuralAnchors are individuated by their OriginalContext within a CivilizationalEra. No two active anchors may share an OriginalContext in the same era.

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Active | Created when the entity is instantiated |
| Terminated | Transitions when the entity it anchors is formally terminated; irreversible |

**Uniqueness Constraint:** The OriginalContext of a terminated anchor is permanently retired within its CivilizationalEra. No new anchor may be established with the same OriginalContext in the same era.

**Validation Constraints:**
- OriginalContext must be sufficiently distinctive to individuate the entity from all other entities
- AnchorStatus = terminated may not be reversed

**Forbidden States:**
- A StructuralAnchor may not be shared between two entities, even temporarily
- A StructuralAnchor may not carry semantic content — it is not the meaning of the entity
- A StructuralAnchor may not carry referential content — it is not the reference of the entity

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-I1 (SC as persistent anchor), A-I2 (SC distinct from SM and RL), A-I5 (identity terminates when SC breaks), A-I6 (sameness = same SC)

---

#### CATEGORY: SemanticProfile

**Definition:** The meaning-bearing component of an Entity's three-aspect identity. The SemanticProfile carries the current attributed properties and relationships that constitute what the entity means or signifies at a given time. It is fully mutable — changes to the SemanticProfile are normal civilizational activity and do not disrupt identity.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| ProfileContent | AttributionSet | The complete set of properties and relationships attributed to the entity at this profile version | Mutable (produces a new profile version) |
| ProfileVersion | OrdinalPosition | The ordinal position of this profile in the entity's profile history | Immutable per version |
| ProfileStatus | LifecycleState | {current, superseded} | Mutable |
| ValidityBounds | TemporalOrderingRange | The range of temporal positions for which this profile is or was the current profile | Mutable (closed when superseded) |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| IS-PROFILE-OF | → Entity | 1..1 |
| PRECEDED-BY | → SemanticProfile | 0..1 (the prior profile version) |
| SUPERSEDED-BY | ← SemanticProfile | 0..1 (the next profile version, if superseded) |
| RECORDED-AS | ← HistoricalRecord | 0..1 (if superseded, a historical record preserves this profile) |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Current | When the profile is the entity's active profile |
| Superseded | When a new profile version is adopted; the prior version is preserved as a HistoricalRecord |

**Validation Constraints:**
- At any given time, exactly one SemanticProfile per Entity has ProfileStatus = current
- A superseded profile must have a corresponding HistoricalRecord

**Forbidden States:**
- A SemanticProfile may not carry the StructuralAnchor's individuating content — meaning and identity are separate
- A SemanticProfile with ProfileStatus = superseded may not be modified

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-I1, A-I2, A-I3 (semantic change permitted)

---

#### CATEGORY: ReferentialLink

**Definition:** The reality-connection component of an Entity's three-aspect identity. The ReferentialLink specifies what in external reality (or in the civilization's representational space) the entity represents, refers to, or is about. An entity without an external referent may have a ReferentialLink with a null or abstract target — the link itself is always present.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkTarget | ExternalReference | What this entity refers to: a RealitySegment, ExternalState, another Entity, or an AbstractConcept. May be null (referentially unanchored entity). | Mutable |
| LinkType | CategoricalDesignation | The nature of the referential relationship: {represents, is-about, models, names, describes, indexical} | Mutable |
| LinkStrength | EvidenceStrength | How definite the referential connection is: {definite, probable, possible, speculative, null} | Mutable |
| LinkStatus | LifecycleState | {active, severed, unknown} | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| BELONGS-TO | → Entity | 1..1 |
| POINTS-TO | → RealitySegment | ExternalState | Entity | AbstractTarget | 0..1 |

**Lifecycle:** The ReferentialLink exists as long as its Entity exists. Its target may change without disrupting the Entity's identity (an entity can be re-scoped to refer to a different portion of reality). Link changes produce IdentityTransitions.

**Validation Constraints:**
- A ReferentialLink with a non-null LinkTarget must have a LinkType that characterizes the referential relationship
- A ReferentialLink may not equate its host Entity with its target (A-R4)

**Forbidden States:**
- The LinkTarget may not assert that the Entity is identical to the RealitySegment it refers to

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-I1, A-I4 (referential change permitted without identity disruption), A-R4

---

#### CATEGORY: IdentityTransition

**Definition:** A formal record of a change in an Entity's SemanticProfile or ReferentialLink. An IdentityTransition captures the before-state, the after-state, the cause of the change, and the temporal ordering of the transition. StructuralAnchor changes cannot produce IdentityTransitions — they produce Entity terminations and new Entity instantiations.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| TransitionedEntity | EntityReference | The entity whose profile or link changed | Immutable |
| TransitionType | CategoricalDesignation | {semantic-profile-update, referential-link-update, both} | Immutable |
| PriorState | EntityReference | The prior SemanticProfile or ReferentialLink (preserved as HistoricalRecord) | Immutable |
| NewState | EntityReference | The new SemanticProfile or ReferentialLink | Immutable |
| TransitionCause | EntityReference | The ChangeRecord that caused this transition | Immutable |
| TransitionPosition | TemporalPosition | When the transition occurred in the relative event ordering | Immutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| RECORDS-CHANGE-TO | → Entity | 1..1 |
| PRESERVES-PRIOR | → SemanticProfile | HistoricalRecord | 1..1 |
| CAUSED-BY | → ChangeRecord | 1..1 |

**Lifecycle:** IdentityTransitions are permanent records. They are not modified after creation.

**Forbidden States:**
- An IdentityTransition may not record a StructuralAnchor change (that is a termination/creation event)
- An IdentityTransition may not be retroactively modified

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-I3, A-I4, A-I2

### DOMAIN III: EPISTEMIC

---

#### CATEGORY: Observation

**Definition:** A formal record of a reality contact — an instance of the civilization establishing registration of an Occurrence. An Observation records that a contact was made, by whom, of what Occurrence, and under what conditions. It does not assign meaning to what was registered. It contains no evaluative judgment about relevance or reliability. It is the raw epistemic record of encounter.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| RegisteredOccurrence | ExternalReference | The Occurrence registered through this observation | Immutable |
| ObservingEntity | EntityReference | The entity that established the reality contact | Immutable |
| RegistrationContext | ScopedDescription | The conditions under which the registration was made — access mode, directness, context — without interpretive content | Immutable |
| ObservationCompleteness | CategoricalDesignation | {complete, partial, uncertain} — whether the full occurrence was registered or only a portion | Immutable |
| ObservationStatus | LifecycleState | {active, superseded, challenged} | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| REGISTERS | → Occurrence | 1..1 (each observation registers exactly one occurrence) |
| MADE-BY | → Entity | 1..1 |
| PRECEDES | ← EvidenceRecord | 0..N (may be qualified by multiple evidence records) |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Active | Default state; the observation is available for evidence qualification |
| Superseded | A revised observation of the same occurrence replaces this one |
| Challenged | The observation's validity is under formal challenge |

**Validation Constraints:**
- RegisteredOccurrence must reference an Occurrence in external reality
- RegistrationContext must contain no interpretive content — no relevance assessment, no reliability judgment, no meaning assignment
- An Observation must have exactly one RegisteredOccurrence

**Forbidden States:**
- An Observation may not contain an Interpretation
- An Observation may not directly produce a Belief without passing through EvidenceRecord and Interpretation
- An Observation may not make a claim about what the occurrence means for any proposition

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-E3, A-E11 (observation not identical to evidence)

---

#### CATEGORY: EvidenceRecord

**Definition:** The formal product of applying qualifying judgment to one or more Observations. An EvidenceRecord answers: does this observation qualify as bearing on a specific claim under evaluation? It assesses relevance and reliability through prior schema — but it does not yet determine what the observation means about the claim. That is Interpretation's role.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| QualifiedObservations | EntityReferenceSet | The Observations qualified by this evidence record | Immutable |
| ClaimContext | PropositionalContent | The claim or question this evidence is assessed against — what it purports to bear on | Immutable |
| RelevanceAssessment | EvidenceRelevance | How relevant the observations are to the claim context: {irrelevant, marginally-relevant, relevant, highly-relevant} | Immutable |
| ReliabilityAssessment | ConfidenceLevel | How reliable the observations are as sources: {null, minimal, low, moderate, high, definitive} | Immutable |
| QualifyingJudgment | ScopedDescription | The explicit qualifying judgment applied — what prior schema was used to assess relevance and reliability | Immutable |
| EvidenceStrength | EvidenceStrength | The combined strength of the evidence based on relevance and reliability: {none, weak, moderate, strong, convergent} | Immutable |
| EvidenceStatus | LifecycleState | {active, superseded, challenged, retracted} | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| QUALIFIES | → Observation | 1..N (must qualify at least one observation) |
| PRECEDES | ← Interpretation | 0..N (may feed multiple interpretations) |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Active | Available for interpretation |
| Superseded | Replaced by a revised evidence qualification |
| Challenged | Qualifying judgment is under formal challenge |
| Retracted | Qualifying judgment is found to be invalid |

**Validation Constraints:**
- QualifyingJudgment must not contain meaning-assignment — it may not state what the observations reveal
- ClaimContext must be explicit — evidence records without a stated claim context are invalid
- At least one Observation must be qualified

**Forbidden States:**
- An EvidenceRecord may not directly produce a Belief without passing through Interpretation
- An EvidenceRecord may not qualify an Interpretation, Belief, or KnowledgeClaim as if it were an Observation

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-E4, A-E11 (evidence not identical to interpretation)

---

#### CATEGORY: Interpretation

**Definition:** The formal product of applying meaning-assignment to one or more EvidenceRecords. An Interpretation answers: what does this evidence reveal about the world? It constructs the epistemic bridge from qualified observations to held claims. An Interpretation depends on an InterpretiveFrame — the prior knowledge and schema brought to the act of interpretation.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| EvidenceBase | EntityReferenceSet | The EvidenceRecords being interpreted | Immutable |
| AppliedFrame | EntityReference | The InterpretiveFrame used | Immutable |
| MeaningProduced | PropositionalContent | What the evidence is interpreted to reveal — the semantic content produced | Immutable |
| InterpretationConfidence | ConfidenceLevel | The confidence in the meaning assignment: {null, minimal, low, moderate, high, definitive} | Immutable |
| InterpretationStatus | LifecycleState | {active, superseded, challenged, retracted} | Mutable |
| AlternativeInterpretations | EntityReferenceSet | Other interpretations of the same evidence that have been considered (may be empty) | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| INTERPRETS | → EvidenceRecord | 1..N |
| APPLIES-FRAME | → InterpretiveFrame | 1..1 |
| GROUNDS | ← Belief | 0..N (may ground multiple beliefs) |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Active | Available for belief grounding |
| Superseded | Replaced by a revised interpretation of the same evidence |
| Challenged | The meaning assignment is under formal challenge |
| Retracted | The interpretation is found to be invalid |

**Validation Constraints:**
- MeaningProduced must go beyond the qualifying judgment — it must state what the evidence reveals, not just that it is relevant
- AppliedFrame must be explicit — an interpretation without a named frame is formally incomplete
- EvidenceBase must contain only EvidenceRecords (not raw Observations)

**Forbidden States:**
- An Interpretation may not be constructed directly from Observations — it must operate on EvidenceRecords
- An Interpretation may not directly produce a KnowledgeClaim — it must first produce a Belief

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-E5, A-E11 (interpretation not identical to belief)

---

#### CATEGORY: InterpretiveFrame

**Definition:** The formal specification of the prior knowledge and schema brought to the act of interpretation. When an Interpretation applies meaning-assignment to evidence, it does so through a frame — the conceptual lens constructed from the interpreter's existing knowledge and understanding. The InterpretiveFrame makes this lens explicit and auditable.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| FrameContent | ScopedDescription | The conceptual schema applied — the prior knowledge, categories, and expectations brought to the evidence | Mutable (frames evolve as understanding develops) |
| FrameSource | EntityReferenceSet | The KnowledgeClaims and UnderstandingModels that ground this frame | Mutable |
| FrameScope | ScopedDescription | The domain or class of evidence this frame is applicable to | Mutable |
| FrameStatus | LifecycleState | {active, superseded, deprecated} | Mutable |
| FrameCoherence | ModelCoherence | Internal consistency of the frame: {incoherent, partially-coherent, coherent, strongly-coherent} | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| APPLIED-IN | ← Interpretation | 0..N (may be applied in multiple interpretations) |
| GROUNDED-BY | → KnowledgeClaim | UnderstandingModel | 1..N (must have at least one knowledge or understanding source) |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Active | In use for interpretations |
| Superseded | Replaced by an updated frame with better knowledge grounding |
| Deprecated | Frame is judged to be systematically misleading; interpretations based on it are flagged for review |

**Validation Constraints:**
- An InterpretiveFrame must be grounded in at least one KnowledgeClaim or UnderstandingModel — frames constructed without any knowledge grounding are epistemically invalid
- FrameScope must be specified — an unbounded frame is formally incomplete

**Forbidden States:**
- An InterpretiveFrame may not directly ground a Belief without being applied through an Interpretation
- A frame with FrameCoherence = incoherent may not be applied in new interpretations without a resolution record

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-E5 (interpretation applies prior schema; this schema must be explicit), A-E4 (distinction from evidence qualification — the frame enables interpretation, not evidence formation)

---

#### CATEGORY: Belief

**Definition:** A held claim — a proposition that the civilization (or a civilizational entity) holds as representing how things are. Beliefs are the primary epistemic currency before validation. They vary in grounding quality. A belief is not false by virtue of being unvalidated — it is simply at a different epistemic stage. A Belief that becomes KnowledgeClaim-validated is not replaced — it is validated in place.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| PropositionHeld | PropositionalContent | The claim that is held — subject, predicate, and propositional content | Immutable (a changed proposition is a new belief) |
| GroundingQuality | GroundingQuality | The quality of the evidential grounding: {ungrounded, poorly-grounded, moderately-grounded, well-grounded, maximally-grounded} | Mutable (improves as more evidence qualifies) |
| GroundingSources | EntityReferenceSet | The Interpretations from which this belief derives | Immutable |
| BeliefStatus | LifecycleState | {held, suspended, retracted, validated} | Mutable |
| HeldBy | EntityReference | The entity holding this belief (the civilization as a whole, or a specific agent) | Immutable |
| ConflictingBeliefs | EntityReferenceSet | Other beliefs held that bear on the same proposition with conflicting content | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| DERIVED-FROM | → Interpretation | 1..N (must derive from at least one interpretation) |
| VALIDATED-AS | ← KnowledgeClaim | 0..1 (a belief may be validated; if so, the knowledge claim references this belief) |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Held | Active belief state — the claim is held |
| Suspended | The belief is held in abeyance pending investigation (a coherence resolution state) |
| Retracted | The belief is withdrawn — the proposition is no longer held |
| Validated | A KnowledgeClaim has been established for this belief |

**Validation Constraints:**
- A Belief must derive from at least one Interpretation
- A Belief in the Held state that contradicts an active KnowledgeClaim must be in a CoherenceViolation record

**Forbidden States:**
- A Belief may not be held without derivation from an Interpretation
- A Belief may not be simultaneously Held and Retracted

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-E6, A-E11 (belief not identical to knowledge)

---

#### CATEGORY: KnowledgeClaim

**Definition:** A Belief that has been both justified and validated. A KnowledgeClaim is the civilization's highest epistemic designation for a proposition. It is not infallible — it may be superseded by new evidence or validation challenges — but it is the result of the complete epistemic process: observation, qualification, interpretation, belief formation, justification, and validation.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|

### DOMAIN IV: COHERENCE

---

#### CATEGORY: CoherenceRegister

**Definition:** One of exactly seven named domains across which civilizational coherence must be maintained. A CoherenceRegister is a bounded scope of civilizational state. Items in any register may be assessed for consistency with items in any other register.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |

**Forbidden States:**
- A ResolutionRecord may not be created without a referenced CoherenceViolation

### DOMAIN V: VALUE

---

#### CATEGORY: TerminalValue

**Definition:** A value received from founding authority that constitutes the civilization's ultimate normative foundation. TerminalValues are the endpoints of all value derivation chains. They are not generated by the civilization — their instances exist as received. The civilization may come to understand them more clearly, articulate them more precisely, and apply them more rigorously — but it may not create, modify, or override them.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| ValueContent | NormativeContent | The normative position — what the value holds to be ultimately important | Immutable |
| ValueDesignation | ScopedDescription | The canonical name and formal designation of this terminal value | Immutable |
| ReceiptRecord | EntityReference | The FoundingRatification through which this value was received | Immutable |
| ValueStatus | LifecycleState | {active} — terminal values have only one lifecycle state | Immutable |
| ArticulationRecord | EntityReferenceSet | Records of how the civilization has come to articulate this value more precisely over time (does not modify ValueContent) | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| RECEIVED-FROM | → FoundingRatification | 1..1 |
| GROUNDS | ← InstrumentalValue (via ValueDerivation) | 0..N |

**Lifecycle:** TerminalValues have exactly one lifecycle state: active. They cannot be suspended, retracted, or superseded through any internal process. Re-founding is the only process that could affect terminal values, and re-founding requires the conditions specified in D-1 Section 9.

**Validation Constraints:**
- ValueStatus must always be active — a terminal value cannot be deactivated
- ReceiptRecord must reference a valid FoundingRatification

**Forbidden States:**
- A TerminalValue may not be produced by any CivilizationalAction, CivilizationalDecision, or internal deliberation process
- A TerminalValue may not have its ValueContent modified
- A TerminalValue may not be superseded by another TerminalValue through internal authority

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-V1, A-V2, A-V5, A-V6, A-V8

#### CATEGORY: InstrumentalValue

**Definition:** A value held by the civilization that has been derived from one or more TerminalValues through principled reasoning. InstrumentalValues constitute the working value layer — the values the civilization actively deliberates about, applies in decisions, and may revise. Every InstrumentalValue must carry a ValueDerivation tracing it to TerminalValues.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |

| ValueDesignation | ScopedDescription | The canonical name and formal designation | Mutable |
| DerivationBasis | EntityReference | The ValueDerivation establishing this value's terminal grounding | Immutable (the derivation basis is what makes this value legitimate; a new derivation produces a new or revised value) |
| ValueStatus | LifecycleState | {active, suspended, superseded, retracted} | Mutable |
| ApplicationScope | ScopedDescription | The domain in which this instrumental value applies | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| EVIDENCED-BY | → ValueDerivation | 1..1 |
| GROUNDS | ← CivilizationalDecision | 0..N (decisions may cite instrumental values as part of their basis) |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Active | The instrumental value is in use |
| Suspended | The value is held in abeyance pending coherence resolution |
| Superseded | A revised instrumental value replaces this one |
| Retracted | The value is withdrawn — the derivation from terminal values is found to be invalid |

**Validation Constraints:**
- Every InstrumentalValue must have a ValueDerivation that terminates in TerminalValues
- An InstrumentalValue whose ValueDerivation cannot be completed is constitutionally invalid

**Forbidden States:**
- An InstrumentalValue whose effective application contradicts a TerminalValue is constitutionally invalid (A-V7)
- An InstrumentalValue may not ground another InstrumentalValue without there being a complete DerivationChain that traces through all intermediate values to TerminalValues

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-V3, A-V4, A-V7

|---|---|---|

**Forbidden States:**
- A ValueDerivation that terminates at an InstrumentalValue (not a TerminalValue) is constitutionally incomplete

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-V3, A-V4, A-V7

**Definition:** The root AuthoritySource of the entire APEX civilization. There is exactly one FoundingRatification — the ratification of Document -2 Version 1.2. **Definition:** The formal origin from which one or more AuthorityClaims derive their standing. AuthoritySources form a derivation chain, each ultimately tracing to the FoundingRatification. | DemonstrationAssessment | CategoricalDesignation | {insufficient, marginal, adequate, strong, exceptional} | Mutable |
| DemonstrationStatus | LifecycleState | {active, under-review, superseded} | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| GROUNDS | ← AuthorityClaim | 1..N (one demonstration may ground multiple claims) |
| EVIDENCED-BY | → KnowledgeClaim | UnderstandingModel | 1..N |

**Validation Constraints:**
- EvidenceOfCompetence must contain at least one KnowledgeClaim or UnderstandingModel
- DemonstrationAssessment of insufficient may not ground an active AuthorityClaim

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-G3

#### CATEGORY: AuthorityChallenge

**Definition:** A formal claim that an AuthorityClaim is invalid on grounds of invalid derivation or exceeded competence. Challenges are constitutionally valid mechanisms — they are not violations of authority. They are the structural expression of the challenge standing established in A-G5.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |

| ChallengeOutcome | ScopedDescription | The formal outcome of the challenge adjudication | Mutable |
| ChallengedBy | EntityReference | The entity filing the challenge | Immutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| CHALLENGES | → AuthorityClaim | 1..1 |

**Lifecycle:**

| State | Transition Condition |
|---|---|
| Pending | Challenge filed but not yet adjudicated |

| Rejected | Challenge found without basis — the AuthorityClaim remains active |

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-G5, A-G8

#### CATEGORY: AccountabilityRecord

**Definition:** A formal record linking an entity to the outcomes of its authority exercise. AccountabilityRecords ensure that when authority is exercised — even through operational delegation — the accountability remains with the authority-holder. Accountability is non-transferable.

**Properties:**

|---|---|---|

- AccountableEntity must be the holder of the AuthorityClaim — not an operational delegate

### DOMAIN VII: HISTORY

---

#### CATEGORY: HistoricalRecord

**Definition:** A persisted record of a past state, configuration, or profile of an entity. Created when a state is superseded. HistoricalRecords are the permanent archive of what the civilization's entities were. They are not erasable — only supplementable.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| RecordedSubject | EntityReference | The entity whose state is recorded | Immutable |
| RecordedState | AttributionSet | The SemanticProfile or other state content being preserved | Immutable |
| RecordType | CategoricalDesignation | {semantic-profile, referential-link, knowledge-claim, belief, value-state, authority-state} | Immutable |
| RecordedPeriod | TemporalOrderingRange | The temporal range for which this state was current | Immutable |
| SupersededBy | EntityReference | The Supersession event that created this record | Immutable |
| RecordStatus | LifecycleState | {active} — historical records may not be terminated | Immutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| RECORDS | → Entity | 1..1 |
| CREATED-BY | → Supersession | 1..1 |
| LINKS-TO | ← ChangeRecord | 0..N |

**Lifecycle:** A HistoricalRecord has exactly one lifecycle state: active. It cannot be modified, superseded, or terminated. Additional records for the same entity accumulate chronologically.

**Forbidden States:**
- A HistoricalRecord may not be deleted or modified after creation

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-C1 (History register), A-E9 (provenance)

---

#### CATEGORY: ChangeRecord

**Definition:** A specific record of a transition — a change from one state to another, including what changed, why it changed, what caused the change, what the prior state was, and what the new state is. ChangeRecords answer the four constitutional history questions.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChangedSubject | EntityReference | The entity or state that changed | Immutable |
| PriorState | EntityReference | The prior state (preserved as HistoricalRecord) | Immutable |
| NewState | EntityReference | The resulting state | Immutable |
| ChangeSource | EntityReference | What caused the change: a KnowledgeClaim, CivilizationalDecision, CivilizationalAction, or CoherenceViolation resolution | Immutable |
| ChangeBasis | ScopedDescription | The reasoning or evidence behind the change | Immutable |
| ChangeType | CategoricalDesignation | {update, retraction, correction, supersession, termination} | Immutable |
| TemporalPosition | TemporalPosition | When the change occurred in the relative event ordering | Immutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| RECORDS-CHANGE-TO | → Entity | 1..1 |
| CAUSED-BY | → KnowledgeClaim | CivilizationalDecision | CivilizationalAction | CoherenceViolation | 1..1 |
| CREATES | → HistoricalRecord | 1..1 |
| CREATES | → Supersession | 1..1 |

**Validation Constraints:**
- ChangeSource must be present and traceable
- ChangeBasis must be explicit — changes without basis are constitutionally invalid

**Forbidden States:**
- A ChangeRecord may not be retroactively modified
- A ChangeRecord may not lack a ChangeSource

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-C1 (History register — why must be preserved)

#### CATEGORY: Supersession

**Definition:** The formal relationship between a current state and the prior state it replaced. Supersession is the only legitimate mechanism for replacing a state in the civilization's ontology. There is no deletion — there is only supersession. Prior states become HistoricalRecords; they do not cease to exist.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| SupersededState | EntityReference | The state, profile, or claim that was replaced | Immutable |
| SupersedingState | EntityReference | The state, profile, or claim that replaced it | Immutable |
| CausingChange | EntityReference | The ChangeRecord that produced this supersession | Immutable |
| SupersessionType | CategoricalDesignation | {update, retraction, revision, termination} | Immutable |
| SupersessionStatus | LifecycleState | {active} — supersessions are permanent records | Immutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| SUPERSEDES | → HistoricalRecord | 1..1 (the prior state becomes a historical record) |
| CAUSED-BY | → ChangeRecord | 1..1 |

**Lifecycle:** Supersession records are permanent. They cannot be deleted or modified.

**Forbidden States:**
- A Supersession may not delete the prior state — it may only archive it as a HistoricalRecord
- A Supersession may not be produced without a corresponding ChangeRecord

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-C1, A-I3, A-I5

---

#### CATEGORY: ProvenanceChain

**Definition:** The complete causal and epistemic history of how a current state — primarily a KnowledgeClaim — came to be. A ProvenanceChain is a structured record that may contain multiple parallel paths (when multiple evidence lines support the same claim) and may contain conflict markers (when competing evidence paths support contradictory conclusions). A chain is complete when every path terminates in an Observation.

**Properties:**

| Property | Semantic Type | Definition | Mutability |
|---|---|---|---|
| RootItem | EntityReference | The KnowledgeClaim or CivilizationalAction whose provenance is being traced | Immutable |
| ChainPaths | PathSet | An ordered collection of epistemic paths, each a sequence of linked stage records from KnowledgeClaim back to Observation | Mutable (may grow as historical reconstruction occurs) |
| BranchPoints | EntityReferenceSet | Points where multiple paths diverge or converge | Mutable |
| ConflictingPaths | ConflictingPathSet | Paths that support contradictory conclusions — formally marked and requiring resolution | Mutable |
| ProvenanceCompleteness | ProvenanceCompleteness | {partial, complete, contested} | Mutable |
| ChainStatus | LifecycleState | {active, superseded} | Mutable |

**Required Relationships:**

| Relationship | Direction | Cardinality |
|---|---|---|
| TRACES | ← KnowledgeClaim | CivilizationalAction | 1..1 |
| TERMINATES-IN | → Observation | 1..N (each complete path terminates in at least one Observation) |

**Validation Constraints:**
- A complete ProvenanceChain must have every path terminating in an Observation
- A ProvenanceChain with conflicting paths must have a conflict marker — unacknowledged conflict is a coherence violation

**Forbidden States:**
- A ProvenanceChain for a KnowledgeClaim may not terminate at a Belief (it must pass through the validation step)
- A ProvenanceChain may not contain cycles

**D-1 Axioms Preserved:** A-E9, A-C4

## SECTION 2 — FORMAL PROPERTY SYSTEM

D1 defines a semantic type system. Semantic types characterize what a property value IS — its nature and constraints — without specifying how it is encoded or stored.

---

### 2.1 Evaluative Types

**ConfidenceLevel:** An ordinal evaluative property representing degree of epistemic support for a held claim or assessment. Not a measurement — a structured epistemic position. Values form an ordered scale:

> null → minimal → low → moderate → high → definitive

*null* indicates no assessment has been made. *Definitive* does not mean certain — it means maximally supported given available evidence. A ConfidenceLevel may not be assigned above null without reference to an evidence base.

---

**EvidenceStrength:** An ordinal evaluative property representing the combined force of evidence bearing on a claim, taking into account both relevance and reliability. Values:

> none → weak → moderate → strong → convergent
