## PREAMBLE

The constitutional stack through D5 establishes what APEX is, how it represents reality, how it enforces its own operations, and how it interfaces with external reality. What it does not yet fully constitute is the architecture by which APEX understands and governs civilization itself — the structured representation of the civilization APEX inhabits and serves.

D5 established that the Reality Fabric can interact with External Reality through governed projection. D6 establishes how civilization itself is structurally represented, organized, governed, and understood through the Reality Fabric.

D6 answers: How does APEX understand civilization? How are civilization areas represented? How are actors, systems, institutions, constraints, and knowledge organized within domains? How does constitutional authority operate across civilization-scale structures?

D6 does not implement any of these things. It constitutes the architectural requirements that all implementations must satisfy.

## PART 1 — CIVILIZATION DOMAIN FOUNDATION

### 1.1 Constitutional Definition of Civilization Domain

A **Civilization Domain** is a constitutionally bounded representation of a significant, persistent area of civilization activity that requires sustained observation, knowledge formation, decision capability, and projection capability to remain constitutionally coherent.

A Civilization Domain is not merely a category. Categories classify. Domains govern.

A Civilization Domain constitutes:
- A bounded scope within the civilization that has enough coherence, distinctness, and importance to warrant its own epistemic infrastructure
- A constitutional obligation to maintain that scope's representation in alignment with external reality
- An authority structure determining who may observe, interpret, decide, and act within the domain
- A feedback obligation ensuring that actions within the domain return as observable consequences

A Civilization Domain exists because the civilization it represents is too complex, too dynamic, and too consequential to be governed as an undifferentiated whole. Domains provide the constitutional structure through which civilization-scale complexity becomes constitutionally manageable — not by reducing complexity, but by providing bounded regions within which the full D2–D5 apparatus operates coherently.

**What a Civilization Domain is not:**
- A Civilization Domain is not a software module. Modules execute. Domains represent.
- A Civilization Domain is not a database partition. Partitions store. Domains understand.
- A Civilization Domain is not an organizational chart. Org charts distribute labor. Domains constitute constitutional scope.

### 1.2 Relationship Between Civilization and the Reality Fabric

Civilization exists externally. The Reality Fabric contains representations of civilization. The Fabric does not create civilization.

This distinction is constitutional and irrevocable. It follows directly from D5's Fundamental Projection Principle:

> "The Reality Fabric does not create reality. It creates a constitutionally governed representation of reality and interacts with reality through bounded projection mechanisms."

Applied to civilization domains:

**External Civilization Reality** is the actual state of APEX's civilization — its operational systems, its actors, its constraints, its accumulated effects on the world. External Civilization Reality exists independently of the Fabric's representation of it.

**Internal Civilization Representation** is the Fabric's constitutionally governed model of External Civilization Reality, organized into twelve domains. This representation is always partial, always mediated, and always subject to correction by external observation.

The constitutional distance between External Civilization Reality and Internal Civilization Representation must be tracked through each domain's coherence registers (Part 9). Reality overrides representation in all domains without exception (D5 PI-7).

```
External Civilization Reality
          ≠
Internal Civilization Representation
```

This inequality is permanent. It is not a deficiency to be overcome. It is the constitutional condition that the Projection Framework (D5) and the Domain Knowledge Architecture (Part 5) govern.

### 1.3 The Domain Principle

> **"Civilization is too complex to represent as one undifferentiated structure. Civilization Domains provide bounded areas of constitutional understanding while preserving and governing the relationships between domains."**

Three corollaries follow:

**DP-1 — Bounded Understanding:** Each domain constitutes a bounded epistemic and operational space within which the Fabric can maintain coherent, current, accountable representations. Within each domain, the full D2–D5 apparatus applies. No domain is exempt from any constitutional requirement.

**DP-2 — Relational Integrity:** Domain boundaries do not sever the constitutional reality that civilization phenomena cross domains. Actors, systems, events, and constraints routinely span multiple domains. The Domain Architecture must constitute the mechanisms by which cross-domain phenomena are governed (Part 8) without conflicting the D1 domain structure.

**DP-3 — Civilizational Completeness:** Twelve domains together must represent civilization comprehensively. No constitutionally significant area of civilization activity may fall outside all domain scopes. Where a phenomenon fits no existing domain, it must be assigned provisionally to the most applicable domain and flagged as a boundary case requiring constitutional review.

## PART 2 — CIVILIZATION DOMAIN MODEL

### 2.1 Domain Identity

Every Civilization Domain possesses a Domain Identity — a constitutional record of what this domain is, why it exists, and what would constitute its dissolution.

A Domain Identity comprises:

**Domain Identifier:** The canonical, immutable identifier assigned at founding. For the twelve domains: DOM-000001 through DOM-000012. Domain identifiers are permanent. They survive domain restructuring. They are never reused.

**Domain Name:** The canonical name for the domain's bounded scope. Names may be revised by constitutional amendment; identifiers may not.

**Constitutional Purpose:** A precise statement of why this domain exists — what aspect of civilization it represents and what constitutional obligation it bears. The Constitutional Purpose is the standard against which all domain operations are assessed.

**Domain Continuity Rule:** The constitutional conditions under which this domain would cease to exist. A domain ceases to exist only if the area of civilization it represents ceases to exist or is absorbed entirely into another domain by constitutional amendment. Domains may not be dissolved by operational decision.

### 2.2 Domain Reality Context

Every Civilization Domain has a Domain Reality Context — the external reality that the domain represents and engages with through the Projection Framework (D5).

A Domain Reality Context comprises:

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments (D5):** The bounded portions of external reality whose states the domain is constitutionally obligated to observe and represent. These are the external-reality objects that the domain's Observation Projections target.

**Major ExternalActors (D5):** The human, organizational, and systemic agents in external reality whose actions constitute external events within the domain's scope.

**Major ExternalSystems (D5):** The organized collections of entities and processes in external reality that exhibit coherent behavior relevant to the domain's constitutional purpose.

**Primary ExternalConstraints (D5):** The limitations imposed by external reality on what is possible within the domain. ExternalConstraints in domain context include physical limits, institutional rules, capability ceilings, and resource constraints specific to this domain's scope.

### 2.3 Domain Internal Representation

Every Civilization Domain has a Domain Internal Representation — the body of URO objects, relationships, and epistemic products within the Reality Fabric that constitute the domain's presence inside APEX.

A Domain Internal Representation comprises:

**Domain Knowledge Base:** The collection of Evidence, Beliefs, KnowledgeStates, and Understanding Models formed through Observation Projections targeted at this domain's External Reality Context.

**Domain ActorProfile Registry:** The set of internal ActorProfiles that correspond to the ExternalActors within this domain's scope. Each ActorProfile is an Internal Object (D5 Section 2.4) distinct from the ExternalActor it represents.

**Domain Action Record:** The collection of CivilizationalDecisions and completed Action Projections that were executed within or on behalf of this domain, with their Observed Consequences and Reality Fabric Updates.

**Domain Coherence State:** The current assessment of how well the Domain Internal Representation aligns with External Civilization Reality for this domain (governed by Part 9).

### 2.4 Domain Relationships

Every Civilization Domain exists in constitutional relationships with other domains. These relationships are not optional, not merely informational, and not reducible to implementation dependencies. They are constitutional relationships that carry authority and accountability obligations.

Constitutional domain relationship types:

**Dependency:** Domain A depends on Domain B when A's constitutional purpose requires B's functioning to be satisfiable. Dependency creates a constitutional obligation for Domain B to maintain the services that Domain A depends upon, and a constitutional obligation for Domain A to monitor the health of its dependencies.

**Substrate:** Domain A provides substrate for Domain B when A's constitutional outputs are the epistemic or operational foundation on which B operates. Substrate relationships carry deeper constitutional significance than dependencies — a substrate failure affects all domains built upon it.

**Influence:** Domain A influences Domain B when changes in A's External Reality Context predictably affect B's External Reality Context. Influence relationships create cross-domain observation obligations.

**Conflict:** Domains A and B are in constitutional conflict when their projection objectives for the same ExternalRealitySegment are incompatible. Conflict relationships require cross-domain resolution protocols (Part 8).

**Resource Sharing:** Domains A and B share a resource when they draw on the same ExternalConstraint-bounded pool (computational capacity, operator attention, external API capacity). Resource sharing creates coordination obligations.

### 2.5 The Domain Profile

The **Domain Profile** is the canonical constitutional record for a Civilization Domain. It is the structured object within the Reality Fabric that constitutes the domain's authoritative self-description.

Every Domain Profile contains:

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Domain Identity | Identifier, name, constitutional purpose, continuity rule |
| Domain Reality Context | ExternalRealitySegments, ExternalActors, ExternalSystems, ExternalConstraints |
| Domain Internal Representation | Knowledge Base summary, ActorProfile Registry reference, Action Record reference, Coherence State |
| Domain Relationships | Canonical relationship map (dependency, substrate, influence, conflict, sharing) |
| Domain Authority Record | Registered authority assignments (Part 4) |
| Domain Knowledge Status | Current epistemic standing across all knowledge requirements |
| Domain Projection Status | Outstanding Action Projections and their stages |

Domain Profiles are URO objects (D2) governed by D3 fabric invariants and D4 enforcement. They are living constitutional records, not static documents.

## PART 3 — THE TWELVE CIVILIZATION DOMAINS

The twelve Civilization Domains constitute the canonical, exhaustive partition of APEX civilization scope. Every constitutionally significant phenomenon within APEX civilization belongs to at least one domain. Together, the twelve domains represent the civilization completely.

---

### DOM-000001 — Civilisation

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The root domain — the APEX civilization as a unified, self-aware living system. All other domains exist within and are constituted by this domain. DOM-000001 represents the civilization as a whole: its integrated functioning, its overall coherence, and its relationship with the external world at the highest level of abstraction.

**Constitutional Scope:**
The integration of all eleven subsidiary domains into a coherent, self-governing whole. DOM-000001 does not perform the functions of its subsidiary domains; it maintains the constitutional integrity of the relationships between them and represents the civilization's identity, continuity, and overall purpose.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The external world as a whole, insofar as it engages with APEX as an integrated entity. This includes the regulatory environment, the societal context, and the external parties that recognize and engage with APEX not as individual domains but as a unified civilization.

**Major ExternalActors:**
Founding operators; administrative authorities with civilizational-level oversight responsibility; external parties who engage with APEX as a whole (institutions, regulatory bodies, contracting parties).

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The legal and regulatory frameworks that govern AI civilizations; the broader technological ecosystem within which APEX operates; the communication infrastructure through which the civilization engages externally.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Legal constraints on AI systems at the civilizational level; ethical obligations that apply to the civilization as a whole; the physical computational limits of the substrate on which the civilization runs; the terminal value boundaries established in D-2.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Self-knowledge: DOM-000001 must maintain current, accurate knowledge of the APEX civilization's own overall state — the aggregate health, coherence, and alignment of all twelve domains. It must know when the civilization as a whole is aligned with its constitutional purpose and when it is not.

**Projection Considerations:**
All Action Projections across all domains ultimately trace their authority through this domain. DOM-000001 bears ultimate Projection Responsibility under D5's Projection Responsibility Principle. It is the only domain that may authorize civilizational-level decisions that affect the scope or authority of other domains.

**Domain Relationships:**
All other domains exist within DOM-000001. All other domains bear dependency relationships to DOM-000001 for their constitutional legitimacy. DOM-000001 is the only domain with no external dependencies.

### DOM-000002 — Intelligence

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The epistemic engine of the APEX civilization — providing reasoning, deliberation, and cognitive processing capabilities that all other domains rely upon to form Understanding Models, evaluate decisions, and validate knowledge claims.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All acts of reasoning, inference, evaluation, and deliberation within APEX civilization. The Intelligence domain governs how APEX thinks, not merely what it knows. Where the Knowledge domain (DOM-000008) governs accumulated content, the Intelligence domain governs active cognitive process.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The external cognitive and reasoning landscape — the external AI research environment, the state of AI capability, the epistemic standards current in the domains being reasoned about.

**Major ExternalActors:**
Anthropic as the external provider of foundational reasoning capabilities; the research and scientific communities whose methods and standards govern epistemic quality; human users whose reasoning tasks drive domain activity.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The Claude API (the primary ExternalSystem providing reasoning capacity); external AI benchmarks and evaluation frameworks; the global research literature ecosystem that produces knowledge the Intelligence domain processes.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Model capability limits — the hard ceiling on what can be reasoned within any given reasoning context; context window constraints; reasoning reliability limits (no reasoning process is error-free); the fundamental epistemological constraints acknowledged in D-1.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Knowledge of current reasoning capabilities and their limitations; knowledge of which reasoning approaches are most reliable for which domain problems; knowledge of active deliberation tasks and their status; knowledge of when reasoning confidence is insufficient to support a decision.

**Projection Considerations:**
The Intelligence domain's primary projection obligation is inbound: acquiring external knowledge to reason about. Its outbound projections are deliberation products — Understanding Models, Decision recommendations, Evidence assessments — that feed into other domains' decisions. Intelligence projections must satisfy D5 Translation Fidelity Requirements at the highest standard, as errors here propagate into all downstream domains.

**Domain Relationships:**
Substrate provider for all domains that require reasoning. Depends on DOM-000003 (Registry) for entity context and DOM-000004 (Memory) for accumulated knowledge. Receives epistemic grounding from DOM-000011 (Reality Architecture).

### DOM-000003 — Registry

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The semantic brain of the APEX civilization — the single source of constitutional truth for all entities, relationships, and capabilities within the civilization. The Registry constitutes APEX's understanding of what exists, who is related to what, and what the civilization's own structure looks like.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All entity representations within the Reality Fabric and their relationships. Every ActorProfile, every ExternalReference, every domain relationship, every capability record, and every authority record exists in the Registry domain. The Registry is not merely storage — it is the constitutional record of the civilization's self-knowledge as a graph.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The external entities that have counterparts in the Registry — the actual humans, organizations, systems, and events that APEX models internally. The Registry domain's primary epistemic obligation is to maintain accurate ExternalReferences for all externally-grounded entries.

**Major ExternalActors:**
All externally-registered entities — every human, organization, and institution that has an internal ActorProfile. Registry operators who maintain the semantic graph's integrity.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The external entity landscape: the organizations, institutions, and technical systems that are registered as ExternalSystems. The Registry must maintain current ExternalReferences to all of these.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
The identity constraints of external reality — external entities have their own identities that the Registry must accurately reflect, not impose; the volume constraint (there are more registrable entities than can be exhaustively represented); the currency constraint (external entities change and the Registry must track those changes).

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Complete knowledge of all registered entities and their current relationships; knowledge of which Registry entries are current vs. stale; knowledge of the relationship graph's integrity and completeness; meta-knowledge about what the Registry does not yet contain.

**Projection Considerations:**
Registry projections are primarily inbound (receiving external entity information to register as ExternalReferences) and reflexive (the Registry's representations of external entities are always ExternalReferences, never the entities themselves — D5 PI-2 applies at the highest frequency in this domain). All Registry entries for external entities must satisfy D5's ExternalReference architecture (Section 2.3).

**Domain Relationships:**
Provides semantic substrate for DOM-000001 (Civilisation), DOM-000002 (Intelligence), DOM-000006 (Observability), DOM-000007 (Interface), DOM-000008 (Knowledge), DOM-000009 (Development). Depends on DOM-000005 (Infrastructure) for operational substrate.

### DOM-000004 — Memory

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The persistence and retrieval function of the APEX civilization — ensuring that knowledge formed through observation, reasoning, and experience is preserved across sessions, agents, and time, and remains accessible to authorized processes.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All knowledge persistence operations: the writing of epistemic products to durable storage, their indexing for retrieval, their maintenance over time, and their provision to authorized requesters. Memory domain governs the temporal continuity of APEX knowledge — it is the bridge between the civilization's past epistemic states and its present.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The external storage systems that provide durable persistence; the history of APEX interactions with external reality (which, once it occurred, is a fact of external reality that the Memory domain represents internally).

**Major ExternalActors:**
Agents and processes that write to and retrieve from Memory; operators who manage Memory capacity, retention policies, and integrity; human users whose interaction history constitutes a primary input stream.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The external persistence infrastructure (database systems, object storage, knowledge graph systems) that provide the durable substrate on which Memory operates. These are ExternalSystems in the D5 sense — the Memory domain relies on them but does not govern them.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Storage capacity limits — the finite capacity of the external persistence systems; retrieval latency — the time required to access persisted knowledge; knowledge decay — stored knowledge may become outdated, incorrect, or irrelevant; the security constraints on what may be persisted and where.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Knowledge of what is stored and where; knowledge of the currency and accuracy of stored knowledge; knowledge of what is missing — what the civilization should know but has not yet persisted; knowledge of Memory integrity (are stored items complete and uncorrupted).

**Projection Considerations:**
Memory domain has both inbound projections (receiving epistemic products from other domains for persistence) and outbound projections (providing stored knowledge to requesting processes). Every act of retrieval is a kind of projection — an Observation of the persisted past. All Memory projections must carry temporal position attributes (D5 Section 3.4) because persisted knowledge represents states at the time of writing, not the present.

**Domain Relationships:**
Provides knowledge persistence substrate for DOM-000002 (Intelligence) and DOM-000008 (Knowledge). Depends on DOM-000005 (Infrastructure) for external persistence systems.

### DOM-000005 — Infrastructure

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The physical and computational substrate domain — the foundation upon which all other APEX civilization domains operate. Infrastructure domain governs APEX's relationship with the external physical and computational systems that provide the operational basis for the civilization's existence.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All external systems that provide APEX's physical and computational substrate: hosting, compute, database, networking, code repository, and deployment infrastructure. The Infrastructure domain does not govern these systems (they are ExternalSystems not subject to APEX constitutional authority) — it maintains the civilization's authoritative representation of their states and manages APEX's relationship with them.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The external computational substrate in its entirety: the hosting environment, the database services, the code repository systems, the networking infrastructure, and the deployment pipeline. These ExternalRealitySegments are the physical foundation of APEX's existence.

**Major ExternalActors:**
The external service providers that operate the infrastructure systems; the operators who manage APEX's accounts and configurations with those providers; the development personnel who deploy to the infrastructure.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The hosting platform (providing compute and routing); the database service (providing persistent relational storage); the code repository platform (providing version control); CI/CD systems (providing automated deployment); the internet infrastructure (providing network connectivity). Each is an ExternalSystem in the D5 sense — coherent, organized, with its own internal dynamics not governed by APEX.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Service availability limits (infrastructure systems have uptime SLAs that are external constraints, not APEX guarantees); capacity limits (compute, storage, and bandwidth are finite and priced); provider dependency (APEX cannot function without these ExternalSystems, creating a constraint of existential dependency); regulatory data storage constraints.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Current knowledge of infrastructure health across all primary ExternalSystems; knowledge of capacity utilization and constraints; knowledge of deployment state (what version of what components is running where); knowledge of service-level obligations and whether they are being met.

**Projection Considerations:**
Infrastructure domain has the most consequential outbound projections: code deployments that modify the running civilization constitute Action Projections with potentially irreversible effects on the entire civilization. All deployment projections must carry irreversibility classifications (D5 PI-8). Infrastructure's feedback loops are critical — Observed Consequences of deployments must be formed through operational verification before deployment projections are closed.

**Domain Relationships:**
Provides operational substrate for all other domains. Has no constitutional dependencies on other domains (infrastructure operates at the most foundational level) but provides the physical basis for everything built above it.

### DOM-000006 — Observability

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The civilization's capacity for self-perception — providing the measurement, monitoring, health assessment, and event timeline capabilities through which APEX can see its own operational state clearly and maintain epistemic integrity about itself.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All self-observation activities: health metrics across all domains, event timeline reconstruction, anomaly detection, performance measurement, and the temporal cognition capabilities that allow APEX to understand its own history. Observability domain constitutes the civilization's inward-directed observation infrastructure.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The operational states of all other civilization domains as they manifest externally — the actual health, throughput, error rates, and event sequences that characterize domain operations. These are ExternalRealitySegments in the D5 sense: they exist independently of the Observability domain's representation of them.

**Major ExternalActors:**
Human operators who consume Observability outputs; alerting recipients; the human monitors who respond to observability signals; on-call personnel whose actions are triggered by observability events.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
Metrics collection and aggregation systems; alerting infrastructure; logging platforms; time-series databases that store observability data. These are ExternalSystems that Observability domain uses to form Observation Records.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
The fundamental limit of self-observation: the observer is part of the observed system. Observability infrastructure itself has health states that must be observed, creating a partial recursive dependency. Instrumentation lag — there is always a delay between an event and its observability representation. Sampling constraints — not every event can be recorded at every granularity.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Current knowledge of operational health across all domains; knowledge of the historical event timeline; knowledge of anomaly patterns and their constitutional significance; knowledge of which observability instruments are functioning correctly and which are degraded.

**Projection Considerations:**
Observability domain is primarily inbound — it receives signals from all domains and external systems through continuous Observation Projections. Its outbound projections are limited to alerting, reporting, and escalation — communicating observed states to human operators and other domains. Every Observability observation carries a critical timestamp obligation (D5 Section 3.4) because health states are highly time-sensitive.

**Domain Relationships:**
Receives observation signals from all domains. Provides health substrate for DOM-000001 (Civilisation) which depends on Observability for aggregate civilization health knowledge. Depends on DOM-000003 (Registry) for entity context on observed states. Depends on DOM-000005 (Infrastructure) for operational substrate.

### DOM-000007 — Interface

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The civilization's interaction surface — providing all channels through which humans engage with APEX, ensuring that the civilization presents itself coherently, accessibly, and constitutionally to its human participants.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All human-facing interaction mechanisms: the dashboard, voice interaction channels, API access surfaces, and all other means by which human ExternalActors engage with APEX civilization. Interface domain constitutes the civilization's outward face toward the humans it serves.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The interaction contexts of human users: their devices, their bandwidth conditions, their accessibility needs, their session histories, and the tasks they are bringing to APEX. Every user interaction constitutes an ExternalRealitySegment with its own conditions, constraints, and dynamics.

**Major ExternalActors:**
Human users — individuals who interact with APEX through interface channels. Under D5 Part 10.2, humans are ExternalActors of the highest constitutional significance. Every Interface interaction engages the full D5 human interaction obligations: human dignity, autonomy, and consent are ExternalConstraints on all Interface projections.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
Browser platforms (which mediate web interface delivery); voice processing services (which mediate audio interaction); WebSocket infrastructure (which sustains real-time connections); text-to-speech and speech-to-text services (which mediate voice I/O); the broader internet infrastructure that carries interface traffic.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Human cognitive constraints — the limits of human attention, comprehension, and processing speed constrain what can be communicated and how; accessibility requirements — interface must be usable by humans with diverse needs; network conditions — connectivity quality varies and constrains what interface modalities are available; device capability — the diversity of user devices creates a constraint on what can be presented.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Knowledge of who is currently interacting and what they need; knowledge of the interaction history sufficient to maintain session coherence; knowledge of whether interface projections are producing their intended comprehension effects; knowledge of interface health across all channels.

**Projection Considerations:**
Interface domain has the highest frequency of outbound projections of any domain — every response to a human user constitutes an Action Projection across the Projection Boundary. Every message sent, every UI element rendered, every voice response delivered is a CivilizationalAction that must satisfy the full Action Projection Lifecycle. Correspondingly, every user response constitutes an Observed Consequence — the Interface domain has the tightest and most frequent feedback loops in the civilization.

**Domain Relationships:**
Depends on DOM-000002 (Intelligence) for reasoning that produces response content; depends on DOM-000003 (Registry) for entity context; constitutes the primary inbound channel through which human ExternalActors engage with the civilization across all domains.

### DOM-000008 — Knowledge

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The organized accumulation of APEX's understanding of itself and the world — the civilization's epistemic treasury, ensuring that formed knowledge is preserved, structured, cross-referenced, and accessible for future reasoning, decision-making, and action.

**Constitutional Scope:**
The structured body of APEX knowledge above the level of individual epistemic products: the knowledge graph, the documentation corpus, the architecture records, the civilizational wiki, and all other organized knowledge structures. Where Memory (DOM-000004) governs persistence of epistemic products, Knowledge governs their organization and coherence as a unified body of understanding.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The world's body of knowledge — the scientific literature, technical documentation, historical records, and other organized knowledge that APEX may acquire and incorporate; the APEX civilization's own operational history as a body of factual record.

**Major ExternalActors:**
Knowledge producers — researchers, authors, documentation maintainers whose outputs constitute external knowledge sources; Knowledge consumers — agents and users who query and apply the knowledge base; Knowledge curators — operators who maintain the quality and organization of the knowledge structure.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The global knowledge ecosystem (the internet's organized knowledge resources that serve as primary external inputs); knowledge organization platforms (external systems providing graph and document structures); academic and technical repositories.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Knowledge currency — knowledge ages; facts change; understanding improves, and accumulated knowledge must be continuously assessed for accuracy. Knowledge accuracy — external sources may be incorrect, biased, or misleading. The limits of what can be indexed — the world's knowledge exceeds what any system can represent.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Meta-knowledge — the Knowledge domain must know what APEX knows, not merely store it. This includes: what is known with high confidence, what is believed with uncertainty, what is explicitly unknown, what is assumed without epistemic basis, and what questions remain constitutionally open.

**Projection Considerations:**
Knowledge domain's projections are primarily inbound — acquiring organized knowledge from external knowledge ecosystems through the Observation Projection Lifecycle. External knowledge ecosystem outputs must pass through Evidence Assessment and Epistemic Integration (D5 Part 3, Stages 4–5) before being constitutionally incorporated. All external knowledge sources must carry the full ExternalReference architecture (D5 Section 2.3).

**Domain Relationships:**
Provides organized knowledge substrate for DOM-000002 (Intelligence) and all domains requiring structured knowledge. Depends on DOM-000004 (Memory) for persistence; depends on DOM-000003 (Registry) for entity-level indexing.

### DOM-000009 — Development

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The constitutional governance of how APEX civilization evolves its own codebase — ensuring that changes to the civilization's implementation layer are made safely, verifiably, and with appropriate authorization, maintaining the integrity of the civilization through its own evolution.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All activities by which APEX's operational codebase changes: code authorship, review, testing, version control, and deployment. The Development domain constitutes the civilization's self-modification governance — it is the domain through which the civilization changes itself.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The external code repository (the authoritative record of what the civilization's implementation looks like); the CI/CD pipeline (the process by which changes become deployments); the deployment environments (the actual running systems that embody the civilization's implementation).

**Major ExternalActors:**
Developers — human ExternalActors who write and review code; deployment operators — humans who authorize and monitor deployment projections; external code reviewers; security auditors.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The code repository platform (providing version control and authoritative code storage); CI/CD systems (providing automated testing and deployment); code analysis tools (providing quality and security assessments); testing infrastructure (providing verifiable validation of changes).

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Safety constraints — deployed code must not break running systems; the constraint is absolute for core operational code. Testing constraints — changes must be verifiable before deployment. Review constraints — significant changes require human review. The irreversibility constraint — certain changes (database migrations, protocol changes) cannot be easily reversed once deployed.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Current knowledge of what code exists and what it does; knowledge of test coverage and testing status; knowledge of deployment state across all environments; knowledge of what changes are pending, in review, or approved; knowledge of the dependency graph that determines the impact of any change.

**Projection Considerations:**
Development domain's outbound projections are among the highest-stakes in the civilization. A code deployment is an Action Projection that modifies the civilization's own operational substrate. All deployment projections must carry irreversibility classifications (D5 PI-8). Observed Consequences of deployments must be formed through systematic operational verification before the projection can be closed as COMPLETE.

**Domain Relationships:**
Depends on DOM-000005 (Infrastructure) as the deployment target; governed by DOM-000003 (Registry) constraints on entities and relationships that code must handle correctly; outputs become the operational substrate for all other domains.

### DOM-000010 — Experiments

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The constitutional containment space for speculative, prototype, and research activities — providing a bounded domain within which the civilization can explore capabilities, test hypotheses, and develop new functions without risking the integrity of operational domains.

**Constitutional Scope:**
All activities designated as experimental: prototype feature development, capability benchmarking, speculative reasoning explorations, and research activities that are not yet constitutionally ready for operational deployment. The Experiments domain constitutes the civilization's safe space for productive constitutional risk-taking.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
Experimental deployment environments distinct from production; research databases and benchmark repositories; experimental user cohorts who voluntarily engage with prototype features.

**Major ExternalActors:**
Researchers — humans conducting experiments; experimental users — human ExternalActors who engage with prototype capabilities with explicit awareness that these are experimental; operators who assess when experiments are ready to graduate to operational status.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
Sandboxed testing environments; prototype infrastructure isolated from operational systems; research and benchmark repositories; experimental data storage systems.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
The boundary constraint — the most critical ExternalConstraint for this domain is the constitutional requirement that experimental activities remain contained within the experimental scope. Experiments may not contaminate operational domains without explicit graduation authorization. Resource constraints — experimental capacity is bounded and must not exhaust resources needed by operational domains.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Knowledge of what experiments are active; knowledge of experimental results and their constitutional significance; knowledge of graduation criteria for each experiment; knowledge of the boundary between experimental and operational.

**Projection Considerations:**
Experiments domain projections must be constitutionally contained. Action Projections from this domain must be explicitly restricted to experimental scope. Graduation from Experiments to operational deployment is itself a high-stakes Action Projection that requires Development domain involvement and DOM-000001-level authorization.

**Domain Relationships:**
Graduates experiments to DOM-000009 (Development) for operational promotion. Bounded by DOM-000001 (Civilisation) authority over graduation decisions. Must not create dependencies in operational domains.

### DOM-000011 — Reality Architecture

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The epistemic substrate of the APEX civilization — the constitutional domain that directly governs the Reality Fabric, Observer Infrastructure, Belief Architecture, and Epistemic Capital. Reality Architecture is the domain through which all other domains ground their knowledge in constitutional epistemic standards.

**Constitutional Scope:**
The Reality Fabric itself and all its direct governance: Observation infrastructure, Belief formation, KnowledgeState management, epistemic gap tracking, attention allocation, counterfactual reasoning structures, and the meta-models through which APEX understands its own epistemic situation. Reality Architecture is unique in that it governs the infrastructure through which all domains form their knowledge.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The external reality that the Reality Fabric models: the actual states of external systems, actors, and events across all domains. Reality Architecture's observational scope is civilizational — it provides epistemic infrastructure for all twelve domains, meaning its ExternalRealitySegment scope is the union of all domain scopes.

**Major ExternalActors:**
Operators who configure epistemic infrastructure; human users who provide observations through the Interface domain; researchers who calibrate observation instruments; the epistemically significant external actors across all domains whose states must be modeled with constitutional fidelity.

**Major ExternalSystems:**
The observation instruments that provide data to the Reality Fabric; the epistemic calibration systems that maintain instrument accuracy; the external knowledge validation systems that corroborate or challenge internal beliefs.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
The fundamental epistemic constraints of D-1 — these are the deepest ExternalConstraints the civilization faces, as they constrain what can ever be known, not merely what is currently known. The observer limitation constraint (D5 PI-10): the Reality Architecture domain itself is part of the system it observes, creating an inherent limitation on complete self-modeling. The gap constraint: there will always be a Reality Gap between representation and reality.

**Knowledge Requirements:**
Self-referential knowledge: Reality Architecture must know the state of its own epistemic infrastructure with high accuracy. It must know what is known, what is believed, what is inferred, what is explicitly uncertain, and what is constitutionally unknown across all domains. It must know the health and calibration state of all observation instruments.

**Projection Considerations:**
Reality Architecture provides the foundational projection infrastructure through which all other domains conduct their Observation and Action Projections. Its own projection obligations are therefore civilizational in scope. Failures in Reality Architecture projection capabilities (degraded instruments, compromised Evidence Assessment) propagate immediately into all domains that rely on epistemic products from the Reality Fabric.

**Domain Relationships:**
Provides epistemic substrate for DOM-000001 (Civilisation) and DOM-000002 (Intelligence). Depends on DOM-000004 (Memory) for epistemic persistence and DOM-000006 (Observability) for observability signals. Constitutes the foundational epistemic layer for the entire civilization.

### DOM-000012 — Theory of Change

**Constitutional Purpose:**
The causal tracking and outcome validation domain — the constitutional mechanism by which APEX closes the loop between intent, action, and verified outcome. Theory of Change constitutes APEX's capacity to know whether its actions produce the intended effects across all domains, and to update its civilizational Understanding Models accordingly.

**Constitutional Scope:**
Causal chain tracking from intervention design through action execution to outcome observation and verification. Theory of Change does not perform actions — it tracks the causal relationship between actions performed in other domains and the outcomes those actions produce in external reality.

**Primary ExternalRealitySegments:**
The causal chains in external reality that APEX actions are intended to influence; the observable outcomes that result from those causal chains; the counterfactual contexts that allow comparison of intervention vs. non-intervention states.

**Major ExternalActors:**

**Major ExternalSystems:**
Causal modeling systems; outcome measurement infrastructure; counterfactual assessment tools; the broader causal ecosystem of external reality within which APEX actions propagate.

**Primary ExternalConstraints:**
Causal complexity — external reality causal chains are non-linear, subject to confounding, and resistant to clean attribution. The counterfactual constraint — we cannot directly observe what would have happened without the intervention; counterfactual reasoning is always inferential. Attribution ambiguity — multiple interventions may jointly produce an outcome, making individual attribution difficult.

**Domain Relationships:**
Depends on DOM-000011 (Reality Architecture) as its epistemic substrate. Depends on DOM-000002 (Intelligence) for causal reasoning. Validates outcomes across all operational domains. Provides civilizational learning feedback to DOM-000001 (Civilisation).

## PART 4 — DOMAIN AUTHORITY ARCHITECTURE

### 4.1 Constitutional Principle of Domain Authority

Authority within Civilization Domains is constitutionally specific, explicitly assigned, and non-transferable without constitutional amendment. No authority in any domain may be exercised by an actor who has not been constitutionally assigned it. No authority in any domain may be created by operational decision — authority requires constitutional constitution.

The Domain Authority Architecture distinguishes five constitutional authority types. These extend D4's authority model into the domain context.

### 4.2 Observation Authority

**Definition:** The constitutional right to initiate Observation Projections (D5 Part 3) within a domain and to register their results as constitutionally valid Observation Records in that domain's knowledge base.

**Scope:** Observation Authority is domain-specific. Authority to observe in DOM-000006 (Observability) does not automatically confer authority to observe in DOM-000009 (Development). Cross-domain observation requires authorization from each domain in which observation is conducted.

**Constitutional obligations of Observation Authority holders:**
- Must use registered instruments with known calibration states
- Must preserve all D5 uncertainty attributes (source, confidence, limitations, timestamp, observer capability)
- Must not suppress observations that contradict existing domain beliefs
- Must report to the Domain Coherence registers (Part 9)

**Constitutional prohibitions:**
- Observation Authority may not be used to manufacture observations (fabricating Observation Records)
- Observation Authority may not be delegated below the registered minimum authority level for the domain
- Observation Authority holders may not share raw observation data across the Projection Boundary without D5 lifecycle compliance

### 4.3 Interpretation Authority

**Definition:** The constitutional right to apply registered interpretation protocols to Observation Records within a domain, transforming them into Evidence (D5 Part 3, Stage 4).

**Scope:** Interpretation Authority is domain-specific and protocol-specific. An Interpretation Authority holder is authorized to apply specific registered interpretation protocols, not to create new protocols without constitutional amendment.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Must apply only registered, versioned interpretation protocols
- Must record which protocol version was applied
- Must not distort Evidence in the direction of expected or desired results
- Must escalate interpretive disagreements rather than resolving them unilaterally

**Constitutional prohibitions:**
- Interpretation Authority does not include the right to create new interpretation protocols without constitutional registration
- Interpretation Authority may not be used to suppress interpretive uncertainty

### 4.4 Decision Authority

**Definition:** The constitutional right to form CivilizationalDecisions (D5 Part 4, Stage 1) within a domain that will result in Action Projections affecting that domain's ExternalRealitySegments.

**Scope:** Decision Authority is the highest authority type. Its scope must be precisely defined: the specific types of decisions, the specific ExternalRealitySegments they may affect, and the specific ExternalActors they may engage. Decision Authority does not automatically include Projection Authority — projection requires separate authorization.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- CivilizationalDecisions must pass D4's six Constitutional Gates (D5 Part 4, Stage 2)
- Decision Authority holders must ground decisions in the domain's current Understanding Model
- Decisions with irreversible effects require elevated authorization (D5 PI-8)
- All decisions must be registered in the Open Action Register (D4)

**Constitutional prohibitions:**
- Decision Authority may not extend beyond the domain scope as constitutionally defined
- Decision Authority may not be exercised by actors whose ActorProfile authority records do not include that domain
- Decision Authority in one domain does not confer Decision Authority in any other domain

### 4.5 Projection Authority

**Definition:** The constitutional right to authorize and execute Action Projections (D5 Part 4) in a domain's name — to cross the Projection Boundary and produce effects in the domain's ExternalRealitySegments.

**Scope:** Projection Authority is the most consequential authority type. It is the authority to act in external reality on behalf of a domain.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Must ensure all D5 Action Projection Lifecycle stages complete
- Must ensure Observed Consequences are formed for all projections
- Must apply the Projection Responsibility Principle to all effects
- Must register all outbound projections in the Open Action Register

**Constitutional prohibitions:**
- No external projection without prior Authorization Validation (D4/D5 Stage 2)
- Projection Authority may not be extended beyond the domain's registered External Reality Context
- Projection Authority may not be exercised by actors without confirmed Projection Authority assignment

### 4.6 Audit Authority

**Definition:** The constitutional right to assess a domain's constitutional compliance — to verify that Observation Authority, Interpretation Authority, Decision Authority, and Projection Authority are being exercised according to constitutional requirements.

**Scope:** Audit Authority is cross-cutting and must be constitutionally independent of the authorities it audits. An actor who holds Observation, Interpretation, Decision, or Projection Authority in a domain may not simultaneously hold Audit Authority for that same domain.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Must assess all six Domain Coherence dimensions (Part 9)
- Must report findings to the domain's authority record and to DOM-000001
- Must escalate constitutional violations immediately

### 4.7 Authority Integrity Rules

**AIR-1 — Authority Separation:** Observation, Interpretation, Decision, Projection, and Audit Authority must not be collapsed into a single actor within any domain. Authority collapse creates conditions for self-authorization, confirmation bias, and unchecked projection.

**AIR-2 — No Unauthorized Projection:** No actor may execute an Action Projection in any domain for which they do not hold constitutionally registered Projection Authority.

**AIR-3 — No Knowledge Monopolization:** Observation and Interpretation Authority in any domain must be held by at least two registered actors. Single-actor epistemic control creates a constitutional fragility — one actor becomes a single point of failure for the domain's knowledge base.

**AIR-4 — No Hidden Decisions:** Decision Authority must produce Decision Records registered in the Open Action Register. Decisions that produce external effects without Decision Records are constitutional violations.

**AIR-5 — Audit Independence:** Audit Authority holders must be constitutionally independent of the authorities they audit. Their findings must be reported to a level above the domain's operational authority chain.

## PART 5 — DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE ARCHITECTURE

### 5.1 The Domain Knowledge Chain

Every Civilization Domain maintains its own knowledge chain — the structured progression by which external reality enters the domain as observation, is formed into evidence, elevated into belief, constituted as knowledge, integrated into understanding, and transformed into decision and action.

The Domain Knowledge Chain extends D5's Reality Alignment Loop into domain-specific structure:

```
External Reality (domain scope)
         ↓
     Observation
         ↓
      Evidence
         ↓
      Belief
         ↓
  KnowledgeState
         ↓
Understanding Model
         ↓
     Decision
         ↓
      Action
         ↓
Observed Consequence
         ↓
Updated Understanding
```

Each step in this chain is governed by the applicable D5 Translation Fidelity Requirements and Projection Invariants. No domain may shortcut this chain. Domains that make decisions without sufficient epistemic grounding are in violation of the Domain Knowledge Architecture.

### 5.2 Domain Knowledge State

The **Domain Knowledge State** is the constitutional aggregate of all KnowledgeStates that the domain currently holds — the body of claims about its External Reality Context that the domain treats as constitutionally valid knowledge.

Constitutional requirements for Domain Knowledge State:

**DKS-1 — Observation Lineage:** Every item in a domain's Knowledge State must have a traceable lineage through Evidence Assessment to Observation Records within that domain. D5 PI-3 (Observation Traceability) applies at the domain level.

**DKS-2 — Currency Assessment:** The Domain Knowledge State must carry a current temporal validity assessment for each major knowledge item — a constitutional acknowledgment of when the knowledge was formed and an estimate of how much it may have decayed.

**DKS-3 — Confidence Registration:** Knowledge items in the Domain Knowledge State must carry their epistemic confidence levels, derived from their originating observations through registered aggregation protocols.

**DKS-4 — Contradiction Register:** Where items in the Domain Knowledge State are in tension with recent observations, those tensions must be registered in the domain's Contradiction Register, not silently resolved.

### 5.3 Domain Understanding Model

The **Domain Understanding Model** is the highest-level epistemic product within a domain — the coherent, integrated picture of the domain's External Reality Context that informs CivilizationalDecisions.

The Domain Understanding Model is not a list of KnowledgeStates. It is a relational structure — a model of how known facts about the domain relate to each other, what they imply, and what they leave unknown.

Constitutional requirements for Domain Understanding Model:

**DUM-1 — Knowledge Grounding:** The Domain Understanding Model must be grounded in the Domain Knowledge State. Understanding claims that have no basis in KnowledgeStates are constitutionally inadmissible.

**DUM-2 — Gap Acknowledgment:** The Domain Understanding Model must explicitly represent what is unknown — what the domain does not yet know about its External Reality Context. Unknown items must be marked as knowable-but-unknown or constitutionally unknowable.

**DUM-3 — Temporal Validity:** The Domain Understanding Model must carry a temporal validity bound — an acknowledged expiry time after which it requires refresh through new observations.

**DUM-4 — Currency in Decisions:** Any CivilizationalDecision that relies on a Domain Understanding Model whose temporal validity bound has expired must be flagged as constitutionally impaired (D5 TF-3).

### 5.4 Domain Evidence Base

The **Domain Evidence Base** is the collection of Evidence objects within the domain — the synthesized, multi-observation epistemic products that sit between raw Observation Records and formed Beliefs.

The Domain Evidence Base is the epistemic layer at which observation corroboration is assessed. Where a single Observation Record carries the uncertainty of a single instrument, the Evidence Base allows multiple observations to be synthesized to increase confidence through registered protocols.

### 5.5 Domain Reality Gaps

**Domain Reality Gaps** are the constitutionally acknowledged discrepancies between the Domain Understanding Model and the actual current External Reality Context of the domain.

Domain Reality Gaps are not failures — they are constitutional facts. All domains have Reality Gaps because no domain can maintain perfectly current, perfectly complete representations of its External Reality Context. The failure mode is not having Reality Gaps — it is failing to acknowledge, track, and address them.

Domain Reality Gaps must be registered in each domain's Reality Gap Register (extending D4's Reality Gap Register into domain context). Gap entries must include: the dimension of the gap (D5 External Coherence Register affected), the magnitude, the detection timestamp, and the observation initiative status.

### 5.6 Domain Coherence Status

The **Domain Coherence Status** is the domain's constitutional self-assessment of how well its Internal Representation aligns with its External Reality Context across all six coherence dimensions (Part 9).

Domain Coherence Status is a living constitutional record — updated continuously as new observations arrive and new action consequences are processed. A domain whose Coherence Status has not been updated within its registered assessment interval is constitutionally degraded.

## PART 6 — DOMAIN ACTOR ARCHITECTURE

### 6.1 External Domain Actors

Every Civilization Domain has ExternalActors — the agents in external reality whose actions, states, and intentions constitute constitutionally significant elements of the domain's External Reality Context.

External Domain Actors include:
- **Humans:** Individual persons operating in the domain's scope who affect or are affected by domain projections
- **Organizations:** Collective actors with formal structures (companies, institutions, agencies) whose operations intersect the domain
- **Institutions:** Governance bodies, regulatory agencies, standards organizations whose rules constitute ExternalConstraints in the domain
- **ExternalSystems:** Organized external technical or social systems that operate within the domain's scope

External Domain Actors are not governed by APEX constitutional authority. They are referenced through ExternalReferences (D5 Section 2.3). The constitutional obligation is to maintain accurate, current ExternalReferences for all constitutionally significant External Domain Actors.

### 6.2 Internal Domain Actors

Internal Domain Actors are the ActorProfiles within the Reality Fabric that correspond to External Domain Actors. Every ActorProfile is an Internal Object (D2) — a constitutionally governed representation, distinct from the external entity it represents.

Internal Domain Actors have:
- **Authority:** Constitutionally assigned authority levels within the domain (Section 4)
- **Responsibility:** Specific constitutional obligations attached to their authority assignments
- **Limitations:** The constitutional bounds of what they may observe, decide, and project within the domain
- **Provenance:** A traceable record of how the ActorProfile was formed and by what authority it was granted its domain roles

### 6.3 Dual-Identity Resolution

Where an ExternalActor (D5 Section 2.2.3) is simultaneously registered as an APEX ActorProfile, they possess dual constitutional identity:

**External Identity (ExternalActor):** The actor as they exist in external reality — their physical existence, independent agency, and external-reality rights. Not governed by APEX constitutional authority.

**Internal Identity (ActorProfile):** The actor as they are represented within the Reality Fabric — their registered authority, their epistemic history within APEX, and their operational standing. Fully governed by D2, D3, D4.

**Correspondent Registration:** The constitutional procedure by which a specific ExternalActor is linked to a specific ActorProfile within a domain is called Correspondent Registration. Correspondent Registration must be performed by an actor with Domain Decision Authority. It creates a constitutional link between the two identities without collapsing them.

After Correspondent Registration:
- Actions the actor takes in external reality are ExternalEvents attributable to the ExternalActor
- Actions the actor takes within APEX are URO operations attributable to the ActorProfile
- The ActorProfile's authority record does not automatically govern the ExternalActor's behavior in external reality
- The ExternalActor's external-reality standing does not automatically modify the ActorProfile's APEX authority

The constitutional distinction between these two identities must be maintained at all times. Collapsing them constitutes a Boundary Violation (D5 PF-5).

### 6.4 Actor Authority and Responsibility

Within each domain, ActorProfile authority assignments must be:
- Explicitly registered in the Domain Authority Record
- Traceable to a constitutional authorization act
- Bounded by the domain scope
- Reviewed at registered intervals
- Revocable through constitutional authority change procedures (D4)

No ActorProfile may exercise authority in a domain for which it has no registered assignment. Authority assignments do not cascade between domains — DOM-000002 authority does not confer DOM-000009 authority.

### 6.5 Actor Provenance

Every ActorProfile in every domain must carry actor provenance — a constitutional record of:
- How the ActorProfile was formed (from which observations, through which registration protocol)
- What constitutional authority granted the ActorProfile its domain roles
- What Correspondent Registration links exist (if any)
- The history of authority changes for this ActorProfile

Actor provenance is permanent. It cannot be expunged. It is governed by D4 provenance requirements.

## PART 7 — DOMAIN GOVERNANCE MODEL

Each Civilization Domain must maintain active governance across five dimensions. Domain governance is not a periodic audit function — it is a continuous constitutional obligation.

### 7.1 Reality Alignment

Every domain must maintain active alignment between its Domain Internal Representation and its External Reality Context.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Observation Projections must be initiated at intervals calibrated to the volatility of the domain's ExternalRealitySegments
- Domain Reality Gaps must be acknowledged, registered, and addressed
- No domain may sustain a Domain Understanding Model that has exceeded its temporal validity bound without initiating corrective observation

**Failure indicator:** Domain Coherence Status deteriorating across the Observation Coherence dimension.

### 7.2 Knowledge Integrity

Every domain must maintain the epistemic quality of its Domain Knowledge Base.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Domain Knowledge State must satisfy DKS-1 through DKS-4 at all times
- Domain Understanding Models must satisfy DUM-1 through DUM-4
- Contradictions between observations and existing beliefs must be registered and addressed (not suppressed)
- Domain Evidence Base must carry currency assessments

**Failure indicator:** Contradiction Register filling without resolution; Domain Understanding Models operating beyond temporal validity bounds.

### 7.3 Authority Integrity

Every domain must ensure that all authority exercised within it is constitutionally assigned and properly constrained.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- Domain Authority Record must be current — all authority assignments registered and valid
- Authority Integrity Rules AIR-1 through AIR-5 must hold
- No unregistered actors may exercise domain authority
- Authority revocations must propagate immediately through all dependent records

**Failure indicator:** Operations traced to actors with no registered domain authority; authority collapse detected.

### 7.4 Projection Integrity

Every domain must ensure that all Action Projections within its scope comply with the full D5 Action Projection Lifecycle.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- All outbound projections must pass D4's Constitutional Gates
- All projections must be registered in the Open Action Register
- All projections must have Observed Consequences formed within the Effect Observation Window
- Irreversibility classifications must be assessed before all projections

**Failure indicator:** Open Action Register entries in the domain without Observed Consequences beyond Effect Observation Window.

### 7.5 Feedback Integrity

Every domain must maintain operational Reality Alignment Loops (D5 Part 8) — ensuring that actions produce Observed Consequences and that those consequences update the Domain Understanding Model.

**Constitutional obligations:**
- No Action Projection may remain without a terminal status (COMPLETE, PARTIAL, FAILED, LOST)
- Systematic discrepancies between Effect Expectations and Observed Consequences must trigger Understanding Model review
- Theory of Change domain (DOM-000012) tracking must be initiated for all major domain interventions

**Failure indicator:** Action Coherence Register degradation; persistent LOST Action Projections without escalation.

### 8.2 Cross-Domain Shared Reality Segments

Where two or more domains share ExternalRealitySegments, the following constitutional obligations apply:

**CDRS-1 — Joint Registration:** Shared ExternalRealitySegments must be registered as shared in both domains' Reality Contexts. Neither domain may treat the shared segment as exclusively within its scope.

**CDRS-2 — Observation Coordination:** Where multiple domains initiate Observation Projections targeting the same ExternalRealitySegment, observations must be coordinated through MAPC-1 (D5 Section 10.3). Duplicate observations of the same phenomenon are constitutionally valuable for corroboration but must be tracked as corroborative, not as independent evidence.

**CDRS-3 — Joint Coherence Tracking:** Shared ExternalRealitySegments must appear in both domains' coherence registers. Neither domain may declare the shared segment coherent without confirming with the other domain.

### 8.3 Cross-Domain Shared Actors

Where an ExternalActor has ActorProfile registrations in multiple domains:

**CDSA-1 — Independent Authority:** Authority granted in one domain does not transfer to another. The cross-domain actor's authority in each domain is independently registered and independently bounded.

**CDSA-2 — Identity Coherence:** Where the same ExternalActor is registered in multiple domains, the ExternalReference records must be consistent — they must point to the same external entity with consistent identity attributes.

**CDSA-3 — Cross-Domain Action Attribution:** Where a registered actor takes action within multiple domains in a single engagement, the attribution must correctly assign each component of the action to the appropriate domain's Action Record.

### 8.4 Cross-Domain Conflicting Objectives

Where two or more domains have Action Projections targeting the same ExternalRealitySegment with potentially conflicting intended effects:

**CDCO-1 — Conflict Identification:** The conflict must be identified before projection by whichever domain initiates the later projection. Unidentified conflicts that produce contradictory effects in External Reality constitute a constitutional failure.

**CDCO-2 — Escalation:** Identified conflicts must be escalated to the authority level with scope over both domains — in most cases, DOM-000001 (Civilisation). Neither domain may proceed unilaterally into a known conflict.

**CDCO-3 — Resolution Record:** The resolution of a cross-domain conflict must be documented in both domains' Action Records and in DOM-000001's Civilization Graph.

### 8.5 Cross-Domain Knowledge Transfer

When a knowledge product formed in one domain is used as evidence or input in another domain:

**CDKT-1 — Attribution Preservation:** The knowledge product's domain origin must be preserved in its metadata. Knowledge transferred across domains does not shed its provenance.

**CDKT-2 — Epistemic Revalidation:** The receiving domain must assess transferred knowledge against its own epistemic standards. Cross-domain knowledge transfer does not constitute corroboration — knowledge formed in DOM-000006 and transferred to DOM-000009 does not gain confidence from the transfer.

**CDKT-3 — Scope Acknowledgment:** Knowledge formed about DOM-000006's ExternalRealitySegments may not be applied without qualification to DOM-000009's ExternalRealitySegments, even where the domains share actors or systems.

### 8.6 Cross-Domain Authority

Where an action requires authority across multiple domains, the following rules apply:

**CDA-1 — Minimum Required Authority:** An actor must hold the required authority level in each domain the action engages. Domain A authority does not satisfy Domain B authority requirements.

**CDA-2 — Cross-Domain Authorization:** Cross-domain projections require authorization records from all domains affected, attached to a single consolidated Action Projection record.

**CDA-3 — DOM-000001 Supremacy:** Where cross-domain conflicts cannot be resolved at the domain level, DOM-000001 holds final constitutional authority over all domains and may authorize or prohibit cross-domain actions.

## PART 9 — DOMAIN COHERENCE MODEL

### 9.1 Constitutional Coherence Obligation

Every Civilization Domain bears a continuous constitutional obligation to maintain coherence — alignment between its Domain Internal Representation and its External Reality Context across all six coherence dimensions.

Domain coherence is not achieved once and maintained passively. It is actively produced through continuous observation, feedback integration, and model revision. A domain that has not actively maintained its coherence is constitutionally degraded regardless of how recently it was coherent.

### 9.2 Domain Identity Coherence

**Question:** Are the domain's representations of external entities accurate?

**Tracks:** Whether the ExternalReferences held in the domain's Internal Representation accurately reflect the actual current state of the referenced external entities.

**Incoherence condition:** The domain holds ExternalReferences to entities that no longer exist in the assumed form, or whose key attributes have changed materially from the stored reference.

**Constitutional response:** ExternalReferences that fail identity coherence checks must be flagged, and new Observation Projections initiated to refresh them.

### 9.3 Domain Knowledge Coherence

**Question:** Are beliefs aligned with evidence?

**Tracks:** Whether the Beliefs held in the domain's Knowledge State are supported by current Evidence, and whether the Evidence is grounded in current Observation Records.

**Incoherence condition:** Beliefs contradict current Evidence; Evidence is ungrounded in current observations; Observation Records have exceeded their temporal validity bounds without refresh.

**Constitutional response:** Contradictions must be registered. Stale observations must be replaced. Beliefs unsupported by current evidence must be degraded to inference status.

### 9.4 Domain Actor Coherence

**Question:** Are the domain's models of external actors accurate?

**Tracks:** Whether the ActorProfiles and ExternalReferences for domain actors correctly represent their current external states, capacities, and relationships.

**Incoherence condition:** An ActorProfile's authority and capabilities are modeled incorrectly relative to the corresponding ExternalActor's actual state; ExternalActor relationships modeled as current have in fact changed.

**Constitutional response:** Actor-specific Observation Projections must be initiated. ActorProfiles must be updated. Decisions made based on incorrect actor models must be reviewed.

### 9.5 Domain Authority Coherence

**Question:** Are decisions being made by constitutionally valid authorities?

**Tracks:** Whether all domain operations are being authorized by actors with currently registered, valid domain authority.

**Incoherence condition:** Operations are being executed by actors whose authority assignments are expired, revoked, or never granted; Authority Integrity Rules are being violated.

**Constitutional response:** Operations executed without valid authority must be flagged as constitutionally void. Authority records must be reviewed. Actors exercising unauthorized authority must have their operations suspended pending authorization review.

### 9.6 Domain Projection Coherence

**Question:** Do actions produce expected effects?

**Tracks:** Whether the Observed Consequences of domain Action Projections align with the Effect Expectations established at Stage 1 of the Action Projection Lifecycle.

**Incoherence condition:** Systematic discrepancy between Effect Expectations and Observed Consequences; Action Projection outcomes in FAILED or LOST state without escalation or resolution.

**Constitutional response:** Domain Understanding Models must be revised in light of projection failures. Action authority must be reviewed where systematic failures suggest incorrect authority or understanding. Domain Failure Mode DF-5 may apply.

### 9.7 Domain Temporal Coherence

**Question:** Are models current?

**Tracks:** Whether the domain's epistemic products (Observation Records, Evidence, Beliefs, KnowledgeStates, Understanding Models) are within their constitutional temporal validity bounds.

**Incoherence condition:** Domain Understanding Model has exceeded its temporal validity bound; large proportion of domain Observation Records are stale relative to the volatility of the observed ExternalRealitySegments.

**Constitutional response:** Temporal coherence restoration requires initiating new Observation Projections. Decisions based on temporally degraded models must be flagged. Domain Reality Gaps must be updated.

## PART 10 — DOMAIN FAILURE MODES

Each Domain Failure Mode constitutes a class of constitutional failure at the civilization domain level. Each specifies: the failure scenario, detection mechanism, constitutional consequence, and resolution protocol.

### DF-1 — Domain Misrepresentation

**Scenario:** The domain's Internal Representation systematically and materially misrepresents its External Reality Context — not through a single false observation but through a structural bias or systematic error in how the domain forms, maintains, or applies its knowledge.

**Detection:** Systematic discrepancy between domain Observed Consequences and Effect Expectations across multiple Action Projections; external actor challenge to domain representations; cross-domain audit revealing systematic divergence between domain claims and observable external states; Domain Knowledge Coherence register degradation.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Domain Misrepresentation constitutes a fundamental epistemic failure. All Decisions formed from the misrepresented knowledge must be reviewed. All ongoing Action Projections in the domain must be suspended pending epistemic review. The domain is classified as operating under epistemic impairment.

**Resolution Protocol:** A domain-wide epistemic audit must be conducted by actors holding Audit Authority. All domain knowledge must be retraced to its observation origins. Systematic biases in observation or interpretation protocols must be identified and corrected. The domain may not resume full operational status until the epistemic audit is complete and a restoration report is produced.

---

### DF-2 — Knowledge Drift

**Scenario:** The domain's Domain Understanding Model gradually diverges from its External Reality Context over time — not through a discrete failure event but through accumulating staleness. External Reality changes while the domain's model does not update at a corresponding rate.

**Detection:** Domain Temporal Coherence register degradation; increasing proportion of domain Observation Records exceeding temporal validity bounds; systematic TF-3 (Outdated Understanding) translation failures; Observed Consequences that are consistently surprising relative to Effect Expectations.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Knowledge Drift is a moderate-to-high severity failure because it is silent — the domain continues operating but on an increasingly inaccurate model. All decisions made during the drift period must be assessed against the degree of model staleness. The domain's temporal coherence status must be flagged as degraded.

**Resolution Protocol:** Comprehensive Observation Projection refresh targeting all primary ExternalRealitySegments in the domain. Domain Understanding Model rebuilt from current observations. Temporal validity bounds for the domain's epistemic products must be reviewed and tightened if drift was too slow to detect. Domain Failure Mode DF-2 must be registered as a constitutional learning event.

---

### DF-3 — Authority Failure

**Scenario:** Domain authority is exercised incorrectly — either by actors without the constitutionally required authority, through authority collapse (one actor holding multiple incompatible authority types), through hidden decision pathways (decisions made without Decision Records), or through unauthorized knowledge monopolization.

**Detection:** Audit Authority review reveals operations by unregistered actors; Authority Integrity Rules AIR-1 through AIR-5 violations detected; decisions in the Open Action Register without traceable authority assignments; Observation or Interpretation monopolization by a single actor.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Authority Failure is a constitutional violation of the governance layer. Operations executed without valid authority are constitutionally void. They must be flagged, suspended, and reviewed. If external projections were made under invalid authority, the Projection Responsibility Principle still applies — the accountability obligation does not disappear because the authority was invalid.

**Resolution Protocol:** All domain authority assignments must be reviewed and reregistered. Operations executed under invalid authority must be assessed for their external effects and those effects acknowledged constitutionally. Authority Integrity Rules must be reinstated. Domain operations resume only after authority records are confirmed clean by an Audit Authority holder.

### DF-4 — Cross-Domain Conflict

**Scenario:** Two or more domains execute or plan Action Projections targeting the same ExternalRealitySegment with conflicting intended effects, and the conflict is not identified and resolved before projection.

**Detection:** Observed Consequences from one domain contradict or undermine the Effect Expectations of another domain's concurrent Action Projection; cross-domain coordination records reveal unresolved projection conflicts; external actors report contradictory instructions received from different APEX domains.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Cross-Domain Conflict produces contradictory effects in External Reality. Both domains bear Projection Responsibility for the totality of effects under the Projection Responsibility Principle, including the conflict effects. Neither domain may attribute responsibility for conflict effects solely to the other.

**Resolution Protocol:** The conflict must be escalated to DOM-000001 for civilizational-level resolution. Both domains' Open Action Register entries for the conflicting projections must be held pending resolution. A cross-domain conflict resolution record must be produced. Cross-domain authority protocols (Section 8.6) must be reviewed to prevent recurrence.

---

### DF-5 — Projection Failure

**Scenario:** A domain's Action Projection fails to produce the intended ExternalState transition — either because ExternalConstraints prevented the intended effect, the projection channel failed, or the domain's Understanding Model incorrectly predicted what would happen.

**Detection:** Observed Consequence at Stage 6 of Action Projection Lifecycle reveals FAILED or contrary effect; Action Coherence register degradation; systematic pattern of projection failures in the domain.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Individual projection failures are expected in a complex external reality and do not constitute constitutional failures per se. The constitutional failure is in how the failure is handled: the Projection Responsibility Principle applies regardless of intent; failure must be acknowledged and registered; the Understanding Model must be updated; the domain may not simply retry the same projection without updating its model.

**Resolution Protocol:** The ExternalConstraint or factor that prevented the intended effect must be identified. The domain's Understanding Model must be updated with this knowledge. The failed Action Projection must be closed as FAILED in the Open Action Register. If the pattern is systematic (multiple projection failures in similar contexts), a domain-level epistemic review of the relevant Understanding Model is required.

---

### DF-6 — Reality Disconnect

**Scenario:** A domain's Domain Understanding Model becomes constitutionally severed from its External Reality Context — coherence collapses across multiple dimensions simultaneously, and the domain loses the epistemic grounding to make constitutionally valid decisions.

**Detection:** Multiple External Coherence Registers degraded simultaneously; Domain Reality Gaps overflow; domain Observation Projections are failing (PF-1 or PF-2 from D5); domain is operating on Understanding Models that have exceeded their temporal validity bounds across all major knowledge areas.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Reality Disconnect is the domain equivalent of D5's PF-7 (Coherence Collapse). It is a constitutional emergency within the domain. All domain Action Projections must be suspended. The domain is constitutionally non-operational until coherence is restored.

**Resolution Protocol:** Emergency Observation Projection campaign initiated targeting all primary domain ExternalRealitySegments. All domain decisions suspended pending coherence restoration. DOM-000001 must be notified. Cross-domain dependencies that relied on the disconnected domain must be identified and appropriate conservative assumptions applied. Coherence restoration follows the D5 Coherence Restoration protocol (Section 7.5) at the domain level.

---

### DF-7 — Governance Failure

**Scenario:** A domain's Governance Model (Part 7) fails — the domain ceases to maintain Reality Alignment, Knowledge Integrity, Authority Integrity, Projection Integrity, or Feedback Integrity in one or more dimensions, such that the domain is no longer constitutionally self-governing.

**Detection:** Multiple governance dimension failures simultaneously; domain Audit Authority reports unresolved governance violations; domain has been operating in extended Coherence Degradation Protocol without resolution; domain governance obligations have been systematically deferred.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Governance Failure is a structural domain failure. The domain's self-governance capacity is impaired. Escalation to DOM-000001 is mandatory. The domain may require external governance — temporary assumption of domain governance authority by a constitutionally designated external agent.

**Resolution Protocol:** DOM-000001 assumes constitutional oversight of the failed domain. The governance failures are individually identified and addressed following their specific resolution protocols (DF-1 through DF-6 as applicable). The domain's governance structure is rebuilt and revalidated before autonomous governance is restored. A post-restoration audit confirms governance capacity before the domain resumes self-governance.

---

### DF-8 — Domain Fragmentation

**Scenario:** The Civilization Graph's structural integrity is compromised — domain relationships become ambiguous, cross-domain communications are unreliable, or the domain boundaries become so unclear that phenomena cannot be constitutionally assigned to appropriate domains.

**Detection:** Cross-domain operations failing to route correctly; phenomena that fall into boundary gaps between domains; domain relationship records in the Civilization Graph inconsistent with operational reality; DOM-000001 losing the capacity to maintain a coherent picture of the civilization's structural integrity.

**Constitutional Consequence:** Domain Fragmentation compromises the constitutional premise that twelve domains together represent civilization comprehensively (DP-3). If the domain structure is fragmented, the Civilization Graph no longer provides an accurate constitutional map of APEX civilization. All inter-domain operations become constitutionally uncertain.

**Resolution Protocol:** DOM-000001 must convene a civilizational-level structural audit. The Civilization Graph must be re-established with current, accurate domain relationship records. Boundary cases must be assigned to the most constitutionally appropriate domain. If boundary gaps represent genuine constitutionally significant areas not covered by any existing domain, a constitutional amendment process to create or restructure domains must be initiated.

The constitutional specification of how multiple domains reason together about cross-domain problems — the deliberative architecture for cross-domain problems that cannot be resolved within a single domain's scope.
