## PREAMBLE

D0 is the first architectural layer of the APEX constitutional hierarchy. It is constitutional architecture, not implementation. It answers one question:

> What fundamental categories and relationships must exist for a civilization to represent reality, identity, knowledge, values, governance, and action while remaining fully compliant with D-1?

D0 defines what must exist. It does not define how anything is stored, processed, communicated, or executed. Every category defined here must justify its existence by reference to a D-1 axiom it satisfies. Every category absent here must be absent because it belongs to a lower layer.

## SECTION 1 — PURPOSE AND SCOPE OF D0

### 1.1 Purpose

D0 is the ontological constitution of the APEX civilization. It establishes the fundamental semantic categories — the types of things that must exist for the civilization to represent reality, validate knowledge, maintain identity, enforce coherence, protect values, derive authority, trace action, and preserve history.

D0 does not design software. D0 does not define databases. D0 does not create protocols. D0 names what must exist in the civilization's conceptual space. How those things are realized is the responsibility of D1 and below.

### 1.2 What D0 Decides

D0 answers: what fundamental categories and relationships must exist?

It defines:
- Entity categories — the types of things the civilization tracks
- Relationship categories — the types of connections between entities
- Property categories — what attributes entities must carry
- Ontological constraints — what structural requirements entities must satisfy

### 1.3 What D0 Does Not Decide

D0 does not decide:
- What software objects implement these categories
- How these categories are stored
- What protocols govern category interaction
- What algorithms operate on these categories
- What governance procedures manage these categories

Those decisions belong to D1 (formal ontology with type system and constraints), D2 (universal representational object specification), and below.

### 1.4 Derivation Basis

D0 derives from D-1 by two mechanisms:

**Grounding:** D-1 axioms establish what the civilization must be capable of representing. D0 names the categories required for that representational capacity.

**Constraint:** D-1 axioms foreclose certain D0 designs. D0 must not define categories that collapse the reality/representation distinction, generate terminal values, hold underived authority, conflate epistemic stages, or disrupt three-aspect identity.

### 1.5 Scope Statement

D0 covers eight ontological domains:

1. Reality Domain — what the civilization represents reality as containing
2. Entity and Identity Domain — how things persist and change
3. Epistemic Domain — how the civilization produces knowledge
4. Coherence Domain — how the civilization maintains structural consistency
5. Value Domain — how the civilization represents what matters
6. Authority Domain — how the civilization represents legitimacy
7. Action Domain — how the civilization represents what it does
8. History Domain — how the civilization preserves its past

## SECTION 2 — ONTOLOGY DESIGN PRINCIPLES

### Principle 1 — Minimum Sufficiency

D0 includes every category required to satisfy D-1. It includes no category that D-1 does not require. A category that is convenient but not constitutionally required belongs in D1 or lower.

### Principle 2 — The External/Represented Distinction

The ontology is organized around a fundamental two-fold division:

**External:** Things that exist independently of the civilization. The civilization does not create them, own them, or constitute them. The civilization references them. Only the Reality Domain contains external things.

**Represented:** Everything the civilization tracks, produces, holds, or records. All represented things have three-aspect identity (StructuralAnchor, SemanticProfile, ReferentialLink). All represented things may be wrong, incomplete, or outdated.

This division must never collapse. A design that makes an external thing and its representation the same category violates A-R4.

### Principle 3 — Every Category Justifies Its Existence Upward

Every category in this document must trace its existence to one or more D-1 axioms. A category that cannot be so traced does not belong in D0.

### Principle 4 — Relationships Are First-Class

The connections between categories are as constitutionally important as the categories themselves. D0 defines relationships formally, not as implicit consequences. A relationship that is required by D-1 must be explicitly named and constrained.

### Principle 5 — No Category Is a Primitive Authority Holder

No D0 category holds authority by virtue of its type membership. Authority is always a property of a specific AuthorityClaim with a derivation, not a property of category membership. Being an "IntelligenceEntity" or "AgentEntity" does not confer authority.

### Principle 6 — No Category Generates Terminal Values

No D0 category has the capacity to produce instances of TerminalValue. TerminalValue instances exist in D0 as received from founding authority. The ontology provides no mechanism by which any civilizational process can create a new TerminalValue.

### Principle 7 — Epistemic Chain Ordering Is Structural

The epistemic chain (Observation → Evidence → Interpretation → Belief → Knowledge → Understanding → Decision → Action) is represented as a structural ordering constraint in the ontology, not as a temporal sequence or workflow. The ordering defines what can precede what — not what happens when.

### Principle 8 — History Is Not Erasable

Every category subject to change must have an associated ChangeRecord structure. The ontology provides no mechanism for erasing a ChangeRecord. Supersession is the only legitimate transition for replacing a historical record.

## SECTION 3 — FUNDAMENTAL ENTITY CATEGORIES

The civilization's ontology contains thirty-five fundamental categories organized across eight domains.

*Notation: D-1 axiom citations appear in brackets.*

---

### DOMAIN I: REALITY

*These categories represent what exists in external reality. Their instances exist independently of the civilization's activity.*

---

**Category: RealitySegment**

Name: RealitySegment

Definition: A bounded portion of external reality that can be the subject of a civilizational representation. RealitySegments are not created, owned, or constituted by the civilization. They exist regardless of whether the civilization represents them.

Purpose: To provide the external referent against which all representations are measured. Without this category, the reality/representation distinction collapses — representations would have nothing to be about that is distinct from themselves.

Required relationships:
- IS-REFERENCED-BY → ReferentialLink (a representation may reference a RealitySegment through a ReferentialLink)
- IS-CONTACTED-THROUGH → Occurrence (a RealitySegment may produce occurrences that can be observed)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-R1 (independence), A-R4 (representation distinctness), A-R6 (reality as validation reference)

---

**Category: Occurrence**

Name: Occurrence

Definition: A detectable event, change, or manifestation that takes place in external reality and can be the object of an Observation. An Occurrence is not produced by the civilization — it happens in reality. The civilization's record of an Occurrence is an Observation, which is distinct from the Occurrence itself.

Purpose: To provide the external object of Observations. Without this category, the observation stage of the epistemic chain has nothing to register — observations would be records of nothing.

Required relationships:
- TAKES-PLACE-IN → RealitySegment (an occurrence takes place within a reality segment)
- IS-REGISTERED-BY → Observation (an occurrence may be registered through observation)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E3 (observation as registration of reality contact), A-R4 (representation distinctness)

---

**Category: ExternalState**

Name: ExternalState

Definition: A condition that holds for a RealitySegment at a given time, independent of whether the civilization has represented or observed it. ExternalStates are not produced by the civilization. They are the actual conditions of reality against which the civilization's StateRepresentations are measured.

Purpose: To provide the standard against which the civilization's representations of states are validated. Without ExternalState as a distinct category, the validation direction in A-R6 cannot be represented — there would be no external state to measure a representation against.

Required relationships:
- HOLDS-FOR → RealitySegment (a state holds for a segment of reality)
- TRANSITIONS-TO → ExternalState (states change; the transition may produce an Occurrence)
- IS-REPRESENTED-BY → StateRepresentation (the civilization may maintain a representation of an external state)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-R6 (reality as validation reference), A-R8 (alignment obligation is non-closing)

---

### DOMAIN II: ENTITY AND IDENTITY

*These categories represent things the civilization tracks over time with three-aspect identity. All categories in this section and all subsequent sections are Represented entities — civilization-produced or civilization-tracked things.*

---

**Category: Entity**

Name: Entity

Definition: The root category for all things the civilization tracks over time. An Entity is any thing that persists with three-aspect identity: a StructuralAnchor, a SemanticProfile, and a ReferentialLink. Every category below this section is a specialization of Entity.

Purpose: To establish that everything tracked by the civilization has the same constitutional identity structure. This prevents ad-hoc treatment of identity for different category types.

Required relationships:
- HAS-ANCHOR → StructuralAnchor (every entity has exactly one structural anchor)
- HAS-PROFILE → SemanticProfile (every entity has a current semantic profile)
- HAS-REFERENCE → ReferentialLink (every entity has a referential link, even if it references only itself or an abstract concept)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-I1 (three-aspect identity), A-I2 (aspect distinctness), A-I7 (implementation independence of identity)

---

**Category: StructuralAnchor**

Name: StructuralAnchor

Definition: The persistence-providing component of an Entity's identity. The StructuralAnchor is what makes an Entity the same Entity over time. It does not change while the Entity exists. It is not a computational identifier — it is the conceptual basis for continuity: the answer to "what makes this thing this thing, rather than a different thing?"

Purpose: To give the civilization a constitutional basis for tracking things through change. Without this category, the civilization cannot represent identity persistence — it could only represent snapshots, not persisting things.

Required relationships:
- ANCHORS → Entity (a structural anchor is the continuity basis for exactly one entity)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-I1 (SC as persistent anchor), A-I5 (identity terminates when SC is broken), A-I6 (sameness criterion)

Prohibited: A StructuralAnchor is not a database key, a UUID, a hash, or any computational artifact. Those are implementation choices. The StructuralAnchor is the conceptual property of being-this-entity. Implementation forms for realizing the anchor are matters for D1 and below.

---

**Category: SemanticProfile**

Name: SemanticProfile

Definition: The meaning-bearing component of an Entity's identity at a point in time. The SemanticProfile contains the properties, relationships, descriptions, and state attributions that constitute what the Entity means or represents at a given moment. The SemanticProfile is mutable — it may change freely without disrupting the Entity's identity, provided the StructuralAnchor persists.

Purpose: To represent that things can change without ceasing to be themselves. Without SemanticProfile as a distinct mutable category, either all change destroys identity (too rigid) or identity is constituted by content (too fluid and contradicted by A-I6).

Required relationships:
- IS-PROFILE-OF → Entity (a semantic profile belongs to exactly one entity)
- PRECEDES → SemanticProfile (semantic profiles form an ordered history — each profile precedes its successor)
- IS-RECORDED-IN → HistoricalRecord (each past semantic profile is preserved as history)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-I3 (semantic change permitted), A-I2 (SM distinct from SC)

---

**Category: ReferentialLink**

Name: ReferentialLink

Definition: The reality-connection component of an Entity's identity. The ReferentialLink specifies what in external reality (if anything) the Entity represents. Some entities represent specific RealitySegments or ExternalStates; some represent abstract concepts with no unique external referent; some represent other Entities. The ReferentialLink is the bridge between the civilization's representational space and external reality.

Purpose: To preserve the representation/reality distinction at the level of individual entities. Without this category, the ontology has no mechanism for tracking what any given entity is a representation of — making reality alignment checking impossible.

Required relationships:
- BELONGS-TO → Entity (a referential link belongs to exactly one entity)
- POINTS-TO → RealitySegment | ExternalState | Entity | AbstractTarget (the target of the link)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-I1 (RR as third identity aspect), A-I4 (RR may change without identity disruption), A-R4 (entity distinct from what it represents)

---

**Category: IdentityTransition**

Name: IdentityTransition

Definition: A record of a change in an Entity's SemanticProfile or ReferentialLink. An IdentityTransition captures: which Entity changed, what aspect changed (SemanticProfile or ReferentialLink), what the prior state was, what the new state is, and what caused the change (linked to a ChangeRecord). IdentityTransitions do not record changes to StructuralAnchors — a StructuralAnchor cannot change while the Entity exists.

Purpose: To preserve the history of how an Entity evolved. Without this category, the civilization cannot represent that a thing has changed — only that a thing is in a current state.

Required relationships:
- RECORDS-CHANGE-TO → Entity
- RECORDS-PRIOR → SemanticProfile | ReferentialLink
- RECORDS-CURRENT → SemanticProfile | ReferentialLink
- CAUSED-BY → ChangeRecord

D-1 constraints preserved: A-I3, A-I4 (changes may occur without identity disruption), A-C1 (History register requires change records)

---

### DOMAIN III: EPISTEMIC

*These categories represent the stages of the epistemic chain. Each stage is a distinct category. No two stages may be collapsed. The ordering is a structural constraint, not a workflow.*

---

**Category: Observation**

Name: Observation

Definition: A record of a reality contact — an instance of the civilization registering an Occurrence. An Observation records: that a contact was made, which Entity or process made the contact, and what Occurrence was registered. An Observation does not assign meaning to what was registered. It does not evaluate relevance. It is the raw record of encounter.

Purpose: To provide the foundation of the epistemic chain. All subsequent epistemic stages depend on having a prior Observation to operate on. Without this category, the chain has no starting point in reality.

Required relationships:
- REGISTERS → Occurrence (an observation registers an occurrence in external reality)
- MADE-BY → Entity (an entity made the observation)
- PRECEDES → EvidenceRecord (observations are the input to evidence-formation)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E3 (observation as registration without meaning-assignment), A-E11 (observation not identical to evidence), A-E1 (first stage of chain)

Prohibited: An Observation is not an Interpretation. It carries no assessment of relevance, reliability, or meaning. Any Observation that contains an interpretive conclusion has been wrongly constructed — it conflates two chain stages.

---

**Category: EvidenceRecord**

Name: EvidenceRecord

Definition: The product of applying qualifying judgment to one or more Observations. An EvidenceRecord records: the Observations it qualifies, the qualifying judgment applied (what relevance and reliability assessment was performed), and the result of that assessment. An EvidenceRecord determines whether an Observation is relevant and reliable enough to bear on a specific claim. It does not determine what the Observation reveals about the world — that is the work of Interpretation.

Purpose: To represent the stage at which the civilization discriminates observations that are epistemically useful from those that are not. Without this category, the chain conflates observation with interpretation — all registered occurrences would be treated as equally evidentially relevant, which is not constitutionally sound.

Required relationships:
- QUALIFIES → Observation (an evidence record qualifies one or more observations)
- APPLIES → QualifyingJudgment (the judgment applied to the observations)
- PRECEDES → Interpretation (an evidence record is the input to interpretation)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E4 (qualifying judgment as distinct from meaning-assignment), A-E11 (evidence not identical to interpretation)

---

**Category: Interpretation**

Name: Interpretation

Definition: The product of applying meaning-assignment to one or more EvidenceRecords. An Interpretation records: the EvidenceRecords it interprets, the interpretive frame used (the prior understanding brought to the evidence), and the meaning produced — what the evidence reveals about the state of the world. Interpretation answers: what does this evidence tell us?

Purpose: To represent the stage at which evidence becomes meaningful. Without this category, the civilization would have qualified observations with no mechanism to derive meaning from them — the chain could produce evidence but not act on it.

Required relationships:
- INTERPRETS → EvidenceRecord (an interpretation applies to one or more evidence records)
- APPLIES → InterpretiveFrame (the prior knowledge/schema used)
- PRODUCES → Belief (an interpretation is the basis for a resulting belief)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E5 (interpretation as meaning-assignment, distinct from evidence-formation), A-E11 (interpretation not identical to belief)

---

**Category: Belief**

Name: Belief

Definition: A held claim — a proposition that the civilization holds as representing how things are. A Belief records: the proposition held, the grounding quality of the belief (how well-evidenced it is), and the Interpretation from which it derives. Beliefs vary in grounding quality. A poorly-grounded belief is still a belief. Holding a belief does not require certainty.

Purpose: To represent the civilization's held positions before validation. Without this category, there would be no distinction between "what we have interpreted" and "what we have validated" — Knowledge would be indistinguishable from raw interpretation.

Required relationships:
- DERIVED-FROM → Interpretation (a belief derives from an interpretation)
- GROUNDS → KnowledgeClaim (a belief is the basis for a knowledge claim, if validated)
- HAS-QUALITY → GroundingQuality (a property representing the quality of its evidential grounding)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E6 (belief as held claim with variable grounding), A-E11 (belief not identical to knowledge)

---

**Category: KnowledgeClaim**

Name: KnowledgeClaim

Definition: A Belief that has been both justified and validated. A KnowledgeClaim records: the Belief it validates, the justification (the evidential basis for the belief), the validation process applied, and the full ProvenanceChain tracing the claim back through the epistemic chain to the original Observations. Every KnowledgeClaim carries provenance — the complete record of how it was formed and validated.

Purpose: To represent the civilization's validated knowledge, distinguished from mere belief. Without this category, the civilization cannot represent the difference between what it holds and what it knows — making the validation requirement in A-E7 unrepresentable.

Required relationships:
- VALIDATES → Belief (a knowledge claim is a validated belief)
- CARRIES → ProvenanceChain (every knowledge claim carries its provenance)
- INTEGRATES-INTO → UnderstandingModel (validated knowledge integrates into understanding)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E7 (knowledge = justified + validated belief), A-E9 (provenance necessity), A-E11 (knowledge not identical to understanding)

---

**Category: UnderstandingModel**

Name: UnderstandingModel

Definition: An integrated model that organizes KnowledgeClaims into a structure capable of generating reliable predictions and explanations about a domain. An UnderstandingModel is not a collection of knowledge — it is an organized structure that reveals implications, generates expectations, and explains observations. Possessing knowledge is not the same as understanding; understanding requires integration into a model that produces novel implications.

Purpose: To represent the level of epistemic achievement above knowledge that is required to justify action. Without this category, the civilization cannot distinguish between "having validated claims" and "having a model that can guide decisions" — collapsing the distinction that A-E8 requires and that A-C4 depends on.

Required relationships:
- INTEGRATES → KnowledgeClaim (an understanding model integrates validated knowledge claims)
- GENERATES → Prediction | Explanation (the model produces testable predictions and explanations)
- JUSTIFIES → CivilizationalDecision (understanding justifies decisions)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E8 (understanding as integration into predictive model), A-C4 (actions must trace to understanding)

---

**Category: CivilizationalDecision**

Name: CivilizationalDecision

Definition: A commitment to action derived from an UnderstandingModel. A CivilizationalDecision records: the UnderstandingModel that grounds it, the possibilities considered, the selection made, and the entity that made it with what authority. A decision is distinct from the action it authorizes — it is the epistemic commitment that precedes action, not the action itself.

Purpose: To represent the distinct stage between understanding and action. Without this category, the epistemic chain would allow actions to proceed directly from understanding without any intermediate commitment stage — losing the constitutional distinction between understanding-derived selection and action.

Required relationships:
- GROUNDED-BY → UnderstandingModel (a decision is grounded by understanding)
- MADE-BY → Entity (an entity, with derivable authority, made the decision)
- AUTHORIZES → CivilizationalAction (a decision authorizes an action)
- CONSIDERED → PossibilitySet (the set of possibilities evaluated before selection)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E1 (decision as distinct epistemic stage), A-G1 (decision-making requires authority)

---

**Category: CivilizationalAction**

Name: CivilizationalAction

Definition: An act taken by the civilization that produces a change in reality or in the civilization's own state. A CivilizationalAction records: the decision that authorized it, the entity that executed it, the UnderstandingModel that justified it (through the authorizing decision), and the effect produced. Every CivilizationalAction is traceable back through the full epistemic chain.

Purpose: To represent what the civilization does as distinct from what it believes, knows, or decides. Without this category, the civilization has no ontological representation of its own acts — making action traceability impossible.

Required relationships:
- EXECUTES → CivilizationalDecision (an action executes an authorized decision)
- EXECUTED-BY → Entity (an entity executed the action)
- PRODUCES → ActionEffect (the action produces changes)
- AFFECTS → RealitySegment | Entity (the action's effect on external reality or civilization state)
- HAS-TRACE → ActionTrace (the full epistemic provenance of the action)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E1 (action as terminal stage), A-C4 (actions traceable to understanding), A-G1 (action requires derived authority)

---

**Category: ActionEffect**

Name: ActionEffect

Definition: A recorded change in the state of external reality or the civilization's internal state, produced by a CivilizationalAction. An ActionEffect carries: the action that produced it, the RealitySegment or Entity affected, the prior state, and the resulting state. ActionEffects are the basis for evaluating whether actions achieved their intended purpose.

Purpose: To give the civilization representational capacity for the consequences of its actions. Without this category, the civilization can record that it acted but cannot represent what changed as a result — making the action-to-reality feedback loop impossible.

Required relationships:
- PRODUCED-BY → CivilizationalAction
- CHANGES → RealitySegment | Entity

D-1 constraints preserved: A-R7 (reality alignment requires knowing what the civilization has changed), A-C1 (action register requires trackable action effects)

---

**Category: ActionTrace**

Name: ActionTrace

Definition: The complete epistemic provenance of a CivilizationalAction, linking it back through the full epistemic chain: Action → Decision → Understanding → Knowledge → Belief → Interpretation → Evidence → Observation. The ActionTrace is the data structure that makes action traceability constitutional rather than aspirational.

Purpose: To make action-to-understanding traceability representable as a structured category rather than an informal annotation. Without this category, A-C4 cannot be operationally satisfied — the civilization could assert traceability without providing it.

Required relationships:
- TRACES → CivilizationalAction
- LINKS-TO → CivilizationalDecision, UnderstandingModel, KnowledgeClaim, Belief, Interpretation, EvidenceRecord, Observation (the full chain)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C4 (action traceability), A-E1 (chain completeness)

### DOMAIN IV: COHERENCE

*These categories represent the structures required for the civilization to maintain and monitor structural consistency across its seven registers.*

---

**Category: CoherenceRegister**

Name: CoherenceRegister

Definition: One of the seven named registers across which civilizational coherence must be maintained. The seven registers are: Values, Beliefs, Knowledge, Understanding, Decisions, Actions, History. Each CoherenceRegister is a named domain of civilizational state that may contain items assessed for consistency with items in other registers.

Purpose: To give the coherence requirement (A-C1) a concrete structure to act on. Without named registers, coherence checking has no scope definition — it would be globally undefined rather than specifically targetable.

Instances (fixed at D0 level):
1. Values Register
2. Beliefs Register
3. Knowledge Register
4. Understanding Register
5. Decisions Register
6. Actions Register
7. History Register

Required relationships:
- CONTAINS → Entity (a register contains items of the appropriate type)
- IS-ASSESSED-AGAINST → CoherenceRegister (registers are assessed for consistency with each other)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C1 (seven-register coherence requirement)

---

**Category: CoherenceRelation**

Name: CoherenceRelation

Definition: A recorded relationship between two items — from the same or different CoherenceRegisters — that asserts their structural consistency or inconsistency. A CoherenceRelation is the semantic bridge that allows the civilization to represent "these two things are consistent with each other" or "these two things are in structural conflict."

Purpose: To give the civilization the representational capacity to express coherence assessments explicitly. Without this category, coherence is invisible — the civilization might be incoherent without being able to represent that fact.

Required relationships:
- RELATES → Entity (two entities, from any registers)
- ASSERTS → ConsistencyAssessment (consistent | inconsistent | under-assessment)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C1, A-C3 (coherence as detectable structural consistency)

---

**Category: CoherenceViolation**

Name: CoherenceViolation

Definition: A recorded structural contradiction between two or more items in the civilization's registers. A CoherenceViolation is not a warning or a flag — it is a first-class entity with identity, provenance, and lifecycle. It records: what items are in contradiction, across which registers, when the contradiction was detected, and what its current resolution status is.

Purpose: To make coherence violations trackable, resolvable, and historically recorded. Without this category, the resolution obligation in A-C5 cannot be met — there would be nothing to track resolution against.

Required relationships:
- INVOLVES → Entity (the items in contradiction)
- ACROSS → CoherenceRegister (the registers involved)
- HAS-STATUS → ResolutionStatus (unresolved | in-resolution | resolved)
- RESOLVES-TO → ResolutionRecord (when resolved)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C5 (incoherence as resolution obligation, not tolerated state), A-C3 (contradiction requires resolution)

---

**Category: ResolutionRecord**

Name: ResolutionRecord

Definition: A record that a CoherenceViolation has been acknowledged, addressed, and resolved. A ResolutionRecord documents: which CoherenceViolation was resolved, through which of the three permissible resolution paths (belief revised, knowledge revised, both suspended with investigation), and what the resulting state is. Resolution is not the same as correction — it is the structured closure of an identified violation.

Purpose: To give coherence violation resolution an explicit, traceable form. Without this category, "in-resolution" is a status with no content, and the history of how the civilization managed its own coherence is lost.

Required relationships:
- RESOLVES → CoherenceViolation
- USES-PATH → ResolutionPath (one of the three permissible paths from A-C3)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C3 (three resolution paths), A-C5 (resolution is obligatory)

### DOMAIN V: VALUE

*These categories represent the civilization's value structure. The distinction between terminal and instrumental is constitutionally critical — it is the primary structural constraint in this domain.*

---

**Category: TerminalValue**

Name: TerminalValue

Definition: A value received from founding authority. A TerminalValue is not created, generated, selected, discovered, or ratified by the civilization. It exists in the civilization's ontology as received — its instances are fixed. No civilizational process, reasoning chain, deliberation, or action may create a new TerminalValue or modify an existing one. TerminalValues are the ultimate justificatory foundation for all InstrumentalValues.

Purpose: To represent the civilization's highest values in a form that is structurally protected from internal modification. Without this category, the civilization has no representational basis for the non-generatability requirement — it could not distinguish "a received value" from "a generated value."

Required relationships:
- GROUNDS → InstrumentalValue (terminal values ground the derivation of instrumental values)
- RECEIVED-FROM → FoundingAuthority (the source of terminal values)

Structural constraint: No civilizational process category in D0 or below produces instances of TerminalValue. The production of TerminalValue instances is constitutionally foreclosed. The D0 ontology provides no generative relationship that produces TerminalValues. They exist; they are not generated.

D-1 constraints preserved: A-V1 (external source), A-V2 (fixedness), A-V5 (generation prohibition)

---

**Category: InstrumentalValue**

Name: InstrumentalValue

Definition: A value held by the civilization that is derived from one or more TerminalValues through principled reasoning. An InstrumentalValue records: the terminal value(s) from which it derives, the reasoning that establishes the derivation, and its current status. InstrumentalValues may be generated, revised, and updated by the civilization within the bounds established by the TerminalValues they serve.

Purpose: To represent the civilization's working values — the values it actively deliberates about and revises — as distinct from its fixed foundational values. Without this category, the civilization has no mechanism to deliberate about values at all, since terminal values are fixed.

Required relationships:
- DERIVES-FROM → TerminalValue (an instrumental value traces to one or more terminal values)
- EVIDENCED-BY → ValueDerivation (the derivation trace is explicitly recorded)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-V3 (instrumental derivation), A-V4 (derivation constraint), A-V7 (instrumental limit — cannot implicitly contradict terminal)

---

**Category: ValueDerivation**

Name: ValueDerivation

Definition: The explicit trace from an InstrumentalValue back to the TerminalValue(s) from which it derives, including the principled reasoning that establishes the derivation. A ValueDerivation makes the justificatory relationship between instrumental and terminal values transparent and assessable.

Purpose: To make the derivation requirement in A-V4 verifiable rather than asserted. Without this category, an InstrumentalValue could claim terminal grounding without providing the trace — the prohibition in A-V7 would be unenforceable.

Required relationships:
- TRACES → InstrumentalValue → TerminalValue
- RECORDS → DerivationReasoning

D-1 constraints preserved: A-V4 (derivation constraint), A-V7 (instrumental limit)

### DOMAIN VI: AUTHORITY

*These categories represent the structures required for the civilization to represent legitimacy, competence, and accountability. No category in this domain holds inherent authority.*

---

**Category: AuthoritySource**

Name: AuthoritySource

Definition: The originating basis from which a specific AuthorityClaim derives. All AuthoritySources trace ultimately to founding ratification. An AuthoritySource is not an entity that holds authority — it is the anchor of a derivation chain. The root AuthoritySource for APEX is the founding ratification of D-2.

Purpose: To make authority derivation representable with a traceable origin. Without this category, the derivation requirement in A-G1 cannot be satisfied — authority claims would have no constitutionally valid starting point.

Required relationships:
- GROUNDS → AuthorityClaim (an authority source grounds one or more authority claims)
- TRACES-TO → AuthoritySource | FoundingRatification (sources form a derivation chain)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-G1 (all authority is derived), A-G2 (derivation requires a source)

---

**Category: AuthorityClaim**

Name: AuthorityClaim

Definition: A specific entity's claimed authority to act within a specific domain, with explicit derivation from an AuthoritySource. An AuthorityClaim records: who claims the authority, over what domain, on what basis (AuthoritySource), what competence grounds the claim (CompetenceDemonstration), and what the limits of the claim are. Authority claims are always specific — there is no general or unlimited authority.

Purpose: To make authority a representable, assessable, and challengeable structure rather than an asserted property. Without this category, the civilization cannot distinguish between legitimate and illegitimate authority claims — making the derivation requirement operational.

Required relationships:
- HELD-BY → Entity (the entity claiming authority)
- GROUNDED-BY → AuthoritySource
- IN-DOMAIN → AuthorityDomain
- SUPPORTED-BY → CompetenceDemonstration
- SUBJECT-TO → AuthorityChallenge (authority claims may always be challenged)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-G1, A-G3 (proportionality), A-G4 (domain specificity), A-G5 (challenge standing)

---

**Category: CompetenceDemonstration**

Name: CompetenceDemonstration

Definition: The record that grounds an AuthorityClaim's proportionality requirement. A CompetenceDemonstration records: the domain in which competence is claimed, the evidence of demonstrated competence, and the basis for the competence assessment. Competence must be demonstrated, not merely asserted.

Purpose: To make the proportionality requirement in A-G3 satisfiable with evidence. Without this category, authority claims could assert competence without providing a basis — the proportionality requirement would be unenforceable.

Required relationships:
- GROUNDS → AuthorityClaim
- EVIDENCES → CompetenceInDomain

D-1 constraints preserved: A-G3 (proportionality to demonstrated competence)

---

**Category: AuthorityChallenge**

Name: AuthorityChallenge

Definition: A formal claim that an AuthorityClaim is invalid on grounds of invalid derivation or exceeded competence. An AuthorityChallenge records: which AuthorityClaim is challenged, on which grounds (invalid derivation | competence limit exceeded | domain exceeded), and what the basis of the challenge is. Challenges are constitutionally valid mechanisms — they are not violations.

Purpose: To make the challenge standing in A-G5 representable and traceable. Without this category, the constitutional right to challenge authority has no structural form.

Required relationships:
- CHALLENGES → AuthorityClaim
- ON-GROUNDS → ChallengeGrounds (derivation-invalid | competence-exceeded | domain-exceeded)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-G5 (challenge standing), A-G8 (epistemic supersession)

---

**Category: AccountabilityRecord**

Name: AccountabilityRecord

Definition: A record linking an entity to the outcomes of its authority exercise. An AccountabilityRecord records: what authority was exercised, by whom, with what outcome, and whether the outcome was within the scope of the authority. Accountability is not transferred when operational execution is assigned — the authority-holder remains accountable.

Purpose: To make the non-delegability of constitutional accountability in A-G7 representable. Without this category, accountability claims are assertions without structural basis.

Required relationships:
- RECORDS-ACCOUNTABILITY-OF → Entity
- FOR → AuthorityClaim (the authority exercised)
- WITH-OUTCOME → ActionEffect | CivilizationalDecision

D-1 constraints preserved: A-G6 (non-delegability), A-G7 (accountability preservation)

### DOMAIN VII: ACTION

*The Action domain supplements the Epistemic domain with categories specifically required for traceability of civilizational acts.*

*Note: CivilizationalAction, ActionEffect, and ActionTrace are defined in the Epistemic Domain (Section 3, Domain III) because they are integral to the epistemic chain. This domain defines the supporting categories.*

---

**Category: ActorProfile**

Name: ActorProfile

Definition: The representation of an entity in its capacity as a potential actor — the record of what actions the entity is authorized to take, on what basis, and in what domains. An ActorProfile does not grant authority — it records what authority has been derived and granted to this entity through AuthorityClaims.

Purpose: To represent what the civilization knows about what each entity may legitimately do. Without this category, the civilization has no way to assess whether a proposed action is within the acting entity's authority prior to the action being taken.

Required relationships:
- PROFILES → Entity
- RECORDS-AUTHORITY → AuthorityClaim (the authority claims that constitute the actor's mandate)
- SUBJECT-TO → AccountabilityRecord

D-1 constraints preserved: A-G1, A-G3, A-G6

**Category: PossibilitySet**

Name: PossibilitySet

Definition: The set of possible actions considered by an entity in forming a CivilizationalDecision. A PossibilitySet records: what alternatives were evaluated, on what basis they were assessed, and which was selected. The PossibilitySet documents that the decision process was deliberate rather than arbitrary.

Purpose: To represent the deliberative dimension of the Decision stage. Without this category, a CivilizationalDecision could be recorded without evidence that alternatives were considered — making the understanding-to-decision traceability in A-C4 incomplete.

Required relationships:
- CONSIDERED-IN → CivilizationalDecision
- CONTAINS → PossibleAction (the alternatives evaluated)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E1 (decision as distinct deliberative stage)

### DOMAIN VIII: HISTORY

*These categories represent the structures required for the civilization to preserve its past in a way that satisfies the History register of coherence and the provenance requirements of the epistemic chain.*

---

**Category: HistoricalRecord**

Name: HistoricalRecord

Definition: A persisted record of a past state, event, or entity configuration. A HistoricalRecord is created when a state changes — the prior state is recorded before the new state is adopted. HistoricalRecords are not erasable. They may be superseded by new states, but the prior state remains in the historical record indefinitely.

Purpose: To give the History register of coherence a first-class representational structure. Without this category, the civilization's history is implicit in current state and cannot be used for coherence checking, provenance tracing, or accountability assessment.

Required relationships:
- RECORDS → Entity | SemanticProfile | ExternalState (a historical record preserves a past state)
- PRECEDED-BY → HistoricalRecord (historical records form a chain)
- RECORDED-IN → CoherenceRegister (history register)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C1 (History register), A-E9 (provenance), A-C4 (action traceability across time)

---

**Category: ChangeRecord**

Name: ChangeRecord

Definition: A specific record of a transition — a change from one state to another. A ChangeRecord records: what changed (the entity or state), when the change occurred (relative ordering, not absolute time), what caused the change (the evidence, decision, or action that produced it), what the prior state was, and what the new state is.

Purpose: To answer the constitutional history questions: What changed? Why did it change? What replaced it? What evidence caused it? Without this category, the civilization has no structured representation of change — only before and after states without the causal link.

Required relationships:
- RECORDS-CHANGE-TO → Entity | SemanticProfile | ExternalState
- CAUSED-BY → KnowledgeClaim | CivilizationalDecision | CivilizationalAction | CoherenceViolation (the cause)
- PRIOR-STATE → HistoricalRecord
- RESULTING-STATE → Entity | SemanticProfile (the new state)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C1 (History register — "why" must be preserved), A-E9 (provenance)

---

**Category: Supersession**

Name: Supersession

Definition: The formal relationship between a current state and the prior state it replaced. Supersession records: what was superseded, what superseded it, and what caused the supersession. Supersession is the only legitimate mechanism for replacing a state — there is no deletion, only supersession.

Purpose: To make the non-erasability of history a structural property rather than a policy. Without this category as the only replacement mechanism, the ontology would have no way to prevent deletion from being structurally equivalent to supersession.

Required relationships:
- SUPERSEDES → HistoricalRecord | SemanticProfile | KnowledgeClaim (the prior state)
- SUPERSEDED-BY → HistoricalRecord | SemanticProfile | KnowledgeClaim (the new state)
- CAUSED-BY → ChangeRecord (the change that produced the supersession)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-C1 (history must be preserved), A-I3 (change is permitted; erasure is not implied by change)

---

**Category: ProvenanceChain**

Name: ProvenanceChain

Definition: The complete causal history of how a current state — typically a KnowledgeClaim — came to be. A ProvenanceChain links the current state back through all the stages that produced it: through ChangeRecords, HistoricalRecords, and epistemic chain records. For a KnowledgeClaim, the ProvenanceChain spans back to the original Observations.

Purpose: To give A-E9's provenance requirement a structured form. Without this category, provenance is an annotation — a property value. With this category, provenance is a traversable structure that can be inspected, challenged, and validated.

Required relationships:
- CHAINS-FROM → KnowledgeClaim | CivilizationalAction (what has provenance)
- THROUGH → (ordered sequence of) ChangeRecord | HistoricalRecord | ActionTrace | CoherenceViolation (the links in the chain)

D-1 constraints preserved: A-E9 (provenance necessity), A-DA-1 (everything traceable)

## SECTION 5 — IDENTITY MODEL

### 5.1 The Three-Aspect Structure

Every Entity in D0 has exactly three identity aspects. These aspects are:

**StructuralAnchor (SC):** The persistence-providing component. SC answers: what makes this thing the same thing over time? SC does not change while the Entity exists. The only event that disrupts SC is entity termination.

**SemanticProfile (SM):** The meaning-bearing component. SM is what the entity currently means — its properties, relationships, attributions. SM may change freely; change to SM is recorded as an IdentityTransition but does not disrupt entity identity.

**ReferentialLink (RL):** The reality-connection component. RL points to what the entity represents in external reality. RL may change (re-scoping, re-classification) without disrupting identity, provided SC persists.

### 5.2 Identity Persistence Conditions

An Entity persists as the same Entity if and only if its StructuralAnchor is maintained.

An Entity ceases to exist when its StructuralAnchor is broken. After termination:
- The entity's final state is preserved as a HistoricalRecord
- The entity's IdentityTransition history remains accessible
- No new entity with the same SC can be created — SC is non-reusable

### 5.3 Identity Transition Model

When an Entity's SemanticProfile or ReferentialLink changes:
1. An IdentityTransition is recorded, capturing prior state, new state, and cause
2. The prior SemanticProfile is preserved as a HistoricalRecord
3. The Entity's StructuralAnchor is unchanged
4. The Entity continues to exist as the same Entity

When an Entity's StructuralAnchor would need to change, the correct representation is:
1. The prior Entity is terminated (final state preserved)
2. A new Entity is created (new SC, new SM, new RL)
3. A Supersession relationship links the new Entity to the prior Entity

### 5.4 Sameness

Two items are the same Entity if and only if they share the same StructuralAnchor. Identical SemanticProfiles are not sufficient for sameness. Identical ReferentialLinks are not sufficient for sameness. SC identity is the single necessary and sufficient condition.

### 5.5 Implementation Neutrality

The identity model specifies constitutional requirements, not implementation forms. The StructuralAnchor is not a UUID, a database key, a hash, or a name. Those are possible implementations. D1 will specify what formal type the SC takes. D0 specifies only that every Entity must have a persistence-providing identity component that satisfies the constitutional requirements.

## SECTION 6 — EPISTEMIC MODEL

### 6.1 The Chain as Structural Ordering

The epistemic chain is an ordered dependency structure. Each stage depends on all prior stages. The ordering is logical, not temporal — in practice, stages may be executed rapidly or habituall, but logical dependency is unconditional.

```
RealitySegment / Occurrence (External)
        ↓
    Observation
        ↓
  EvidenceRecord
        ↓
   Interpretation
        ↓
       Belief
        ↓
  KnowledgeClaim
        ↓
UnderstandingModel
        ↓
CivilizationalDecision
        ↓
CivilizationalAction
        ↓
    ActionEffect → RealitySegment (closes the loop)
```

### 6.2 Allowed Transitions

| From | To | Governing relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Occurrence | Observation | REGISTERS |
| Observation | EvidenceRecord | QUALIFIES |
| EvidenceRecord | Interpretation | INTERPRETS |
| Interpretation | Belief | GROUNDS |
| Belief | KnowledgeClaim | VALIDATES |
| KnowledgeClaim | UnderstandingModel | INTEGRATES |
| UnderstandingModel | CivilizationalDecision | AUTHORIZES |
| CivilizationalDecision | CivilizationalAction | EXECUTES |
| CivilizationalAction | ActionEffect | PRODUCES |

### 6.3 Forbidden Transitions

| Transition | Violation |
|---|---|
| Observation → Belief | Skips EvidenceRecord and Interpretation |
| Observation → KnowledgeClaim | Skips EvidenceRecord, Interpretation, Belief, Validation |
| EvidenceRecord → Belief | Skips Interpretation |
| Interpretation → KnowledgeClaim | Skips Belief formation and Validation |
| Belief → CivilizationalAction | Skips Knowledge, Understanding, Decision |
| KnowledgeClaim → CivilizationalAction | Skips Understanding and Decision |
| UnderstandingModel → CivilizationalAction | Skips Decision |

### 6.4 Provenance

Every KnowledgeClaim carries a ProvenanceChain. The ProvenanceChain links backward through: the Belief validated, the Interpretation that grounded the belief, the EvidenceRecord(s) that were interpreted, and the Observation(s) that were qualified as evidence.

A KnowledgeClaim without a complete ProvenanceChain is constitutionally invalid — it does not satisfy the Knowledge category's requirements.

### 6.5 Chain Independence

The epistemic chain model in D0 is not a software architecture. It is a semantic ordering constraint. Any implementation — symbolic, neural, probabilistic, distributed — that satisfies the dependency ordering is constitutionally compliant.

## SECTION 7 — COHERENCE MODEL

### 7.1 The Seven Registers

Civilizational coherence spans seven registers. These are named in D0 as CoherenceRegister instances:

| Register | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Values | TerminalValues and InstrumentalValues held by the civilization |
| Beliefs | Beliefs held by the civilization |
| Knowledge | KnowledgeClaims validated by the civilization |
| Understanding | UnderstandingModels maintained by the civilization |
| Decisions | CivilizationalDecisions made by the civilization |
| Actions | CivilizationalActions taken by the civilization |
| History | HistoricalRecords, ChangeRecords, and Supersessions |

### 7.2 Coherence Defined in D0

Coherence is structural consistency across registers. A CoherenceViolation exists when two items from any registers stand in structural contradiction — when holding both simultaneously requires that one of them be false.

Coherence is NOT:
- Certainty (holding uncertain items is coherent if the uncertainty is acknowledged)
- Completeness (having gaps is coherent if the gaps are identified)
- Agreement (having different views in deliberation is coherent if not asserted as simultaneous facts)
- Instantaneous consistency (brief transitions are coherent if resolution is in progress)

### 7.3 Coherence Assessment

The ontology provides:
- CoherenceRelation: a semantic bridge between items that asserts their relationship
- CoherenceViolation: a record of confirmed structural contradiction
- ResolutionRecord: a record of violation resolution

The ontology does not provide:
- A checking algorithm
- An assessment schedule
- A violation-detection process
- A resolution workflow

These are matters for D1 and below.

### 7.4 Resolution Paths

When a CoherenceViolation is identified, exactly three resolution paths are permissible (from A-C3):
1. The belief in violation is revised to accord with established knowledge
2. The knowledge in violation is revised with appropriate evidential grounds, and the belief is updated accordingly
3. Both are suspended pending investigation, with the suspension explicitly acknowledged

The ResolutionRecord captures which path was taken and the outcome.

## SECTION 8 — VALUE MODEL

### 8.1 Value Taxonomy

The civilization's ontology contains exactly two value categories:

**TerminalValue** — received from founding authority, fixed, non-generatable internally
**InstrumentalValue** — derived from terminal values through principled reasoning, generatable internally

These two categories are exhaustive. There is no third value type. A value that is neither received from founding authority nor derivable from terminal values is not a legitimate value in APEX's constitutional framework.

### 8.2 TerminalValue Structural Constraints

The TerminalValue category has the following structural properties enforced at D0:

**No generative relationship:** D0 contains no relationship category of the form CivilizationalProcess → TerminalValue. There is no relationship by which any internal process produces a TerminalValue instance. The only valid producer is founding authority.

**Fixedness:** TerminalValue instances do not have a SemanticProfile mutation path that changes their substantive content. They have identity persistence. They do not have content revision capability through internal processes.

**Visibility:** TerminalValue instances are representable and inspectable. The constraint is on generation and modification, not on access or reference.

### 8.3 InstrumentalValue Derivation

Every InstrumentalValue must carry a ValueDerivation — the explicit trace from the instrumental value to the terminal value(s) it serves.

An InstrumentalValue without a ValueDerivation is constitutionally invalid — it cannot be distinguished from an ungrounded preference.

An InstrumentalValue whose ValueDerivation traces to another InstrumentalValue (not to a TerminalValue) requires a further trace — the chain must terminate at one or more TerminalValues.

### 8.4 Value and Action

CivilizationalActions may not produce TerminalValues. The ActionEffect category does not include "new TerminalValue" as a possible effect type. This is a structural property of the ontology, not a policy constraint.

## SECTION 9 — AUTHORITY MODEL

### 9.1 No Inherent Authority

No D0 category possesses authority by virtue of type membership. An entity is not authorized to act simply because it is an Entity of a certain type. Authority is always a specific AuthorityClaim with explicit derivation, domain, and limits.

This constraint is architectural, not policy-based. The D0 ontology does not contain a category that inherits authority from its type definition.

### 9.2 Authority Structure

Authority in D0 is represented as:

```
FoundingRatification
        ↓
  AuthoritySource
        ↓
  AuthorityClaim
    ← GROUNDED-BY → CompetenceDemonstration
    ← EXERCISED-BY → Entity (through ActorProfile)
    ← SUBJECT-TO → AuthorityChallenge
    → RECORDS-ACCOUNTABILITY-IN → AccountabilityRecord
```

Every AuthorityClaim has:
- A source (derivation chain)
- A domain (scope)
- A grounding (competence demonstration)
- A limit (defined scope — authority is never unlimited)
- Challenge exposure (any authority claim may be challenged)

### 9.3 Authority and Domain

Authority is domain-specific. An AuthorityClaim in one domain does not extend to another domain. An Entity with high authority in one CoherenceRegister has no derived authority in another.

### 9.4 Non-Delegation

AuthorityClaims carry accountability that cannot be transferred. An entity that assigns operational execution to another entity remains accountable for the outcomes. The AccountabilityRecord structure enforces this — it links outcomes to the authority-holder, not the operational executor.

```
        ↓ (DERIVES-FROM)

        ↓ (TRACES-TO)
FoundingRatification
```

An action that cannot complete both the epistemic trace and the authority trace is constitutionally invalid.

## SECTION 11 — HISTORICAL CONTINUITY MODEL

### 11.1 History as a Register

The History register is one of the seven CoherenceRegisters. Maintaining its integrity is a coherence obligation. The ontology provides structural guarantees for history preservation.

### 11.2 The Non-Erasability Principle

The D0 ontology provides no deletion relationship. Historical records are not deleted — they are superseded. The prior state always remains accessible. This is a structural property of the ontology — it is not enforced by policy.

Every change produces:
1. A ChangeRecord (capturing what, why, prior state, new state)
2. A Supersession relationship (linking old to new)
3. A HistoricalRecord (preserving the prior state)

The HistoricalRecord persists indefinitely in the History register.

### 11.3 The Four History Questions

The D0 History domain is designed to answer four questions constitutionally required by A-C1:

| Question | Answered By |
|---|---|
| What changed? | ChangeRecord.what |
| Why did it change? | ChangeRecord.caused-by (KnowledgeClaim, Decision, or Action) |
| What replaced it? | Supersession.current-state |
| What evidence caused the change? | ChangeRecord.caused-by → ProvenanceChain |

### 11.4 Provenance Chains

ProvenanceChains span multiple layers of the history model. For a KnowledgeClaim, the ProvenanceChain traces through: the validation process → the Belief → the Interpretation → the Evidence → the Observations. For a CivilizationalAction, the ActionTrace performs the equivalent function.

## SECTION 13 — D0 → D+ HANDOFF

D0 establishes what must exist. It leaves all design choices about form, type system, constraints, and implementation to lower layers. The following defines precisely what remains for D1 and below.

---

### What D1 Receives From D0

D1 (Civilization Ontology) receives the complete category set defined in this document. D1's task is to:

1. **Formalize the type system.** D0 names categories conceptually. D1 specifies them formally — with typed properties, cardinality constraints, and formal relationship schemas.

2. **Specify the StructuralAnchor.** D0 says every Entity has a SC but does not specify its formal type. D1 specifies: what type realizes the SC, what cardinality it has, what immutability guarantees it provides.

3. **Define property types for all categories.** D0 identifies which concepts are properties (confidence, grounding quality, validation status, etc.). D1 specifies the formal type of each property.

4. **Formalize cardinality.** D0 says "an Observation may be qualified by multiple EvidenceRecords." D1 specifies minimum and maximum cardinalities for all relationships.

5. **Define CoherenceRelation formally.** D0 names CoherenceRelation as a category. D1 specifies its formal structure and what a consistency assessment formally contains.

6. **Formalize the Seven CoherenceRegister instances.** D0 names the seven registers. D1 specifies what formal type each register contains and how membership is determined.

7. **Specify InstrumentalValue derivation chain structure.** D0 says InstrumentalValues trace to TerminalValues. D1 specifies the formal structure of the ValueDerivation chain, including how many links are permitted and what formal content is required at each link.

8. **Define PossibilitySet formally.** D0 names PossibilitySet as a category. D1 specifies what formally constitutes a "possible action" and what the set structure requires.

---

### What D1 Must Not Do

D1 must not:

- Introduce a new category not grounded in D0
- Collapse any D0 category distinction (especially SC/SM/RL; TerminalValue/InstrumentalValue; Entity/RealitySegment)
- Specify implementation forms (schemas, storage, computation)
- Introduce ontological commitments not derivable from D0
- Define authority-by-type for any category
- Provide a generative path for TerminalValues

---

### What D2 Receives From D0 (Through D1)

D2 (Universal Reality Object Specification) receives the formal ontology of D1. From D0 specifically, D2 inherits:

- The requirement that every tracked object have three-aspect identity
- The requirement that every object's representation be distinct from external reality
- The provenance requirement for KnowledgeClaims
- The non-erasability structure (Supersession as the only replacement mechanism)
- The epistemic chain dependency ordering

D2's task is to specify the universal root abstraction for all representational objects — the single type from which all civilization entities derive, satisfying D0's identity requirements, provenance requirements, and coherence requirements.
